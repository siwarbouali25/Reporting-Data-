### Governance

#### Board oversight

In 2024, Eurolux Universal Bank AG (“the Bank”) was overseen by a ten-member Board of Directors, with 68.5% classified as independent. The Board convened eight full meetings during the year (up from four in 2022 and seven in 2023). Climate-related matters appeared on the agenda at 72.6% of these meetings in 2024 (69.8% in 2022 and 73.1% in 2023), based on available evidence that this metric reflects the share of Board meetings where climate topics were included, not the proportion of agenda time.

The Board was supported by an ESG & Sustainability Committee, which met seven times in 2024 (five in 2022, six in 2023). Available documentation evidences regular committee activity and meeting frequency, but does not provide the committee charter or terms of reference; no formal governance mandate text is therefore described in this report. The committee decision summary confirms that the ESG & Sustainability Committee and the Full Board each considered climate-related topics within their respective remits, as evidenced by their meeting records.

Climate risk reporting from management to the Board occurred on a semi-annual basis in 2024, supported by the Climate Risk Management Committee. Available decision evidence shows that both the Full Board and the ESG & Sustainability Committee took specific climate-related decisions during 2024; however, the underlying records do not describe explicit trade-offs between climate considerations and factors such as profitability, capital allocation, implementation cost, risk appetite, or competing strategic priorities.

Source data indicate that the Bank’s climate-related disclosures are flagged as aligned with TCFD and IFRS S2 in 2024. This section describes the oversight structures through which the Board and its ESG & Sustainability Committee consider climate-related risks and opportunities in connection with those disclosures.

#### Management responsibility

Climate-related risk management at Eurolux Universal Bank AG in 2024 was coordinated through the Climate Risk Management Committee, which operated as the primary management body responsible for climate risk oversight. This committee reported to the Board on a semi-annual basis on climate-related risks, mitigation activities, and scenario analysis outcomes.

Available evidence confirms that the Bank maintained a dedicated climate risk register in 2024. The process followed a structured flow:

1. **Risk identification and registration**  
   Climate-related risks were identified across the Bank’s lending, investment, and other relevant activities, and recorded in the climate risk register. In 2024, eight climate risks were captured, with four having changed in profile or assessment since the prior period. The register included both physical and transition climate risks and was used as the central inventory for management reporting.

2. **Classification by category, time horizon, and rating**  
   Each risk in the register was classified using climate-specific categories: physical acute, physical chronic, transition market, transition policy, transition reputational, and transition technology. Risks were also assigned time horizons (short 0–2 years, medium 2–5 years, or long 5+ years) and ratings (high or medium).  
   Examples from the 2024 register include:  
   - A high-rated transition technology risk relating to the shift to electric vehicles in the auto lending portfolio (short-term, 0–2 years).  
   - A high-rated transition market risk relating to stranded assets in fossil fuel sector exposures (long-term, 5+ years).  
   - A high-rated transition policy risk associated with carbon pricing impacts on industrial loans (medium-term, 2–5 years).  
   - A high-rated physical acute risk reflecting mortgage book exposure to flood events (long-term, 5+ years).  
   - A medium-rated transition reputational risk linked to scrutiny of financed emissions (medium-term, 2–5 years).

3. **Monitoring frequency**  
   Risks in the register were assigned monitoring frequencies of either quarterly or semi-annual review. High-rated risks, including the EV transition, stranded asset, carbon pricing, and flood exposure risks, were monitored quarterly. The medium-rated reputational risk arising from financed emissions scrutiny was monitored on a semi-annual basis. These monitoring cycles informed the timing of updates to the climate risk register and management reporting to the Climate Risk Management Committee and, in turn, to the Board.

4. **Scenario analysis links**  
   Where applicable, individual climate risks were linked to specific climate scenario analyses. Available documentation shows that several register entries referenced scenario analysis identifiers, including SCN0002, SCN0004, SCN0011, and SCN0013. For example, the transition risk arising from the electric vehicle shift in auto lending was linked to scenario SCN0011, the flood-related physical risk in the mortgage book referenced SCN0004, and the reputational risk related to financed emissions scrutiny was linked to SCN0013. For some risks, such as certain transition market and policy risks, no scenario reference was recorded in the risk-level evidence.

5. **Mitigation actions**  
   The climate risk register documented mitigation actions aligned to specific risks. The general list of mitigation actions evidenced in 2024 included:  
   - Climate scenario stress testing integrated into ICAAP  
   - Collateral revaluation and flood mapping overlay  
   - Engagement and transition-plan covenants with borrowers  
   - Green retrofit financing incentives  
   - Portfolio decarbonisation glide-path monitoring  
   - Sector exposure limits and enhanced due diligence  
   Examples from the register include: the EV shift in auto lending risk was mitigated by climate scenario stress testing integrated into ICAAP; the stranded assets risk by collateral revaluation and flood mapping overlay; the carbon pricing risk by sector exposure limits and enhanced due diligence; the mortgage book flood exposure by engagement and transition-plan covenants with borrowers; and the reputational risk from financed emissions scrutiny by portfolio decarbonisation glide-path monitoring.

   These mitigation actions were used to track management responses over time, including the six risks identified as integrated into the enterprise risk management (ERM) framework.

6. **Integration with ERM and Board reporting**  
   According to available evidence, six of the eight climate risks in the register were integrated into the Bank’s broader ERM framework. ERM-integrated climate risks followed the Bank’s standard risk processes for aggregation, reporting, and capital planning, with climate scenario stress testing incorporated into ICAAP. The Climate Risk Management Committee oversaw the ongoing maintenance of the register, reviewed quarterly and semi-annual updates from risk owners, and prepared semi-annual climate risk reports for the Board.

   Climate considerations were also applied to major transactions, with available documentation indicating that climate checks were undertaken for such transactions. This evidence does not, however, demonstrate the existence of a formal mandatory policy or quantitative thresholds.

Formal escalation thresholds or trigger-based escalation mechanisms for climate risks are not documented in the available evidence and are therefore not described in this report.

#### Climate skills and competencies

Board-level climate competence is tracked using the proportion of directors with recognised climate-related expertise. In 2024, 37.5% of Board members were recorded as having climate expertise, up from 33.5% in 2022 and 35.5% in 2023. This trend indicates a gradual increase in the representation of climate-competent directors over the three-year period.

The Bank operated a climate- and sustainability-related skills development programme in 2024, which provided ongoing education and updates to Board members and relevant senior executives on climate risk, climate-related disclosure practices, and evolving regulatory expectations. This programme supported the Board’s capacity to interpret climate risk register outputs, scenario analysis, and related management reports.

Available documentation does not describe a formal Board skills adequacy assessment process in relation to climate or broader ESG competencies. As a result, this report relies on outcome evidence, such as the percentage of Board members with climate expertise and the existence of a skills development programme, rather than a documented assessment framework.

#### Remuneration and climate incentives

In 2024, the Bank’s remuneration structures for senior management incorporated climate- and ESG-related performance elements. The Chief Executive Officer’s variable compensation included ESG-linked components representing 9.3% of total CEO compensation, compared with 6.3% in 2022 and 7.8% in 2023. For the broader executive population, climate-linked remuneration components represented 15.1% of compensation in 2024, up from 8.7% in 2022 and broadly aligned with 15.3% in 2023.

Available evidence indicates that the Full Board considered climate-related executive remuneration key performance indicators as part of its 2024 oversight of executive pay. The climate- and ESG-linked elements in remuneration were designed to align management incentives with climate-related objectives, including risk management and the execution of transition and green finance initiatives, although specific KPI definitions are not detailed in the source data.

#### Board and committee decisions during 2024

Available Board and committee records for 2024 identify a set of climate-related decisions taken by the Full Board and the ESG & Sustainability Committee. The decisions are summarised below by date, decision body, and subject; each decision is listed once only.

- **24 January 2024 – Full Board**  
  The Full Board approved the Bank’s 2024 ESG report for publication, in a meeting where transition plan review and carbon credit approval were discussed.

- **15 April 2024 – ESG & Sustainability Committee**  
  The ESG & Sustainability Committee approved the carbon credit procurement budget. This decision followed committee discussions on physical risk updates, TCFD-aligned climate disclosure review, and the Bank’s transition plan.

- **15 April 2024 – ESG & Sustainability Committee**  
  In a separate decision on the same date, the ESG & Sustainability Committee approved the climate scenario analysis methodology, following discussions on TCFD-aligned climate disclosure review and physical risk updates.

- **17 July 2024 – Full Board**  
  The Full Board approved the climate scenario analysis methodology after considering transition plan review topics.

- **7 October 2024 – Full Board**  
  The Full Board endorsed an updated transition plan, following Board discussions on TCFD-aligned climate disclosure matters and green finance growth.

- **26 November 2024 – Full Board**  
  The Full Board approved the carbon credit procurement budget, in a meeting where climate-related executive remuneration KPIs and physical risk updates were discussed.

According to the committee decision summary, the ESG & Sustainability Committee focused on physical risk, transition plan, and climate disclosure topics, while the Full Board addressed a broader set of issues, including green finance growth, carbon credit procurement budget approvals, transition plan endorsement, executive remuneration ESG KPIs, and climate scenario methodologies. The underlying decision records do not describe explicit trade-offs between climate considerations and other factors such as profitability, capital allocation, implementation cost, risk appetite, or competing strategic priorities; such trade-offs are therefore not characterised in this report.

#### External assurance and controls

External assurance over climate-related information for 2024 was obtained on a limited assurance basis from EY, in accordance with ISAE 3000. The assurance engagement covered the Bank’s Scope 1 and Scope 2 greenhouse gas emissions only. The Bank’s financed emissions, reported at 35,973,167.69 tCO₂e for 2024, are explicitly outside the stated assurance scope based on available evidence. No other Scope 3 categories were included in the assurance engagement.
