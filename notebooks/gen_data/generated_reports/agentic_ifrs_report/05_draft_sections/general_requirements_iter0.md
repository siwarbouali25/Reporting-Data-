## General Requirements

### Basis of preparation, reporting period, currency and comparatives
This section explains how the sustainability-related financial disclosures have been prepared so that users can interpret the information on a consistent basis across time and in connection with the entity’s general purpose financial reporting.

The disclosures are prepared for the reporting year **2024**, with a fiscal year end of **31 December 2024**. Monetary amounts in these sustainability-related financial disclosures are presented in **euro (EUR)**.

Comparative information is provided for **2022** and **2023**. This supports analysis of trends and year-on-year movements in sustainability-related metrics and related financial measures, including:

- **Climate-related capital expenditure (EUR million):** 322.20 (2022), 373.91 (2023), 476.95 (2024).
- **Tier 1 capital (EUR million):** 95,149.4 (2022), 143,979.2 (2023), 151,208.6 (2024).

Where sustainability-related metrics are expressed in currency-based intensity terms, the use of EUR as the unit of measure supports comparability within the reporting package. For example, the entity reports **carbon intensity for 2024 of 1,154.8131 tCO₂e per EUR million**.

### Material sustainability-related information and why it matters to prospects
The purpose of sustainability-related financial disclosures is to present material information about sustainability-related risks and opportunities that could reasonably be expected to affect the entity’s prospects, including effects on cash flows, access to finance and cost of capital over the short, medium and long term.

In the current reporting package, climate-related risks are described in a structured climate risk register and are complemented by scenario analysis and performance metrics. The climate risk register includes risks that are relevant to a bank’s prospects because they can affect credit quality, collateral values, client behaviour and reputational outcomes.

Examples of sustainability-related risks described as relevant to the entity’s prospects include:

- **Acute physical risk affecting mortgage collateral.** The risk titled **“Physical risk — mortgage book flood exposure”** is described as residential and commercial mortgage collateral located in flood-prone zones facing rising acute physical risk, where property damage and devaluation could impair collateral coverage. This risk is rated **critical**, with a **likelihood score of 4** and a **severity score of 5**. The rating is derived using a defined approach (see “Measurement approaches, assumptions and judgement”).

- **Chronic physical risk affecting agricultural counterparties.** The risk register describes that chronic heat and shifting precipitation can reduce yields for agricultural counterparties, weakening repayment capacity over the medium term. This type of risk is relevant to prospects because it can translate into higher credit risk and changes in portfolio performance.

- **Transition risk from rapid electrification.** The risk register describes that rapid electrification could strand internal-combustion vehicle manufacturers and suppliers in the lending book. This is relevant to prospects because it can affect counterparty viability and asset quality through stranded asset dynamics.

- **Reputational and stakeholder risk linked to financed emissions and fossil financing.** The risk register describes that stakeholder and NGO scrutiny of financed emissions and fossil financing could damage brand and trigger client attrition. This is relevant to prospects because it can affect franchise value, client retention and the stability of revenue-generating relationships.

These risks are not presented as isolated topics. They are linked to the entity’s financing activities and portfolio profile, as reflected in the reporting of **financed emissions for 2024 of 35,973,167.6878965 tCO₂e** and the disclosure of **green loans as 10.71%** of the loan book in 2024. Together, these disclosures help users understand how climate-related risks and opportunities can influence the entity’s future performance and resilience.

### Connected information across governance, strategy, risk management, and metrics
This section provides connected information so that users can understand how governance arrangements, strategic responses, risk management processes and performance metrics relate to each other.

**Governance connectivity.** Climate-related matters are embedded in governance structures and oversight routines. The entity reports that an **ESG committee exists** and that it held **5 meetings per year** (as reported in governance disclosures). Climate is also a recurring topic in board oversight: climate-related topics represent **69.8%** of the board agenda, and climate risk reporting is provided to the board on a **semi-annual** basis. The board comprises **10** members, and **33.5%** of board members have climate expertise (with a separate governance maturity indicator reporting **37.5%** board climate expertise).

These governance disclosures connect directly to risk management and monitoring practices described in the climate risk register. For example, the critical mortgage flood exposure risk is monitored **monthly**, supporting timely escalation and oversight. Other risks in the register are monitored at different frequencies, including **quarterly** and **semi-annual**, reflecting differentiated monitoring aligned to the nature of the risk.

**Strategy and resilience connectivity.** Scenario analysis provides a bridge between strategic planning and risk assessment. The entity applies climate scenarios to the banking book and reports resilience assessments under both orderly and disorderly transition pathways:

- Under **orderly transition scenarios**, the portfolio is assessed as demonstrating **adequate resilience**, with transition risk losses remaining within **Pillar 2 capital buffer thresholds**. The resilience narrative links expected portfolio evolution to **green loan growth** and **client engagement on transition plans**, which are expected to progressively reduce high-carbon sector concentration over the medium term.

- Under **disorderly transition scenarios**, near-term resilience is assessed as acceptable, while **medium-term capital consumption from stranded asset impairments is material**. The narrative identifies **sector exposure limits** and **portfolio decarbonisation glide-path monitoring** as primary resilience mechanisms, and notes that **additional capital buffers may be required post-2030** under the most adverse disorderly assumptions.

These scenario disclosures connect to the entity’s capital position and risk appetite considerations through the reporting of **Tier 1 capital (EUR million)** and the explicit reference to Pillar 2 capital buffer thresholds in the resilience assessment.

**Metrics and targets connectivity.** Operational and financed emissions metrics provide a quantitative basis for monitoring performance and for evaluating progress against targets. For 2024, the entity reports:

- **Scope 1 emissions:** 2,634.908317 tCO₂e (with corporate fleet included).
- **Scope 2 emissions (location-based):** 6,963.4977 tCO₂e.
- **Scope 2 emissions (market-based):** 3,060.5382 tCO₂e.
- **Scope 3 business travel emissions:** 289.8833 tCO₂e.
- **Financed emissions:** 35,973,167.6878965 tCO₂e.

Targets are disclosed in a way that enables users to relate ambition and progress to the reported metrics. The entity reports a target with identifier **TGT001**, with a **2020 baseline year**, covering **Scope 1 and Scope 2**, with an **absolute reduction** target type and progress measured as **absolute tCO₂e**. Progress against this target is reported as **40.0%** as at 2024. Additional target progress indicators are reported as **25.0%** (progress measured in tCO₂e per EUR million) and **13.3%** (progress measured as percentage reduction versus baseline), supporting a multi-dimensional view of performance.

### Measurement approaches, assumptions, judgement and data-quality characteristics
This section explains key measurement approaches and assumptions used to produce sustainability-related metrics and forward-looking information, and describes characteristics of the underlying data that are relevant to users’ interpretation.

**Risk rating methodology and judgement.** Climate risk ratings in the climate risk register are derived from a defined **5×5 risk matrix** based on the product of likelihood and severity scores. The methodology is:

- Risk score = **likelihood score × severity score**.
- Scores **1–2** are classified as **low**.
- Scores **3–6** are classified as **medium**.
- Scores **8–12** are classified as **high**.
- Scores **15–25** are classified as **critical**.

This approach supports consistent classification and prioritisation of risks. For example, the mortgage flood exposure risk is rated **critical** based on a likelihood score of **4** and a severity score of **5** (score 20), and is monitored **monthly**.

**Scenario analysis methodology and assumptions.** The entity applies climate scenarios to the banking book using defined methodologies:

- The **NGFS v4 Net Zero 2050** scenario is applied using a **top-down sector-pathway approach**. Carbon price assumptions are taken from the **IEA Net Zero Emissions 2050 trajectory** and mapped to counterparty **NACE sectors**. Stranded asset estimates are derived from **sector-level fossil fuel capital expenditure exposure**. The analysis scope covers **all lending book exposures** across **DE, FR, IT, NL and ES** jurisdictions.

- The **NGFS v4 Below 2°C (orderly)** scenario is applied using NGFS integrated assessment model outputs aligned to a **1.8°C median temperature pathway**, with a carbon price trajectory from the **REMIND-MAgPIE model**. Financial impact is assessed through **loan-level probability of default sensitivity** to carbon cost pass-through by sector carbon intensity tier.

These methodological disclosures are intended to make forward-looking information supportable by explaining the underlying assumptions and modelling approach.

**Emissions measurement and data-quality characteristics.** The entity reports a summary of emissions data quality characteristics across its emissions information, expressed as a distribution across data sources and estimation approaches:

- **Estimated using economic data:** 47.5%.
- **Proxy model basis:** 16.3%.
- **CDP disclosure-based inputs:** 35.5%.
- **Audited report-based inputs:** 0.7%.

This profile indicates that a portion of emissions information is derived using estimation techniques, including proxy model approaches, alongside reported and audited sources.

**Market-based Scope 2 approach.** Market-based Scope 2 emissions are prepared using utility invoice data that already reflects market-based accounting with grid emission factors adjusted for renewable energy certificates and power purchase agreements. Renewable energy certificate registry information is used for disclosure narrative (certificate type, volume and registry), and the preparation approach is designed to avoid double-counting.

**Financed emissions methodology note for sovereign bonds.** For sovereign bonds, the entity applies a methodological position that **enterprise value including cash (EVIC)** and **issuer revenue** are corporate concepts and therefore are not applied to sovereign bonds.

### Comparative consistency and change monitoring
This section explains how the entity supports comparability over time and how it monitors changes in sustainability-related risks and metrics.

**Comparative consistency.** The inclusion of comparative years **2022** and **2023** supports trend analysis for key metrics and financial measures, including climate-related capital expenditure and Tier 1 capital. The reporting of Scope 1 emissions across **2022, 2023 and 2024** also supports multi-year analysis of operational emissions.

**Change monitoring in risk management.** The climate risk register includes an explicit indicator of whether a risk has **changed since the prior period**, supporting monitoring of evolving risk conditions. For example:

- The mortgage flood exposure risk is indicated as **unchanged since the prior period** and is monitored **monthly**.
- The stakeholder and NGO scrutiny risk is indicated as **changed since the prior period** and is monitored **quarterly**.

This change monitoring is complemented by defined monitoring frequencies (monthly, quarterly and semi-annual), supporting timely identification of shifts in risk profile and enabling consistent follow-through through governance reporting routines (including semi-annual climate risk reporting to the board).

**Comparability of narrative and descriptive information.** Scenario resilience assessments are presented for both orderly and disorderly transition pathways, enabling users to compare how different transition dynamics could affect resilience, capital consumption and the role of portfolio management mechanisms such as sector exposure limits and decarbonisation glide-path monitoring.

Together, these general requirements disclosures are intended to provide a coherent basis for interpreting the detailed disclosures in the governance, strategy, risk management, and metrics and targets sections, using consistent units of measure, defined methodologies and monitoring practices.
