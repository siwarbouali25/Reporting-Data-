## General Requirements

This section explains the basis on which the Bank’s sustainability-related financial disclosures have been prepared, including the reporting period, presentation currency, comparative information, connected information, and the key methodologies, estimates and judgements applied to climate-related disclosures.

### Basis of preparation and fair presentation

The Bank’s sustainability-related financial disclosures are prepared to present fairly sustainability-related risks and opportunities that could reasonably be expected to affect the Bank’s prospects. Fair presentation is supported through disclosures that are intended to be complete, neutral and accurate, and that are prepared to be comparable, verifiable, timely and understandable.

The disclosures are structured to enable users to understand connections:

- **Between sustainability-related matters**—for example, how acute physical risks to mortgage collateral and transition risks affecting counterparties can interact with credit risk and portfolio resilience.
- **Within the sustainability-related financial disclosures**—for example, how governance arrangements (such as Board reporting cadence) relate to risk monitoring frequencies and to the metrics used to track performance.

Where currency is used as a unit of measure in these sustainability-related financial disclosures, the Bank uses the presentation currency applied in its related financial statements.

### Reporting period, fiscal year-end and reporting year

The Bank’s fiscal year ends on **31 December 2024**. The reporting year for these sustainability-related financial disclosures is **2024**.

### Presentation currency and units of measure

The Bank’s reporting currency is **EUR**. Monetary amounts in this section are presented in **EUR million (EURm)** where applicable.

Non-monetary measures are presented in their relevant units, including:

- **tCO₂e** for greenhouse gas (GHG) emissions
- **tCO₂e per EURm** for carbon intensity
- **Percentages (%)** for governance and portfolio composition indicators

### Comparative information

Comparative years included in the sustainability-related financial disclosures are **2022** and **2023**.

Selected metrics are presented across multiple years to support comparability, including climate-related capital expenditure and Tier 1 capital (EURm) for 2022–2024.

### Connected information

The Bank prepares its sustainability-related financial disclosures in a manner intended to enable users to understand connections across governance, strategy, risk management, and metrics and targets.

Examples of connected information reflected in the Bank’s climate-related disclosures include:

- **Risk identification and monitoring**: climate-related risks are recorded with defined monitoring frequencies (monthly, quarterly, and semi-annual), supporting an understanding of how risks are tracked over time.
- **Governance oversight and reporting cadence**: climate risk reporting to the Board is conducted on a **semi-annual** basis, and climate-related matters are included on the Board agenda (**69.8%** of agenda coverage as measured).
- **Scenario analysis and resilience**: climate scenario analysis is used to assess portfolio resilience under orderly and disorderly transition pathways, linking strategy and risk assessment to potential capital impacts.

### Methodologies

This subsection summarises the principal methodologies used to prepare climate-related disclosures, including risk rating, scenario analysis, and emissions accounting.

#### Climate risk rating methodology

Climate risk ratings in the climate risk register are derived using a **5×5 risk matrix** based on the product of a likelihood score and a severity score. The methodology is applied as follows:

- **Risk score** = likelihood score × severity score
- **Low**: scores **1–2**
- **Medium**: scores **3–6**
- **High**: scores **8–12**
- **Critical**: scores **15–25**

For example, the risk titled **“Physical risk — mortgage book flood exposure”** is assessed as **critical**, with a likelihood score of **4** and a severity score of **5** (risk score 20), and is monitored **monthly**.

#### Climate scenario analysis methodology

The Bank applies climate scenario analysis to the banking book using externally developed scenario pathways and a combination of top-down and loan-level modelling approaches.

Key methodologies applied include:

- **NGFS v4 Net Zero 2050** scenario applied to the banking book using a **top-down sector-pathway approach**. Carbon price assumptions are mapped from the **IEA NZE 2050** trajectory to counterparties by **NACE sector**. Stranded asset estimates are derived from **sector-level fossil fuel capex exposure**. The analysis scope covers **all lending book exposures** across **DE, FR, IT, NL and ES** jurisdictions.
- **NGFS v4 Below 2°C (orderly)** scenario applied using **NGFS integrated assessment model outputs** aligned to a **1.8°C median temperature pathway**. The carbon price trajectory is sourced from the **REMIND-MAgPIE** model. Financial impact is assessed through **loan-level probability of default sensitivity** to carbon cost pass-through, differentiated by **sector carbon intensity tier**.

Scenario analysis outputs are used to support resilience assessments. Under **orderly transition scenarios**, the portfolio is assessed as demonstrating **adequate resilience**, with transition risk losses remaining within **Pillar 2 capital buffer thresholds**. Under **disorderly transition scenarios**, near-term resilience is assessed as acceptable, while medium-term capital consumption from stranded asset impairments is assessed as material; sector exposure limits and portfolio decarbonisation glide-path monitoring are described as primary resilience mechanisms, and additional capital buffers may be required post-2030 under the most adverse disorderly assumptions.

#### Greenhouse gas emissions accounting (Scope 2 market-based)

For Scope 2 market-based emissions, the Bank’s Scope 2 market-based figure is sourced directly from utility invoice data that already reflects market-based accounting. Grid emission factors are adjusted for renewable energy certificates and power purchase agreements. Renewable energy certificate registry data is used for disclosure narrative (such as certificate type, volume and registry) and is not subtracted again during preparation, avoiding double-counting.

#### Financed emissions methodology note (sovereign bonds)

For financed emissions calculations aligned to PCAF concepts, the Bank notes that **issuer EVIC** and **issuer revenue** are corporate concepts and are not applicable to sovereign bonds; accordingly, these fields are treated as not applicable for sovereign bonds by design.

### Estimates and judgements

The preparation of sustainability-related financial disclosures involves estimates and judgements, particularly where outcomes depend on forward-looking assumptions or where measurement requires modelling.

Key areas where estimates and judgements are applied include:

- **Scenario analysis**: translating scenario pathways (including carbon price trajectories and temperature pathways) into sector-level and loan-level impacts requires judgement in mapping counterparties to sectors and in applying probability of default sensitivities.
- **Stranded asset estimation**: stranded asset estimates are derived from sector-level fossil fuel capex exposure, requiring judgement in how sector-level indicators are used to approximate exposure and vulnerability.
- **Climate risk assessment**: risk ratings are determined through the application of the risk matrix methodology, which requires judgement in assigning likelihood and severity scores.

### Data quality and key qualitative characteristics

The Bank’s disclosures are prepared to support the qualitative characteristics of useful information, including comparability, verifiability and understandability.

#### Emissions data quality profile

For emissions-related information, the Bank summarises the composition of data sources and estimation approaches as follows:

| Emissions data quality category | Share of emissions data (%) |
|---|---:|
| Estimated (economic-based) | 47.5 |
| CDP disclosure | 35.5 |
| Proxy model | 16.3 |
| Audited report | 0.7 |

This profile is intended to help users understand the extent to which emissions information is derived from reported data versus estimation and modelling.

#### Monitoring frequency and change tracking in the climate risk register

The climate risk register includes monitoring frequencies that vary by risk, including **monthly**, **quarterly**, and **semi-annual** monitoring. The register also tracks whether a risk has changed since the prior period for the reporting year **2023**, supporting consistency and comparability of risk information over time.

### Value chain boundary considerations (climate-related)

The Bank’s value chain mapping identifies climate-related nodes flagged as material within the Bank’s own operations, including **“Corporate fleet & business travel”**. This supports the inclusion of operational emissions sources such as fleet (included in Scope 1) and business travel (reported as Scope 3 travel).

### Consistency with related financial statements

Data and assumptions used in preparing the sustainability-related financial disclosures are prepared to be consistent, to the extent possible, with the corresponding data and assumptions used in preparing the related financial statements.
