## Governance

This Governance section describes how climate-related risks and opportunities are overseen and managed through the organisation’s governance arrangements, including board oversight, management committee structures, integration with enterprise risk management (ERM) and major-transaction decision-making, capability development, and the linkage between executive remuneration and climate-related performance considerations.

### Board oversight, agenda integration and reporting flow

Effective governance of climate-related risks and opportunities depends on clear board oversight, regular information flows, and structured agenda time to enable challenge and decision-making. The organisation’s governance arrangements reflect climate-related matters as a recurring topic for board consideration and review.

The board comprises **10** members and meets **four** times per year as a full board. Climate-related matters are integrated into board discussions, with climate included on the board agenda **69.8%** of the time. This agenda integration is evidenced by board discussions spanning multiple climate-related themes, including **transition plan review**, **scenario analysis**, **review of climate-related disclosures (TCFD review)**, **net zero progress**, and **approval of carbon credits**. Board minutes also show repeated consideration of **executive remuneration ESG KPIs**, indicating that climate-related performance considerations are discussed alongside broader governance and performance topics.

The reporting flow to support board oversight includes a defined cadence for climate risk reporting. Climate risk reporting is provided to the board on a **semi-annual** basis. This reporting frequency supports periodic board-level review of climate-related risks and opportunities and provides a structured mechanism for escalation and oversight.

Board discussions captured in minutes demonstrate how agenda integration supports oversight across strategic and performance dimensions:

- **Strategy and transition planning:** board minutes record **transition plan review** and **scenario analysis**, indicating that the board considers forward-looking climate-related information when overseeing strategic direction.
- **Disclosure and governance alignment:** board minutes record **TCFD review**, reflecting board engagement with climate-related disclosure considerations.
- **Progress monitoring:** board minutes record **net zero progress**, indicating board attention to progress tracking.
- **Decision-making on specific instruments:** board minutes record **carbon credit approval**, indicating that the board considers and approves climate-related instruments as part of governance oversight.
- **Incentives and accountability:** board minutes repeatedly record **executive remuneration ESG KPIs**, supporting board oversight of how climate-related performance considerations are reflected in remuneration.

Taken together, the board’s meeting structure, agenda integration, and semi-annual climate risk reporting cadence provide a governance framework intended to ensure climate-related matters are considered in oversight of strategy, performance monitoring, and selected approvals.

### Management-level responsibility and committee structures

Management-level governance is designed to translate board oversight into operational monitoring, coordination, and execution. A defined management committee structure supports the monitoring and management of sustainability- and climate-related risks and opportunities.

A dedicated committee structure is in place, with an **ESG committee** established. The ESG committee meets **five** times per year. In addition, management-level responsibility is delegated to a named management committee, identified as the **Group Sustainability Committee**.

These management-level structures support governance in several ways:

- **Coordination and escalation:** a management committee (Group Sustainability Committee) provides a focal point for consolidating climate-related topics and preparing inputs for board oversight.
- **Regular management attention:** the ESG committee’s meeting cadence (five meetings per year) supports recurring management review of sustainability- and climate-related matters.
- **Linkage to board oversight:** the semi-annual climate risk reporting to the board is supported by management-level monitoring and committee processes that can aggregate and synthesise information for board review.

The governance arrangements also reflect an integrated approach to sustainability- and climate-related oversight. Climate-related topics discussed at board level (such as scenario analysis, transition plan review, and net zero progress) are consistent with the type of matters typically coordinated through management committees and ESG governance processes, supporting a coherent flow from management monitoring to board oversight.

### ERM integration and major-transaction climate checks

Governance of climate-related risks and opportunities is strengthened when climate considerations are embedded in core risk management processes and applied to significant decisions. The organisation’s governance arrangements incorporate climate-related considerations into ERM and major-transaction decision-making.

Climate-related risks and opportunities are **integrated into enterprise risk management**, reflecting that climate is treated within the organisation’s broader risk governance framework. This integration supports consistent identification, assessment, and escalation of climate-related risks alongside other enterprise risks.

In addition, a **climate check is applied to major transactions**. This governance control indicates that climate-related considerations are incorporated into decision-making for significant transactions, supporting board and management oversight of how climate-related risks and opportunities may affect major commitments.

The combination of ERM integration and major-transaction climate checks supports governance connectivity across pillars:

- **Strategy oversight:** board discussions on **transition plan review** and **scenario analysis** align with an ERM-integrated approach, where forward-looking assessments can inform strategic oversight.
- **Risk management processes:** ERM integration provides a structured pathway for climate-related risks to be assessed and reported, consistent with the **semi-annual** reporting cadence to the board.
- **Decision-making discipline:** applying a climate check to major transactions embeds climate considerations into significant decisions, supporting governance oversight of transaction-related risk and opportunity evaluation.

### Skills, competence and development programme

Board and management oversight of climate-related risks and opportunities depends on appropriate skills and competencies, supported by ongoing development. The organisation’s governance arrangements include both an assessment of board-level climate expertise and a skills development programme.

Board-level climate expertise is quantified at **33.5%**. This indicates that approximately one-third of board members are identified as having climate expertise, supporting the board’s ability to oversee climate-related risks and opportunities and to engage with topics such as scenario analysis, transition planning, and progress monitoring.

A **skills development programme** is in place. This programme supports the development and maintenance of competencies relevant to sustainability- and climate-related oversight. In governance terms, the existence of a skills development programme complements the board’s quantified climate expertise by providing a mechanism to build and sustain capability over time.

The board’s recurring engagement with climate-related topics—such as **scenario analysis**, **transition plan review**, **TCFD review**, and **net zero progress**—is consistent with a governance approach that relies on both existing expertise and ongoing competence development to support informed oversight.

### Executive remuneration linkage and how governance information supports decision-making

Linking executive remuneration to climate-related performance considerations can reinforce accountability and support delivery of climate-related objectives. Governance information also supports decision-making by ensuring that climate-related topics are reviewed through structured forums and escalated to the board with an established cadence.

Climate-related performance considerations are reflected in executive remuneration through a quantified linkage: **8.7%** of executive remuneration is linked to climate-related considerations (expressed as the percentage of remuneration linked to climate). Board minutes further evidence that **executive remuneration ESG KPIs** are a recurring board discussion topic, indicating board-level oversight of how ESG and climate-related performance measures are incorporated into remuneration considerations.

The governance system supports decision-making through multiple reinforcing mechanisms:

- **Board agenda and oversight:** climate is included on the board agenda **69.8%** of the time, enabling the board to consider climate-related matters as part of routine oversight rather than only on an ad hoc basis.
- **Structured reporting cadence:** climate risk reporting to the board occurs **semi-annually**, supporting periodic review and enabling the board to consider climate-related information in oversight of risk and strategy.
- **Decision rights on specific climate-related matters:** board minutes record **carbon credit approval**, demonstrating that governance processes extend beyond monitoring to include approvals of specific climate-related instruments.
- **Progress and accountability:** board minutes record **net zero progress**, supporting governance monitoring of progress-related information and enabling the board to consider performance in the context of oversight and incentives.

By combining (i) recurring board agenda time, (ii) semi-annual climate risk reporting, (iii) management committee and ESG committee structures, (iv) ERM integration and major-transaction climate checks, and (v) remuneration linkage, the governance framework is designed to support informed oversight and decision-making on climate-related risks and opportunities across strategy, risk management, and performance monitoring.
