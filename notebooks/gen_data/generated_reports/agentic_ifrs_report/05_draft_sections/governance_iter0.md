## Governance

This section describes how climate-related risks and opportunities are overseen and governed, including how climate matters are integrated into board and management agendas, how information flows to decision-makers, and how governance mechanisms support strategy oversight, risk management and performance monitoring.

### Board oversight, agenda integration and reporting flow

Effective governance of climate-related risks and opportunities depends on clear board oversight, regular consideration of climate topics in board deliberations, and a defined reporting cadence that enables timely challenge and decision-making.

The Board provides oversight through its regular meeting cycle and through the integration of climate topics into its agenda. The Board comprises **10 directors** and holds **four full board meetings per year**. Climate is a recurring agenda item: climate-related matters are included on the board agenda **69.8%** of the time, indicating that climate considerations are routinely brought into board-level discussions rather than treated as exceptional items.

The Board’s oversight is supported by a defined reporting flow on climate risk. Climate risk reporting is provided to the Board on a **semi-annual** basis. This reporting cadence establishes a structured mechanism for the Board to be informed about climate-related risks and opportunities and to monitor developments over time.

Board materials and minutes evidence that the Board’s climate oversight extends beyond general updates and includes review and approval activities that are decision-relevant. Across multiple board meetings, climate topics discussed included:

- **Transition plan review** and **scenario analysis**, indicating that the Board considers forward-looking assessments and transition planning as part of its oversight.
- **TCFD review**, demonstrating that the Board reviews climate-related disclosure and reporting frameworks as part of governance and accountability.
- **Net zero progress**, indicating that the Board monitors progress against climate-related ambitions and associated delivery.
- **Carbon credit approval**, showing that the Board considers and approves specific climate-related instruments or actions.
- **Executive remuneration ESG KPIs**, evidencing that the Board considers how climate and broader ESG performance measures are reflected in executive incentives.

Taken together, these agenda items show that the Board’s oversight covers (i) strategic direction (transition plan review), (ii) resilience and forward-looking analysis (scenario analysis), (iii) reporting and accountability (TCFD review), (iv) progress monitoring (net zero progress), and (v) specific decisions (carbon credit approval) and performance levers (executive remuneration ESG KPIs). This governance approach supports connectivity to the Strategy and Risk Management pillars by ensuring that board oversight includes both forward-looking planning and risk-informed decision points.

### Management-level responsibility and committee structures

Clear management accountability and committee structures support consistent execution of climate-related governance expectations, including monitoring, coordination across functions, and escalation of material matters to the Board.

Management responsibility for sustainability and climate matters is delegated to a management-level committee structure. A management committee is in place, identified as the **Climate Risk Management Committee**. This committee structure provides a formal mechanism for management to monitor, manage and oversee climate-related risks and opportunities and to coordinate inputs that inform board oversight.

In addition, an **ESG committee** is established and meets **seven times per year**. The frequency of ESG committee meetings indicates an operating rhythm that supports ongoing management attention to sustainability and climate topics, including the ability to address emerging issues between board reporting cycles.

The combined governance architecture—management committee oversight through the Climate Risk Management Committee and the recurring ESG committee meeting cycle—supports a structured approach to:

- consolidating climate-related information for decision-making;
- coordinating climate-related actions and monitoring across the organisation; and
- enabling escalation and structured reporting to the Board in line with the semi-annual climate risk reporting cadence.

This management-level governance is designed to support the Board’s oversight by ensuring that climate-related risks and opportunities are actively managed and that relevant information is prepared for board consideration.

### ERM integration and major-transaction climate checks

Embedding climate considerations into enterprise risk management (ERM) and major transaction governance helps ensure that climate-related risks and opportunities are assessed consistently and are reflected in decisions with potentially significant financial implications.

Climate-related risks and opportunities are integrated into the organisation’s **enterprise risk management** processes. ERM integration **applies**, indicating that climate is treated as part of the broader risk management framework rather than as a standalone topic. This integration supports consistent identification, assessment and monitoring of climate-related risks alongside other principal risks.

Climate considerations are also incorporated into governance for significant decisions. A **climate check for major transactions applies**, indicating that climate-related risks and opportunities are considered when evaluating major transactions. This mechanism supports the Board’s oversight of major decisions by ensuring that climate-related considerations are incorporated into transaction evaluation and governance processes.

The combination of ERM integration and major-transaction climate checks provides a governance pathway from risk identification through to decision execution:

- ERM integration supports systematic assessment and monitoring of climate-related risks and opportunities.
- Major-transaction climate checks embed climate considerations into decision gates for significant transactions.
- Semi-annual climate risk reporting to the Board provides a structured channel for oversight and challenge.

This governance design supports decision-useful information flows by linking climate risk assessment processes to board oversight and to transaction governance.

### Skills, competence and development programme

Board and management oversight of climate-related risks and opportunities depends on having appropriate skills and competencies, and on maintaining those capabilities through structured development.

The Board’s climate competence is evidenced through the proportion of directors with climate expertise. **37.5%** of the Board is assessed as having climate expertise. This provides a basis for informed oversight and challenge on climate-related matters, including transition planning, scenario analysis and climate-related reporting.

A **skills development programme applies**, indicating that the organisation maintains a structured approach to developing relevant skills and competencies for sustainability and climate oversight. In governance terms, this supports the Board and management in maintaining the capability to oversee strategies designed to respond to climate-related risks and opportunities.

The combination of quantified board climate expertise and an active skills development programme supports continuity of oversight as climate-related expectations, methodologies and regulatory requirements evolve. It also supports the Board’s ability to engage with management on technical topics evidenced in board discussions, including scenario analysis and review of climate-related reporting.

### Executive remuneration linkage and how governance information supports decision-making

Linking executive remuneration to climate-related performance metrics can reinforce accountability for delivery of climate-related objectives and can strengthen the connection between governance oversight and operational execution.

Climate-related performance measures are incorporated into executive remuneration. Across the executive population, **15.1%** of executive remuneration is linked to climate-related measures. Board minutes further evidence that **executive remuneration ESG KPIs** are a recurring board discussion topic, indicating that the Board actively considers how sustainability and climate performance measures are reflected in incentive structures.

This remuneration linkage is part of the governance system used to monitor progress and reinforce delivery. In practice, the governance information flow supports decision-making in several ways:

- **Board agenda integration** (climate included on the agenda 69.8% of the time) ensures that climate-related performance and decisions are considered routinely.
- **Semi-annual climate risk reporting** provides a structured basis for the Board to review climate-related risks and opportunities and to assess whether management actions remain aligned with the organisation’s climate-related direction.
- **Board review topics**—including transition plan review, scenario analysis, TCFD review, net zero progress and carbon credit approval—demonstrate that the Board uses climate-related information to oversee strategic direction, evaluate forward-looking resilience considerations, monitor progress, and approve specific climate-related actions.
- **Executive remuneration linkage** (15.1% of executive remuneration linked to climate-related measures) provides an accountability mechanism that aligns management incentives with climate-related performance considerations discussed at Board level.

By combining structured reporting, recurring agenda coverage, management committee oversight, ERM integration and incentive alignment, the governance framework supports consistent consideration of climate-related risks and opportunities in oversight and decision-making processes.
