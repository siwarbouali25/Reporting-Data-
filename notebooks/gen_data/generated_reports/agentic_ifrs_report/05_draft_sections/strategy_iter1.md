## Strategy

### Climate-related risks and opportunities that could affect prospects

The bank has identified climate-related risks affecting its lending and collateral exposures, including:

- **Physical risk — mortgage book flood exposure (long term)**: Residential and commercial mortgage collateral located in flood-prone zones faces rising acute physical risk; property damage and devaluation could impair collateral coverage (linked to scenario analysis **SCN0009**).
- **Physical risk — wildfire exposure in southern portfolio (medium term)**: Risk rated **medium** (linked to scenario analysis **SCN0007**).
- **Transition risk — building energy performance regulation (short to medium term)**: Minimum energy performance standards may require costly retrofits for low-EPC mortgage collateral, reducing values and increasing loan-to-value (LTV); risk rated **medium** (linked to scenario analysis **SCN0011**).
- **Stranded assets — fossil fuel sector exposure (medium to long term)**: Demand shifts away from fossil fuels could strand reserves and assets held by oil, gas and coal counterparties, reducing their enterprise value.
- **Carbon pricing and policy tightening (short to medium term)**: Tightening EU ETS and national carbon pricing could raise operating costs for carbon-intensive borrowers, increasing default probability in cement, steel and chemicals (linked to scenario analysis **SCN0007**).
- **Technology transition risk (automotive) (medium term)**: Rapid electrification could strand internal-combustion vehicle manufacturers and suppliers in the lending book.
- **Physical risk (agriculture) (medium term)**: Chronic heat and shifting precipitation could reduce yields for agricultural counterparties, weakening repayment capacity over the medium term (linked to scenario analysis **SCN0005**).
- **Reputational risk (short term)**: Stakeholder and NGO scrutiny of financed emissions and fossil financing could damage brand and trigger client attrition.

The bank has also identified climate-related opportunities that could affect prospects:

- **Green loan growth** (**OPP-BANK01-01**, **medium term**).
- **Sustainable bond underwriting** (**OPP-BANK01-02**, **short term**).
- **Transition advisory** (**OPP-BANK01-03**, **short to medium term**): Fee income from climate risk advisory, transition planning support, and ESG due diligence services offered to large corporate clients.
- **Renewable energy project finance** (**OPP-BANK01-04**, **medium term**): Project finance for solar, wind, and battery storage infrastructure aligned with REPowerEU and national renewable energy targets.

### Current and anticipated effects on the business model and value chain

Climate-related risks and opportunities are concentrated in the bank’s value chain primarily through **financing counterparties** and collateral exposures. Key concentrations include:

- **Mortgage collateral** in flood-prone zones, where acute physical risk can transmit through property damage and devaluation, affecting collateral coverage.
- **Southern portfolio exposures** subject to wildfire risk.
- **Carbon-intensive sectors** exposed to transition risk from carbon pricing and demand shifts, including lending to the **manufacture of coke and refined petroleum**, which carries elevated transition risk from carbon pricing and demand shifts.
- **Automotive manufacturing exposures**, where the manufacture of motor vehicles is subject to moderate physical and transition risk.
- **Agricultural counterparties**, where chronic heat and shifting precipitation can reduce yields and weaken repayment capacity.

Within the bank’s own operations, operational sites and travel generate Scope 1–2 emissions and face physical disruption; this includes **corporate fleet and business travel**.

### Effects on strategy and decision-making

#### Time horizons used in strategic decision-making

For the purposes of climate-related strategy and decision-making, the bank uses the following time horizons, aligned to internal planning and risk processes:

- **Short term**: **0–3 years** (aligned to annual budgeting, ICAAP cycle and near-term risk appetite setting).
- **Medium term**: **3–10 years** (aligned to the strategic plan cycle, portfolio steering and client transition engagement).
- **Long term**: **10+ years** (aligned to long-dated mortgage and infrastructure exposures and to physical risk assessment horizons, including 2050 analyses).

#### Strategic response

The bank’s strategy incorporates climate-related considerations through portfolio actions and client engagement. Under orderly transition scenarios, the bank expects **green loan growth** and **client engagement on transition plans** to progressively reduce high-carbon sector concentration over the medium term.

The bank has set a portfolio intensity target of **20.0 tCO₂e per €m**.

#### Trade-offs considered

In implementing its climate strategy, the bank considers trade-offs, including:

- **Near-term revenue vs. transition risk reduction**: tightening sector limits and underwriting standards can reduce near-term revenue in carbon-intensive sectors while lowering expected credit losses and stranded-asset risk over the medium term.
- **Client support vs. reputational and policy risk**: continuing to finance certain high-emitting clients to enable credible transition plans may increase reputational scrutiny in the short term, while abrupt withdrawal could increase credit risk and reduce influence on real-economy decarbonisation.
- **Growth in green assets vs. concentration and execution risk**: scaling renewable energy and retrofit financing can increase exposure to technology, policy and supply-chain constraints, requiring enhanced due diligence and portfolio limits.

### Resilience of strategy and business model (scenario analysis)

The bank assesses resilience using scenario analysis applied to the banking book.

- **Orderly transition scenarios (NGFS v4 Net Zero 2050; Below 2°C (orderly))**: Applied using a top-down sector-pathway approach, mapping carbon price assumptions (including IEA NZE 2050 trajectory) to counterparty NACE sectors and deriving stranded asset estimates from sector-level fossil fuel capex exposure. The analysis scope covers **all banking book exposures across DE, FR, IT, NL, ES**. Under these scenarios, the portfolio demonstrates **adequate resilience**, with transition risk losses remaining within **Pillar 2 capital buffer thresholds**. Reported revenue-at-risk includes **€9,689.2m** (scenario **SCN0001**) and **€9,214.0m** (medium-term horizon).

- **Disorderly transition scenarios (NGFS v4 Divergent Net Zero)**: Used to capture technology pathway uncertainty, with varying carbon price assumptions by country based on national ETS and carbon tax trajectories, and applied primarily to stress-test technology transition risk in the automotive and energy manufacturing lending sub-portfolio. Under these scenarios, near-term resilience is **acceptable**, but medium-term capital consumption from stranded asset impairments is **material**. The bank’s **sector exposure limits** and **active portfolio decarbonisation glide-path monitoring** are described as primary resilience mechanisms; additional capital buffers may be required post-2030 under the most adverse disorderly assumptions.

- **Hot house world scenarios (NGFS v4 Current Policies; NGFS v4 NDC)**: Physical risk parameters are sourced from NGFS chronic and acute hazard layers and downscaled to counterparty postcode and collateral location, with RCP 8.5 physical risk projections used for a 2050 horizon analysis. Under these scenarios, physical risk losses represent the dominant long-term resilience challenge. The primary transmission channels identified are **collateral revaluation risk in flood-exposed mortgage books** and **agricultural borrower default risk from chronic heat stress**. The bank’s **flood-zone overlay** and **EPC-based collateral monitoring** are described as resilience mechanisms deployed for the 2050 horizon.
