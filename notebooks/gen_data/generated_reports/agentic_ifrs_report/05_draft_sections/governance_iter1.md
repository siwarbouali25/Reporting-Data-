## 1. Governance

This section describes governance arrangements for climate-related risks and opportunities, including Board oversight, management committee structures, reporting flows, and integration with enterprise risk management and major-transaction decision processes.

### 1.1 Board oversight, agenda integration and reporting flow

The Board is the governance body responsible for oversight of climate-related risks and opportunities. The Board comprises **10 directors** and holds **four full board meetings per year**. Climate-related matters are included on the Board agenda **69.8%** of the time.

The Board is informed about climate-related risks and opportunities through a defined reporting cadence. Climate risk reporting is provided to the Board on a **semi-annual** basis.

Board minutes record that climate-related topics discussed by the Board include:

- **Transition plan review**
- **Scenario analysis**
- **TCFD review**
- **Net zero progress**
- **Carbon credit approval**
- **Executive remuneration ESG KPIs**

These topics evidence Board engagement across forward-looking planning (transition plan review and scenario analysis), climate-related reporting (TCFD review), progress monitoring (net zero progress), specific climate-related decisions (carbon credit approval), and incentive-related governance (executive remuneration ESG KPIs).

Refer to [Section: Strategy] for how transition planning and scenario analysis inform the entity’s climate-related strategic planning.

### 1.2 Management-level responsibility and committee structures

Management responsibility for climate-related matters is delegated to a management-level committee. The management committee identified for this role is the **Climate Risk Management Committee**.

An **ESG committee** is also established. The ESG committee meets **seven times per year**.

Together, these management-level committees provide a structured forum for management discussion and coordination of climate-related matters, and for preparing inputs that are reported to the Board through the semi-annual climate risk reporting cadence.

Refer to [Section: Risk Management] for how management governance connects to the entity’s climate-related risk identification, assessment and monitoring processes.

### 1.3 ERM integration and major-transaction climate checks

Climate-related risks and opportunities are integrated into the organisation’s **enterprise risk management (ERM)** processes.

Climate considerations are also incorporated into governance for significant decisions. A **climate check for major transactions applies**, meaning climate-related risks and opportunities are considered when evaluating major transactions.

These arrangements link climate-related risk identification and assessment (through ERM integration) with decision processes for significant transactions (through the major-transaction climate check), and with Board oversight through semi-annual climate risk reporting.

Refer to [Section: Risk Management] for further detail on how climate-related risks are managed within ERM.

### 1.4 Skills, competence and development programme

Board oversight of climate-related risks and opportunities is supported by climate-related skills and competence. **37.5%** of the Board is assessed as having climate expertise.

A **skills development programme applies**, supporting the development of relevant skills and competencies for sustainability and climate oversight.

Refer to [Section: Strategy] for how governance capability supports oversight of climate-related strategic responses.

### 1.5 Executive remuneration linkage and governance information used in decision-making

Climate-related performance measures are incorporated into executive remuneration. Across the executive population, **15.1%** of executive remuneration is linked to climate-related measures.

Board minutes record that **executive remuneration ESG KPIs** are among the climate-related topics discussed by the Board. This links Board oversight of climate-related performance considerations with remuneration-related governance.

Across the governance arrangements described in this section, decision-making is supported through:

- routine Board agenda coverage of climate-related matters (**69.8%** of agendas);
- a defined cadence for Board information (**semi-annual** climate risk reporting);
- management committee structures (the **Climate Risk Management Committee** and an **ESG committee** meeting **seven times per year**);
- integration of climate-related risks and opportunities into **ERM**; and
- application of a **climate check** for **major transactions**.

Refer to [Section: Metrics and Targets] for the climate-related metrics and targets that are monitored and used in performance management, including those linked to remuneration.
