## 1. Governance

This section describes the governance arrangements used to oversee climate-related risks and opportunities, including board oversight, management committee structures, integration with enterprise risk management (ERM) and major-transaction decision-making, capability development, and the linkage between executive remuneration and climate-related performance considerations.

The governance disclosures in this section are presented on an integrated basis for climate-related and broader sustainability-related matters, reflecting that oversight is addressed through the same governance bodies, reporting cadence and committee structures.

### 1.1 Board oversight, agenda integration and reporting flow

Board oversight of climate-related risks and opportunities is supported through board composition, agenda integration and a defined reporting cadence.

**Board composition and meeting cadence.** The board comprises **10** members and meets **four** times per year as a full board.

**Agenda integration.** Climate-related matters are integrated into board discussions, with climate included on the board agenda **69.8%** of the time.

**Topics evidenced in board minutes.** Board minutes record consideration of multiple climate-related themes, including:

- **Transition plan review** (board_minutes[0]; board_minutes[8])
- **Scenario analysis** (board_minutes[8])
- **Review of climate-related disclosures (TCFD review)** (board_minutes[2]; board_minutes[8])
- **Net zero progress** (board_minutes[34]; board_minutes[35])
- **Approval of carbon credits** (board_minutes[2]; board_minutes[34])
- **Executive remuneration ESG KPIs** (board_minutes[0]; board_minutes[2]; board_minutes[8]; board_minutes[34]; board_minutes[35])

These recorded topics show that board oversight spans forward-looking assessment (for example, scenario analysis), strategic direction (transition plan review), disclosure governance (TCFD review), progress monitoring (net zero progress), and specific approvals (carbon credit approval), alongside consideration of remuneration-related ESG KPIs.

**Reporting flow and frequency.** Climate risk reporting is provided to the board on a **semi-annual** basis. This semi-annual reporting cadence provides a structured mechanism for the board to receive climate risk information and to review climate-related risks and opportunities as part of its oversight.

Connectivity: The board’s agenda integration and semi-annual climate risk reporting cadence link governance oversight to the organisation’s approach to strategy oversight (transition plan review and scenario analysis), risk oversight (climate risk reporting), and performance monitoring (net zero progress and remuneration-related ESG KPIs).

### 1.2 Management-level responsibility and committee structures

Management-level governance is designed to support monitoring and coordination of climate-related risks and opportunities and to provide inputs for board oversight.

**Delegated management committee.** Management-level responsibility is delegated to a named management committee, the **Group Sustainability Committee**.

**ESG committee.** A dedicated **ESG committee** is established and meets **five** times per year.

These management-level structures provide recurring forums for management review of sustainability- and climate-related matters and support the preparation of information for escalation and reporting to the board, including through the semi-annual climate risk reporting cadence.

Connectivity: The management committee and ESG committee meeting cadence supports the flow of climate-related information to the board and aligns with the board’s recurring consideration of transition planning, scenario analysis, disclosure review, net zero progress and remuneration-related ESG KPIs.

### 1.3 ERM integration and major-transaction climate checks

Embedding climate-related considerations in core risk management processes and significant decisions is a governance mechanism used to support consistent oversight.

**ERM integration.** Climate-related risks and opportunities are integrated into **enterprise risk management (ERM)**.

**Major-transaction climate check.** A **climate check** is applied to **major transactions**.

Together, these arrangements indicate that climate-related considerations are incorporated into (i) the organisation’s broader risk governance framework through ERM and (ii) decision-making for significant transactions through a climate check.

Connectivity: ERM integration and the major-transaction climate check connect board oversight of climate-related matters (including transition plan review and scenario analysis recorded in board minutes) with the organisation’s risk management processes and decision-making on significant commitments.

### 1.4 Skills, competence and development programme

Oversight of climate-related risks and opportunities depends on the availability of relevant skills and competencies and on ongoing capability development.

**Board climate expertise.** Board-level climate expertise is quantified at **33.5%**.

**Skills development programme.** A **skills development programme** is in place to support sustainability- and climate-related oversight capability.

The board’s recorded engagement with climate-related topics in board minutes—such as scenario analysis, transition plan review, TCFD review and net zero progress—sits alongside the quantified board climate expertise and the existence of a skills development programme.

Connectivity: The quantified board climate expertise and the skills development programme support the board’s ability to review and challenge climate-related information presented through the semi-annual reporting cadence and through agenda items such as scenario analysis and transition plan review.

### 1.5 Executive remuneration linkage and how governance information supports decision-making

Executive remuneration linkage is one mechanism used to reflect climate-related performance considerations within governance.

**Remuneration linkage.** **8.7%** of executive remuneration is linked to climate-related considerations.

**Board oversight of remuneration-related ESG KPIs.** Board minutes record **executive remuneration ESG KPIs** as a recurring discussion topic (board_minutes[0]; board_minutes[2]; board_minutes[8]; board_minutes[34]; board_minutes[35]).

**How governance information supports decision-making.** The governance arrangements described above support decision-making through:

- **Recurring board agenda time** for climate-related matters (climate included on the board agenda **69.8%** of the time).
- **A defined reporting cadence** (climate risk reporting to the board is **semi-annual**).
- **Board consideration of specific climate-related approvals**, including **carbon credit approval** recorded in board minutes (board_minutes[2]; board_minutes[34]).
- **Board monitoring of progress-related information**, including **net zero progress** recorded in board minutes (board_minutes[34]; board_minutes[35]).

Connectivity: The linkage of **8.7%** of executive remuneration to climate-related considerations, together with board discussion of remuneration-related ESG KPIs, connects governance oversight to performance monitoring and accountability mechanisms used in the organisation’s climate-related governance framework.
