## Risk Management

This section describes the processes used to identify, assess, prioritise and monitor climate-related risks, and how those processes are integrated into overall risk management.

### Risk identification

Climate-related risks are identified and documented in a climate risk register. The register captures risk statements and supporting attributes used to support consistent assessment and ongoing monitoring.

Identification considers risks arising from the entity’s own operations and from the value chain. Examples of value-chain risk themes reflected in the mapping of climate risk descriptions include:

- **Own operations**: operational sites and travel generate Scope 1–2 emissions and face physical disruption.
- **Financed activities / lending**: lending to **other mining and quarrying** carries elevated transition risk from carbon pricing and demand shifts.

The climate risk register also includes risks linked to counterparties’ exposure to physical climate hazards. For example, one risk description in the register states that **chronic heat and shifting precipitation reduce yields for agricultural counterparties, weakening repayment capacity over the medium term**.

### Inputs and parameters used in assessment

Risk identification and assessment draw on external climate data inputs for physical risk exposure analysis. Physical risk exposure data used in the process is sourced from **ECB climate indicators**.

Scenario analysis is used to inform the identification and assessment of climate-related risks. Individual risks in the climate risk register are linked to scenario analysis references, including **SCN0005**, **SCN0007**, **SCN0009**, and **SCN0011**. These references provide traceability between the risk register and the scenario analysis work used to inform the risk view.

### Risk assessment methodology (likelihood, severity and overall rating)

Climate-related risks in the register are assessed using a **5×5 risk matrix**. The overall risk score is calculated as the product of a **likelihood score** and a **severity score**. The resulting score is translated into a risk rating using the following thresholds:

- **Critical**: scores from **15 to 25**
- **High**: scores from **8 to 12**
- **Medium**: scores from **3 to 6**
- **Low**: scores from **1 to 2**

Likelihood is captured as a numeric score in the climate risk register (examples include likelihood scores of **3**, **4**, and **5** across different risks). The risk register also records the resulting qualitative risk rating for some risks; for example, several risks are rated **medium**, including the agricultural counterparty risk described above.

### Prioritisation

Prioritisation is performed through the risk matrix outcomes and the resulting risk ratings. Risks assessed as higher on the matrix (for example, those that would fall into the high or critical score ranges) are prioritised for management attention relative to risks assessed as medium or low.

The climate risk register also supports prioritisation through structured attributes that enable comparison across risks, including likelihood scoring, risk rating, scenario analysis linkage, and defined monitoring frequency.

### Monitoring and review cadence

Climate-related risks are monitored on a defined cadence recorded in the climate risk register. Monitoring frequencies applied across risks include:

- **Monthly** monitoring for some risks
- **Quarterly** monitoring for some risks
- **Semi-annual** monitoring for some risks

This cadence-based approach supports ongoing review of risk status and enables escalation and management attention to be aligned with the assessed risk profile.

The register also tracks whether a risk entry has changed since the prior reporting period. Within the register, some risks are marked as having changed since the prior period, while others are unchanged. This supports structured review of developments in the risk profile over time.

### Integration into overall risk management (ERM)

Climate-related risk management is integrated into overall risk management through the use of ERM integration flags within the climate risk register. A number of risks in the register are identified as integrated into ERM.

This integration is intended to ensure that climate-related risks are assessed, prioritised and monitored using consistent methods and that climate-related risk information can inform broader risk management activities.

### Illustrative examples from the climate risk register

The climate risk register includes risk statements and attributes that support consistent assessment and monitoring. Examples evidenced in the register include:

- **Agricultural counterparty physical risk transmission**: chronic heat and shifting precipitation reduce yields for agricultural counterparties, weakening repayment capacity over the medium term. This risk is assessed as **medium** in the register.
- **Scenario-linked risks**: multiple risks are explicitly linked to scenario analysis references (including **SCN0005**, **SCN0007**, **SCN0009**, and **SCN0011**), supporting traceability between scenario analysis and the risk register.
- **Cadence-based monitoring**: risks are assigned monitoring frequencies (monthly, quarterly, or semi-annual), supporting structured oversight and review.

### Avoiding unnecessary duplication

Where climate-related risk oversight and monitoring are managed on an integrated basis through the climate risk register and its linkage to overall risk management, disclosures are provided in an integrated manner to avoid unnecessary duplication while still explaining the climate-related risk processes, inputs, assessment approach, monitoring cadence, and ERM integration.
