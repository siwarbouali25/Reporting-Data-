The climate risk register is used to document climate-related risks and related assessment attributes.

## 3.1 Identify: documenting climate-related risks

This subsection describes how climate-related risks are identified and recorded for ongoing management.

Climate-related risks are documented in the climate risk register using a risk description. Risk descriptions include risks that arise through counterparties and financed activities as well as risks associated with the entity’s own operations.

Value-chain mapping is used to capture examples of where climate-related risks can arise across operations and counterparties. Examples recorded include:

- **Own operations**: operational sites and travel generate Scope 1–2 emissions and face physical disruption.
- **Financed activities / counterparties**: lending to other mining and quarrying carries elevated transition risk from carbon pricing and demand shifts.
- **Counterparty physical risk**: chronic heat and shifting precipitation reduce yields for agricultural counterparties, weakening repayment capacity over the medium term.

These descriptions are recorded as climate-related risk statements and provide the basis for subsequent assessment and monitoring.

## 3.2 Assess: inputs, scenario references, and risk rating methodology

This subsection describes the inputs and parameters used in assessing climate-related risks, including how likelihood and magnitude are reflected in the risk rating methodology.

### Inputs and parameters

For physical risk exposure assessment, ECB climate indicators are used as a data source. ECB climate indicators are recorded as the data source across multiple physical risk exposure records.

### Scenario references

The climate risk register includes scenario reference links for individual risks. Scenario references recorded include **SCN0005**, **SCN0007**, **SCN0009**, and **SCN0011**.

### Likelihood and magnitude reflected in the risk matrix

The climate risk register records a **likelihood score** on a five-point scale. Likelihood scores recorded include **3**, **4**, and **5**.

The overall risk rating is derived from a **5×5 risk matrix** that combines likelihood and severity into a single score. The score is calculated by multiplying the likelihood score by a severity score.

The resulting score is mapped to rating bands as follows:

- **Critical**: scores from **15 to 25**
- **High**: scores from **8 to 12**
- **Medium**: scores from **3 to 6**
- **Low**: scores from **1 to 2**

In addition, the methodology applies the following thresholds: scores of **15 or above** are classified as critical; scores of **8 or above** are classified as high; scores of **3 or above** are classified as medium; and scores below **3** are classified as low.

Risk ratings recorded in the climate risk register include **“medium”** for multiple risks.

## 3.3 Prioritise: using rating bands within the climate risk register

This subsection describes how assessed ratings are used to order attention within the climate risk register.

Within the climate risk register, risks are recorded with an overall rating band (low, medium, high, critical) derived from the 5×5 risk matrix methodology described above. The register includes multiple risks recorded with a **medium** rating.

## 3.4 Monitor: cadence and change tracking

This subsection describes how climate-related risks are monitored through defined review frequencies and change indicators recorded in the climate risk register.

### Monitoring cadence

The climate risk register specifies a monitoring frequency for individual risks. Monitoring frequencies recorded include:

- **Monthly**
- **Quarterly**
- **Semi-annual**

### Change tracking

The climate risk register includes an indicator of whether a risk has changed since the prior reporting period. The register includes risks recorded as changed since the prior period and risks recorded as not changed since the prior period.

*Refer to [Section: Governance – Risk oversight] for information on how risk information is overseen and reviewed.*

## 3.5 Integration and value-chain coverage

This subsection describes how the climate risk register records whether a climate-related risk is integrated into ERM and how value-chain exposures are reflected.

### ERM integration status

The climate risk register includes an ERM integration status for individual risks. The register includes multiple risks recorded as integrated into ERM and at least one risk recorded as not integrated into ERM.

### Value-chain examples recorded

Climate-related risks are recorded with descriptions that include value-chain pathways. Examples recorded include:

- operational sites and travel generating Scope 1–2 emissions and facing physical disruption;
- lending to other mining and quarrying with elevated transition risk from carbon pricing and demand shifts; and
- chronic heat and shifting precipitation reducing yields for agricultural counterparties and weakening repayment capacity over the medium term.
