## 3. Risk Management

This section explains how climate-related risks are identified, assessed, prioritised and monitored, and how the resulting information is integrated into the entity’s overall risk management process. The processes described are implemented through a climate risk register that records risk statements and key assessment and monitoring attributes.

### 3.1 Identify climate-related risks

Climate-related risks are identified and documented in a climate risk register. The register includes risk descriptions and supporting attributes that enable consistent assessment, comparison across risks and ongoing monitoring.

Identification considers risks arising from the entity’s own operations and from the value chain. Examples of climate risk themes reflected in the value-chain mapping include:

- **Own operations**: operational sites and travel generate Scope 1–2 emissions and face physical disruption.
- **Financed activities / lending**: lending to other mining and quarrying carries elevated transition risk from carbon pricing and demand shifts.

The climate risk register also captures risk descriptions that reflect how physical climate hazards can transmit through counterparties. For example, one risk description states that chronic heat and shifting precipitation reduce yields for agricultural counterparties, weakening repayment capacity over the medium term.

### 3.2 Inputs and parameters used in risk identification and assessment

The identification and assessment of climate-related risks draw on external climate data inputs for physical risk exposure analysis. Physical risk exposure data used in the process is sourced from **ECB climate indicators**.

Climate-related scenario analysis is also used to inform the identification and assessment of climate-related risks. Individual risks in the climate risk register are linked to internal scenario references, including **SCN0005**, **SCN0007**, **SCN0009** and **SCN0011**, providing traceability between the risk register and scenario analysis work.

### 3.3 Assess climate-related risks (likelihood, magnitude and rating)

Climate-related risks in the register are assessed using a **5×5 risk matrix**. Each risk is assigned a likelihood score and a severity score. The overall risk score is determined by combining the likelihood and severity scores, and the resulting score is translated into a qualitative risk rating.

Risk ratings are assigned using the following score ranges:

- **Critical**: scores from **15 to 25**
- **High**: scores from **8 to 12**
- **Medium**: scores from **3 to 6**
- **Low**: scores from **1 to 2**

Likelihood is captured as a numeric score in the climate risk register. Likelihood scores evidenced in the register include **3**, **4** and **5** across different risks.

The register also records qualitative risk ratings for individual risks. For example, the agricultural counterparty risk described above is rated **medium**.

### 3.4 Prioritise climate-related risks

Climate-related risks are prioritised based on their position in the risk matrix and the resulting risk rating. Risks with higher scores (those falling within the high or critical ranges) are prioritised for management attention relative to risks assessed as medium or low.

The climate risk register supports prioritisation by enabling comparison across risks using structured attributes, including likelihood scoring, risk rating, scenario linkage and defined monitoring frequency.

### 3.5 Monitor and review climate-related risks

Climate-related risks are monitored on a defined cadence recorded in the climate risk register. Monitoring frequencies applied across risks include:

- **Monthly** monitoring for some risks
- **Quarterly** monitoring for some risks
- **Semi-annual** monitoring for some risks

This cadence-based monitoring supports regular review of risk status and helps align management attention with the assessed risk profile.

The climate risk register also records whether a risk entry has changed since the prior reporting period. The register includes risks identified as having changed since the prior period and risks identified as unchanged, supporting structured review of developments in the risk profile over time.

### 3.6 Integration into overall risk management (ERM)

Climate-related risk management is integrated into overall risk management through the use of ERM integration indicators recorded in the climate risk register. The register includes risks identified as integrated into ERM.

This integration enables climate-related risks to be assessed, prioritised and monitored using consistent methods and allows climate-related risk information captured in the register to inform broader risk management activities.

### 3.7 Illustrative examples evidenced in the climate risk register

The climate risk register includes risk statements and attributes that support consistent assessment and monitoring. Examples evidenced in the register include:

- **Agricultural counterparty physical risk transmission**: chronic heat and shifting precipitation reduce yields for agricultural counterparties, weakening repayment capacity over the medium term. This risk is rated **medium**.
- **Scenario-linked risks**: multiple risks are explicitly linked to internal scenario references, including **SCN0005**, **SCN0007**, **SCN0009** and **SCN0011**, supporting traceability between scenario analysis and the risk register.
- **Cadence-based monitoring**: risks are assigned monitoring frequencies (monthly, quarterly or semi-annual), supporting structured oversight and review.

### 3.8 Avoiding unnecessary duplication

Where climate-related risk oversight and monitoring are managed on an integrated basis through the climate risk register and its linkage to overall risk management, disclosures are provided in an integrated manner to avoid unnecessary duplication while still explaining the climate-related risk processes, inputs, assessment approach, monitoring cadence and ERM integration.
