## Risk Management

### Risk identification and assessment

This section describes the processes used to identify, assess, prioritise and monitor climate-related risks, and how those processes are integrated into overall risk management.

**Risk register as the core record of climate-related risks.** Climate-related risks are captured and maintained in a climate risk register. The register is used as the central artefact to document key attributes of each risk, including (i) links to climate-related scenario analysis where used, (ii) assessment inputs used to derive risk ratings, and (iii) the cadence at which each risk is monitored.

**Inputs and parameters used in the process (including data sources).** The organisation uses external physical risk exposure information as an input to its climate-related risk processes. Physical risk exposure data sources include **ECB climate indicators**. These inputs support the identification and assessment of physical climate-related risks and are used alongside the organisation’s internal risk documentation in the climate risk register.

**Use of climate-related scenario analysis in risk identification.** Climate-related scenario analysis is used to inform the identification of climate-related risks, and scenario references are captured in the climate risk register. Scenario links recorded in the register include **SCN0009**, **SCN0007**, **SCN0011** and **SCN0005**. Recording scenario references in the register provides traceability between identified risks and the scenario analysis used to inform their identification.

**Assessment approach—likelihood scoring and risk rating methodology.** Climate-related risks are assessed using a likelihood scoring approach on a **1–5** scale, with likelihood scores recorded in the climate risk register (examples of recorded likelihood scores include **3**, **4** and **5**).

Risk ratings in the climate risk register are derived from a **5x5** risk matrix using the formula **likelihood_score * severity_score**. The rating thresholds applied are:

- **Scores 1–2 = low**
- **Scores 3–6 = medium**
- **Scores 8–12 = high**
- **Scores 15–25 = critical**

Equivalently, the thresholds are expressed as: **critical >= 15, high >= 8, medium >= 3, low < 3**.

The use of a consistent matrix-based methodology supports comparability of climate-related risk ratings across the register and provides a structured basis for prioritisation and monitoring.

**Risk rating outcomes recorded in the register.** The climate risk register includes risks rated **“medium”**. The presence of “medium” ratings in the register demonstrates application of the rating methodology to climate-related risks and provides a basis for differentiating monitoring intensity and management attention across risks.

**Illustrative physical risk captured in the register.** An example climate-related physical risk described in the climate risk register is that **chronic heat and shifting precipitation reduce yields for agricultural counterparties, weakening repayment capacity over the medium term**. This example illustrates how physical climate drivers can translate into counterparty impacts over time and be documented as a discrete risk within the register.

**Prioritisation and linkage to overall risk management.** Climate-related risks are managed in connection with the organisation’s overall enterprise risk management process. The climate risk register indicates that climate-related risk management is integrated into the organisation’s overall risk management process for many risks. This integration supports consistent assessment and prioritisation of climate-related risks alongside other risk types using common risk documentation and rating approaches.

### Monitoring and change over time

This subsection explains how climate-related risks are monitored and how changes are tracked over time.

**Defined monitoring frequencies.** Climate-related risks are monitored at defined frequencies as recorded in the climate risk register. Monitoring cadences recorded in the register include **monthly**, **quarterly** and **semi-annual**. Using defined monitoring frequencies supports a repeatable monitoring process and helps ensure that risks are revisited on a schedule aligned to their characteristics and management needs.

**Tracking changes since the prior reporting period.** The climate risk register includes an indicator of whether a risk has changed since the prior reporting period. The register shows that **some climate-related risks have changed since the prior reporting period**, while others have not. This approach provides a structured way to track changes in the risk profile over time within the same register used for identification, assessment and monitoring.

### Value chain considerations reflected in risk identification

This subsection summarises how climate-related risks are considered across parts of the value chain, based on documented risk descriptions.

**Operational activities.** Climate-related risks identified for operational activities include that **operational sites and travel generate Scope 1–2 emissions and face physical disruption**. This description reflects both transition-related considerations (emissions associated with operations and travel) and physical considerations (disruption risk).

**Financed activities / lending exposures.** Climate-related risks identified in relation to lending include that **lending to Other mining and quarrying carries elevated transition risk from carbon pricing and demand shifts**. This description reflects how transition drivers can affect counterparties and sectors relevant to lending activities.

### Risk management processes and controls (integration and avoidance of duplication)

This subsection explains how climate-related risk processes are positioned within overall risk management disclosures.

**Integrated approach to risk management disclosures.** Climate-related risk management processes are integrated into the organisation’s overall risk management process for many risks, as reflected in the climate risk register. Consistent with an integrated approach, climate-related risks are documented, assessed (including likelihood scoring and matrix-based rating), and monitored (using defined monitoring frequencies) through the same core risk artefact—the climate risk register—supporting coherent risk management disclosure without unnecessary duplication.
