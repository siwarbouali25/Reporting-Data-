## Risk identification and assessment

This section describes the processes used to identify, assess, prioritise and monitor climate-related risks, including the key inputs used, how scenario analysis is incorporated, how risks are rated, how monitoring is performed, and how climate-related risk management is integrated into overall enterprise risk management.

### Identification of climate-related risks and value chain considerations

Climate-related risks are captured and maintained in a climate risk register. The register includes risk descriptions that articulate how climate drivers can translate into business-relevant effects. For example, one recorded risk describes how **chronic heat and shifting precipitation can reduce yields for agricultural counterparties, weakening repayment capacity over the medium term**. This illustrates how the risk identification process considers climate-related physical conditions and their potential transmission into counterparty performance.

Climate-related risks are also considered across parts of the value chain. Documented value chain risk descriptions include:

- **Operational sites and travel generate Scope 1–2 emissions and face physical disruption.**
- **Lending to Other mining and quarrying carries elevated transition risk from carbon pricing and demand shifts.**

These value chain descriptions support the identification of both physical disruption themes (for operational activities) and transition risk themes (for lending exposures to emissions-intensive activities).

### Inputs and data sources used in assessment

The risk management process uses defined inputs and data sources to support assessment of climate-related risks. For physical risk exposure analysis, the data source used includes **ECB climate indicators (ECB_climate_indicators)**. This input supports the assessment of physical risk exposures and provides an external indicator set used within the risk process.

### Use of climate-related scenario analysis

Scenario analysis is used to inform the identification and assessment of climate-related risks, and scenario references are recorded in the climate risk register. Scenario links captured in the register include **SCN0005**, **SCN0007**, **SCN0009**, and **SCN0011**. By linking individual risks to specific scenario references, the process connects risk identification and assessment to scenario-based considerations.

### Risk assessment methodology and risk rating approach

Climate-related risks are assessed using a scoring approach that includes a likelihood score. Likelihood scores recorded in the climate risk register include **3**, **4**, and **5**, demonstrating the use of a structured likelihood scale in the assessment process.

Risk ratings in the climate risk register are derived from a **5x5 risk matrix** using the following methodology:

- **likelihood_score * severity_score**
- Scores are mapped to ratings as follows: **1–2 = low**, **3–6 = medium**, **8–12 = high**, **15–25 = critical**.
- Thresholds are applied as: **critical >= 15**, **high >= 8**, **medium >= 3**, **low < 3**.

Consistent with this approach, multiple risks in the register are rated **medium**.

### Prioritisation and integration with enterprise risk management

Climate-related risk management is integrated into the entity’s overall enterprise risk management process for many climate-related risks, as reflected by integration indicators recorded in the climate risk register. This integration supports the prioritisation and management of climate-related risks alongside other risk types within the broader risk management framework.

### Monitoring cadence and ongoing review

Climate-related risks are monitored at defined frequencies recorded in the climate risk register. Monitoring cadences include:

- **Monthly** monitoring for some risks.
- **Quarterly** monitoring for some risks.
- **Semi-annual** monitoring for some risks.

This defined cadence supports ongoing oversight of climate-related risks and enables periodic review aligned to the monitoring frequency assigned to each risk.

### Change tracking versus the prior reporting period

The climate risk register includes indicators showing whether individual climate-related risks have changed since the prior reporting period. The register reflects that some risks are flagged as having changed since the prior period, while others are not flagged as changed. This demonstrates that changes in climate-related risks are tracked over time within the risk management process.

## Risk management processes and controls

The climate risk register functions as a central artefact supporting the risk management lifecycle, linking identified risks to assessment inputs (including external indicators for physical risk exposures), scenario analysis references, scoring and rating outcomes, monitoring frequencies, and change tracking versus the prior period. For many risks, the register also reflects integration into enterprise risk management, supporting an integrated approach to managing climate-related risks within overall risk management processes.
