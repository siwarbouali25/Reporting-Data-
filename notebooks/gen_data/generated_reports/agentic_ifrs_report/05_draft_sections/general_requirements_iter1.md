## General Requirements

### Basis of preparation, reporting period, currency and comparatives

This section explains how the sustainability-related financial disclosures have been prepared so that users can understand the reporting basis, the period covered, the units of measure used, and how comparative information is presented.

The disclosures are prepared for the reporting year **2024**, with a financial year end of **31 December 2024**, and are presented in **EUR** when currency is used as the unit of measure. Comparative information is presented for **2022** and **2023** alongside the current reporting year where amounts are disclosed across periods. For example, climate-related capital expenditure is presented on a consistent basis across the three-year period: **EUR 322.2 million (2022)**, **EUR 373.91 million (2023)** and **EUR 476.95 million (2024)**. Tier 1 capital is also presented across the same comparative years: **EUR 95,149.4 million (2022)**, **EUR 143,979.2 million (2023)** and **EUR 151,208.6 million (2024)**.

Where climate-related metrics are expressed in non-currency units, the disclosures use consistent units of measure to support comparability and understandability. For 2024, operational greenhouse gas emissions are disclosed as **Scope 1: 2,634.908317 tCO₂e**, **Scope 2 (location-based): 6,963.4977 tCO₂e**, **Scope 2 (market-based): 3,060.5382 tCO₂e**, and **Scope 3 (business travel): 289.8833 tCO₂e**. Financed emissions are disclosed for 2024 as **35,973,167.6878965 tCO₂e**, and a portfolio carbon intensity metric is disclosed for 2024 as **1,154.8131 tCO₂e per EUR million**.

To support consistent preparation of market-based Scope 2 emissions, the bank’s approach is that **Scope 2 market-based emissions are sourced directly from utility invoices** and already reflect market-based accounting with grid emission factors adjusted for renewable energy certificates and power purchase agreements. Renewable energy certificate registry information is used for disclosure narrative only, and is not subtracted again during preparation.

### Material sustainability-related information and why it matters to prospects

The objective of these disclosures is to present material sustainability-related risks and opportunities that could reasonably be expected to affect the bank’s prospects, including effects on cash flows, access to finance and cost of capital over the short, medium and long term.

The bank identifies climate-related risks in its climate risk register and describes how those risks could affect the banking book and, therefore, the bank’s prospects. A key example is **“Physical risk — mortgage book flood exposure”**, categorised as **acute physical risk** and rated **critical**. The risk description explains the transmission mechanism to financial outcomes: **residential and commercial mortgage collateral located in flood-prone zones faces rising acute physical risk; property damage and devaluation could impair collateral coverage**. This framing links climate hazards to credit risk drivers (collateral value and coverage), which can affect expected losses and capital consumption.

Other climate-related risks described in the risk register similarly connect climate drivers to counterparty performance and portfolio quality. These include:

- **Chronic physical risk affecting agricultural counterparties**: chronic heat and shifting precipitation are described as reducing yields and **weakening repayment capacity over the medium term**.
- **Transition risk from rapid electrification**: rapid electrification is described as potentially **stranding internal-combustion vehicle manufacturers and suppliers in the lending book**.
- **Reputational and stakeholder risk**: stakeholder and NGO scrutiny of financed emissions and fossil financing is described as potentially **damaging brand and triggering client attrition**.
- **Regulatory transition risk affecting mortgage collateral**: minimum energy performance standards are described as potentially requiring **costly retrofits for low-EPC mortgage collateral**, reducing values and increasing loan-to-value.

In addition to risks, the disclosures include indicators of climate-related opportunities and strategic positioning that are relevant to prospects. For example, the bank discloses **green loans as 10.71% (2024)**, and scenario analysis narratives refer to **green loan growth** and **client engagement on transition plans** as mechanisms expected to reduce high-carbon sector concentration over the medium term.

### Connected information across governance, strategy, risk management, and metrics

Connected information enables users to understand how governance oversight, strategic responses, risk management processes, and performance metrics relate to the same underlying sustainability-related risks and opportunities.

**Governance connectivity.** The bank discloses governance structures and oversight practices relevant to climate-related matters. The board has a size of **10**, with **33.5%** board climate expertise disclosed, and climate topics are reflected in **69.8%** of board agenda coverage. Climate risk reporting to the board occurs **semi-annually**. An ESG committee exists and meets **5 times per year**. Management-level governance structures include a **Group Sustainability Committee**, an **ESG Executive Committee**, and a **Climate Risk Management Committee**.

These governance disclosures connect to the risk management and strategy content by indicating that climate-related risks (such as flood exposure in the mortgage book and transition risks in specific sectors) are subject to structured oversight and periodic reporting.

**Risk management connectivity.** The climate risk register provides a structured view of climate-related risks, including risk descriptions, ratings, and monitoring frequencies. For the critical acute physical risk relating to mortgage flood exposure, monitoring is **monthly**, and the risk is assessed using a defined scoring approach (see “Measurement approaches, assumptions, judgement and data-quality characteristics” below). Other risks in the register are monitored at different frequencies, including **quarterly** and **semi-annual**, reflecting differentiated monitoring aligned to the nature of the risk.

The disclosures also include indicators of physical risk exposure classification, with multiple exposures flagged as **high risk** (high-risk flags are **True** for five of the eight listed physical risk exposure entries). This supports the narrative that acute physical risk is relevant to the bank’s risk profile and is monitored.

**Strategy and resilience connectivity.** Climate scenario analysis provides forward-looking information about portfolio resilience under different transition pathways. The bank applies **NGFS v4 Net Zero 2050** using a **top-down sector-pathway approach**, mapping **carbon price assumptions from the IEA NZE 2050 trajectory** to counterparties by **NACE sector**, and deriving stranded asset estimates from **sector-level fossil fuel capex exposure**. The analysis scope covers **all lending book exposures across DE, FR, IT, NL and ES jurisdictions**.

Under **orderly transition scenarios**, the bank assesses portfolio resilience by comparing modelled transition-risk impacts with its internal capital planning and risk appetite metrics (including Pillar 2-related buffers). The disclosures describe the direction of effects and the governance and monitoring processes used to manage those impacts.

Under **disorderly transition scenarios**, the bank’s scenario analysis indicates higher transition-risk impacts than under orderly pathways, driven primarily by stranded-asset and impairment channels in more carbon-intensive sectors. The disclosures describe the key drivers and the portfolio management actions used to monitor and manage these risks over time.

**Metrics and targets connectivity.** The disclosures include metrics that relate to both operational emissions and financed emissions, supporting users’ understanding of performance and exposure. For operational emissions, Scope 1 includes the fleet (**scope 1 fleet included: True**), and Scope 2 is presented on both location-based and market-based bases. For financed emissions, the bank discloses **35,973,167.6878965 tCO₂e (2024)** and provides a portfolio carbon intensity metric of **1,154.8131 tCO₂e per EUR million (2024)**.

Targets are disclosed with progress measures that connect performance metrics to management intent. For example, target **TGT001** is an **absolute reduction** target for **Scope 1 and 2**, with a **2020 baseline year**, measured in **tCO₂e**, and progress in 2024 of **40.0%** (progress metric: **tCO₂e absolute**). Additional target progress measures are disclosed for intensity (**tCO₂e per EUR million**, progress **25.0%**) and percentage reduction versus baseline (progress **13.3%**).

### Measurement approaches, assumptions, judgement and data-quality characteristics

This subsection describes the key measurement approaches and assumptions used to produce climate-related metrics and forward-looking analyses, and provides information that helps users assess the quality and uncertainty characteristics of the disclosed information.

**Risk rating methodology.** Climate risk ratings in the climate risk register are derived from a **5×5 risk matrix**. The rating is calculated as **likelihood score × severity score**, with thresholds defined as: **scores 1–2 = low**, **3–6 = medium**, **8–12 = high**, and **15–25 = critical** (critical ≥ 15; high ≥ 8; medium ≥ 3; low < 3). For the critical acute physical risk “Physical risk — mortgage book flood exposure”, the disclosed scores are **likelihood 4** and **severity 5**, resulting in a product of **20**, consistent with a **critical** rating.

**Scenario analysis methodology and assumptions.** The bank’s scenario analysis uses NGFS scenarios and specifies key methodological choices and inputs. For the NGFS v4 Net Zero 2050 scenario, the bank applies a **top-down sector-pathway approach**, maps **IEA NZE 2050 carbon price assumptions** to counterparties by **NACE sector**, and derives stranded asset estimates from **sector-level fossil fuel capex exposure**. For the NGFS v4 Below 2°C (orderly) scenario, the bank uses NGFS integrated assessment model outputs aligned to a **1.8°C median temperature pathway**, with a carbon price trajectory from the **REMIND-MAgPIE model**, and assesses financial impact through **loan-level probability of default sensitivity** to carbon cost pass-through by sector carbon intensity tier.

**Data-quality characteristics for emissions.** The bank provides a summary of financed emissions data quality characteristics, expressed as a distribution across data sources and estimation approaches: **audited report (0.7%)**, **CDP disclosure (35.5%)**, **estimated economic (47.5%)**, and **proxy model (16.3%)**. This information supports users’ assessment of verifiability and measurement uncertainty, particularly for value chain-dependent metrics such as financed emissions.

**Methodological note for sovereign bonds.** For financed emissions calculations, the bank notes that **issuer EVIC (EUR million) and issuer revenue (EUR million) are null for sovereign bonds by design**, because EVIC and revenue are corporate concepts not applicable to sovereigns. This clarifies a boundary condition in the application of financed emissions methodologies.

### Comparative consistency and change monitoring

Comparability over time is supported by consistent presentation of key metrics and by monitoring changes in risk assessments.

The bank presents multi-year comparatives for selected climate-related financial and performance indicators, including climate-related capital expenditure and Tier 1 capital for **2022–2024**. This supports trend analysis and helps users relate sustainability-related actions and exposures to financial capacity.

Change monitoring is also reflected in the climate risk register through explicit flags indicating whether a risk assessment has **changed since the prior period**, alongside defined monitoring frequencies. For example, the critical acute physical risk relating to mortgage flood exposure is flagged as **not changed since the prior period (False)** and is monitored **monthly**. In contrast, the stakeholder/NGO scrutiny risk description is flagged as **changed since the prior period (True)** and is monitored **quarterly**. These disclosures provide users with insight into how the bank tracks the evolution of climate-related risks and updates assessments over time.

Finally, the bank’s resilience narratives under disorderly transition scenarios explicitly reference **active portfolio decarbonisation glide-path monitoring** as a resilience mechanism. This links the forward-looking assessment of transition-risk drivers (including stranded-asset and impairment channels) to ongoing monitoring practices that are intended to inform risk management and strategic responses over time.
