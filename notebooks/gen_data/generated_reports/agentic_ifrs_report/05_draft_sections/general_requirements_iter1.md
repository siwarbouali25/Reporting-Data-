## 1. General Requirements

### 1.1 Purpose and basis of preparation
These sustainability-related financial disclosures are prepared for the reporting year **2024**. The disclosures are prepared to provide information about sustainability-related risks and opportunities that could reasonably be expected to affect the bank’s prospects.

The disclosures are prepared to support decision-useful information and are structured to enable users to understand:

- the sustainability-related risks and opportunities described in the disclosures; and
- connections between governance, strategy, risk management, and metrics and targets.

### 1.2 Reporting period, fiscal year-end and comparative information
The reporting year is **2024** and the fiscal year-end is **31 December 2024**.

Comparative information is presented for **2022** and **2023**.

### 1.3 Reporting currency
The reporting currency is **EUR**.

Where currency is used as a unit of measure in these sustainability-related financial disclosures (for example, climate-related capital expenditure and Tier 1 capital), amounts are presented in euros.

### 1.4 Connected information
The disclosures are prepared to provide connected information that helps users understand how climate-related risks and opportunities are identified, assessed, monitored and managed, and how these matters relate to performance.

**Connections between risks and opportunities.** The climate risk register includes risks spanning physical and transition drivers, including:

- **Acute physical risk (mortgage collateral flood exposure):** residential and commercial mortgage collateral located in flood-prone zones faces rising acute physical risk; property damage and devaluation could impair collateral coverage.
- **Chronic physical risk (agricultural counterparties):** chronic heat and shifting precipitation reduce yields for agricultural counterparties, weakening repayment capacity over the medium term.
- **Transition risk (electrification):** rapid electrification could strand internal-combustion vehicle manufacturers and suppliers in the lending book.
- **Reputational risk (stakeholder scrutiny):** stakeholder and NGO scrutiny of financed emissions and fossil financing could damage brand and trigger client attrition.
- **Policy and standards risk (energy performance):** minimum energy performance standards may require costly retrofits for low-EPC mortgage collateral, reducing values and increasing loan-to-value.

These risks are connected to the bank’s financed emissions profile and to the way the bank assesses transition pathways through scenario analysis. For 2024, the bank discloses financed emissions of **35,973,167.6878965 tCO₂e**.

**Connections across governance, risk management and strategy.** Governance oversight and risk monitoring are connected through defined reporting and monitoring frequencies.

- Climate risk reporting to the board occurs **semi-annually**.
- The board has a size of **10**, with **33.5%** board climate expertise, and climate topics represent **69.8%** of the board agenda.
- An ESG committee exists and meets **5** times per year.
- Management-level governance structures referenced in the disclosures include the **Group Sustainability Committee**, **ESG Executive Committee**, and **Climate Risk Management Committee**.

Risk monitoring is operationalised through the climate risk register, which includes monitoring frequencies such as **monthly**, **quarterly**, and **semi-annual**. For example, the “Physical risk — mortgage book flood exposure” risk is monitored **monthly**.

Strategy and resilience are connected through climate scenario analysis. Scenario methodology notes describe the use of **NGFS v4** scenarios applied to the banking book, including:

- **NGFS v4 Net Zero 2050:** applied using a top-down sector-pathway approach; carbon price assumptions from the IEA NZE 2050 trajectory are mapped to counterparty NACE sectors; stranded asset estimates are derived from sector-level fossil fuel capex exposure; scope covers all lending book exposures across **DE, FR, IT, NL and ES** jurisdictions.
- **NGFS v4 Below 2°C (orderly):** applied using NGFS integrated assessment model outputs aligned to a **1.8°C** median temperature pathway; carbon price trajectory from the REMIND-MAgPIE model; financial impact assessed through loan-level probability of default sensitivity to carbon cost pass-through by sector carbon intensity tier.

Resilience assessments are presented for orderly and disorderly transition scenarios. Under orderly transition scenarios, the portfolio is described as demonstrating adequate resilience, and transition risk losses are described as remaining within Pillar 2 capital buffer thresholds. Under disorderly transition scenarios, near-term resilience is described as acceptable, while medium-term capital consumption from stranded asset impairments is described as material. Sector exposure limits and active portfolio decarbonisation glide-path monitoring are described as primary resilience mechanisms, and additional capital buffers may be required post-2030 under the most adverse disorderly assumptions.

### 1.5 Methodologies and measurement approaches
The disclosures use defined methodologies for key climate-related measures and risk assessments.

**Risk rating methodology (climate risk register).** Climate risk ratings are derived from a **5×5 risk matrix** based on the product of a likelihood score and a severity score. The resulting score is mapped to rating bands as follows:

- **Low:** scores **1–2**
- **Medium:** scores **3–6**
- **High:** scores **8–12**
- **Critical:** scores **15–25**

This methodology is applied to individual risks in the climate risk register. For example, “Physical risk — mortgage book flood exposure” has a likelihood score of **4** and a severity score of **5**, resulting in a **critical** risk rating.

**Scenario analysis methodology.** Scenario analysis is performed using NGFS v4 scenarios and specified modelling approaches.

- For the Net Zero 2050 scenario, the bank applies a top-down sector-pathway approach, mapping carbon price assumptions from the IEA NZE 2050 trajectory to counterparty NACE sectors and deriving stranded asset estimates from sector-level fossil fuel capex exposure.
- For the Below 2°C (orderly) scenario, the bank uses NGFS integrated assessment model outputs aligned to a 1.8°C median temperature pathway, with carbon prices from the REMIND-MAgPIE model, and assesses financial impact through loan-level probability of default sensitivity to carbon cost pass-through by sector carbon intensity tier.

**Scope 2 market-based accounting and renewable instruments.** Scope 2 market-based emissions are sourced directly from utility invoice data that already reflects market-based accounting with grid emission factors adjusted for renewable energy certificates and power purchase agreements. Renewable certificate registry data is used for disclosure narrative (certificate type, volume and registry) and is not subtracted again during preparation.

**Financed emissions methodology note (sovereign bonds).** For sovereign bonds, issuer EVIC and issuer revenue are treated as not applicable because EVIC and revenue are corporate concepts and are not used for sovereigns.

### 1.6 Estimates, assumptions and judgement
Sustainability-related financial disclosures involve estimates and judgement, particularly where forward-looking analysis is required.

- **Scenario analysis** incorporates assumptions about carbon price trajectories (including those from the IEA NZE 2050 trajectory and the REMIND-MAgPIE model) and uses sector mapping to counterparty NACE sectors.
- **Risk assessment** involves judgement in assigning likelihood and severity scores within the 5×5 matrix.

These estimates and judgements are connected to governance and risk management through defined monitoring and reporting cadences (for example, monthly monitoring for selected risks and semi-annual reporting of climate risk to the board).

### 1.7 Data quality characteristics and assurance
The disclosures include information intended to support decision-usefulness, including transparency about emissions data quality characteristics.

For emissions-related information, the bank summarises the composition of emissions data quality inputs as follows:

- **Estimated (economic): 47.5%**
- **CDP disclosure: 35.5%**
- **Proxy model: 16.3%**
- **Audited report: 0.7%**

External assurance over sustainability information is described as **limited**.

### 1.8 Consistency, comparability and change monitoring
Comparability is supported through the presentation of comparative years **2022** and **2023**, and through consistent measurement approaches for key metrics.

Change monitoring is embedded in the climate risk register through an indicator of whether a risk has changed since the prior period, alongside defined monitoring frequencies. For example, the “Physical risk — mortgage book flood exposure” entry indicates it has not changed since the prior period and is monitored monthly, while the stakeholder scrutiny risk indicates it has changed since the prior period and is monitored quarterly.

Where metrics are presented for multiple years, they are intended to support users’ understanding of trends relevant to prospects. For example, climate-related capital expenditure is presented for 2022–2024 (**€322.2 million**, **€373.91 million**, and **€476.95 million**, respectively), and Tier 1 capital is presented for 2022–2024 (**€95,149.4 million**, **€143,979.2 million**, and **€151,208.6 million**, respectively).

### 1.9 Value chain boundary considerations
The bank’s value chain mapping identifies material nodes within its own operations, including **“Corporate fleet & business travel”**. This is connected to operational emissions disclosures, including Scope 1 emissions (with fleet included) and Scope 3 travel emissions for 2024 (**289.8833 tCO₂e**).

For 2024, the bank discloses:

- Scope 1 emissions of **2,634.908317 tCO₂e** (fleet included);
- Scope 2 emissions on a location-based basis of **6,963.4977 tCO₂e**; and
- Scope 2 emissions on a market-based basis of **3,060.5382 tCO₂e**.
