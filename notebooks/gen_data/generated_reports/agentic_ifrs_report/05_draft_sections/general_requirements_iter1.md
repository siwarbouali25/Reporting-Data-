## General Requirements

### Basis of preparation, reporting period, currency and comparatives

This section explains how the sustainability-related financial disclosures have been prepared so that users can interpret the information on a consistent basis over time and in connection with the entity’s general purpose financial reporting.

The sustainability-related financial disclosures are prepared for the reporting year **2024**, with a fiscal year end of **31 December 2024**. Amounts presented in currency are reported in **euros (EUR)**. Where comparative information is presented, the comparative years are **2022** and **2023**, enabling users to assess direction of travel and year-on-year movements in selected metrics and financial measures.

Comparatives are used to support trend analysis for key measures that are relevant to the entity’s prospects. For example, climate-related capital expenditure (climate capex) is presented for **2022 (EUR 322.2 million)**, **2023 (EUR 373.91 million)** and **2024 (EUR 476.95 million)**. Similarly, Tier 1 capital is presented for **2022 (EUR 95,149.4 million)**, **2023 (EUR 143,979.2 million)** and **2024 (EUR 151,208.6 million)**. These measures provide context for understanding the scale of climate-related investment activity and the capital position against which climate-related risks are assessed.

The disclosures also include climate-related performance measures for the reporting year 2024, including **financed emissions (35,973,167.6878965 tCO₂e)** and **carbon intensity (1,154.8131 tCO₂e per EUR million)**. These metrics are presented alongside governance, risk and scenario analysis information to support a connected understanding of how climate-related risks and opportunities could affect the entity’s prospects.

### Material sustainability-related information and why it matters to prospects

The objective of these disclosures is to present material sustainability-related risks and opportunities that could reasonably be expected to affect the entity’s prospects, including effects on cash flows, access to finance and cost of capital over the short, medium and long term.

The entity’s climate-related risk register identifies risks that are linked to core banking activities and credit risk transmission channels. A key example is **“Physical risk — mortgage book flood exposure”**, categorised as **acute physical risk** and rated **critical**. The risk description explains that **residential and commercial mortgage collateral located in flood-prone zones faces rising acute physical risk**, and that **property damage and devaluation could impair collateral coverage**. This matters to prospects because collateral impairment can affect loss given default, credit outcomes and the resilience of the mortgage portfolio under acute physical events.

The risk register also describes transition and stakeholder-related risks that can affect portfolio performance and franchise value. For example:

- **Rapid electrification** is described as a transition dynamic that **could strand internal-combustion vehicle manufacturers and suppliers in the lending book**, linking climate transition pathways to counterparty viability and credit risk.
- **Minimum energy performance standards** are described as potentially requiring **costly retrofits for low energy performance certificate (EPC) mortgage collateral**, which could **reduce values and increase loan-to-value (LTV)**. This connects regulatory transition risk to collateral valuation and credit risk metrics.
- **Stakeholder and NGO scrutiny of financed emissions and fossil financing** is described as a risk that **could damage brand and trigger client attrition**, linking climate-related expectations to reputational outcomes and business volumes.
- **Chronic heat and shifting precipitation** are described as reducing yields for **agricultural counterparties**, **weakening repayment capacity over the medium term**, linking chronic physical risk to counterparty cash flows and credit performance.

These risk descriptions are complemented by portfolio-level climate metrics that help users understand scale and exposure. In 2024, the entity reports **financed emissions of 35,973,167.6878965 tCO₂e** and a **carbon intensity of 1,154.8131 tCO₂e per EUR million**. The disclosures also include an indicator of portfolio positioning through **green loans of 10.71% (2024)**. Together, these measures support an understanding of how climate-related risks and opportunities may influence portfolio composition, risk profile and longer-term prospects.

### Connected information across governance, strategy, risk management, and metrics

A coherent set of sustainability-related financial disclosures enables users to understand how governance oversight, strategic assessment, risk management processes and performance metrics relate to one another.

**Governance connectivity.** Governance arrangements described in these disclosures indicate structured oversight of climate-related matters. The board has a size of **10**, with **33.5%** board climate expertise reported, and climate topics appearing on the board agenda **69.8%** of the time. The board meets **4** times per year, and climate risk reporting is provided to the board on a **semi-annual** basis. An **ESG committee is in place** and meets **5** times per year. Management-level governance structures referenced include the **Group Sustainability Committee**, the **ESG Executive Committee**, and the **Climate Risk Management Committee**. These governance features provide the organisational context for how climate-related risks and opportunities are monitored and escalated.

**Risk management connectivity.** The climate risk register includes defined monitoring frequencies, supporting a link between risk identification and ongoing oversight. For example, the critical acute physical risk relating to mortgage flood exposure is monitored **monthly**. Other risks in the register are monitored **monthly**, **quarterly**, or **semi-annually**, reflecting differentiated monitoring aligned to the nature of the risk. The register also tracks whether a risk has **changed since the prior period**; for example, some risks are indicated as having changed since the prior period, while others are indicated as unchanged. This supports change monitoring and helps users understand how the risk profile is evolving.

The risk rating approach is defined using a **5×5 risk matrix** based on **likelihood score × severity score**, with thresholds that classify risks as low, medium, high or critical. For the mortgage flood exposure risk, the likelihood score is **4** and the severity score is **5**, consistent with a critical rating under the stated methodology.

**Strategy and resilience connectivity.** Scenario analysis disclosures provide forward-looking context for how climate-related risks could affect resilience and capital. The entity applies **NGFS v4 Net Zero 2050** using a **top-down sector-pathway approach** to the banking book, mapping **carbon price assumptions from the IEA NZE 2050 trajectory** to counterparty **NACE sectors**, and deriving stranded asset estimates from **sector-level fossil fuel capex exposure**. The analysis scope covers **all lending book exposures across DE, FR, IT, NL and ES jurisdictions**.

The scenario analysis includes both orderly and disorderly transition pathways. The disclosures describe how scenario outputs are used to inform portfolio steering (for example, through sector exposure limits and decarbonisation glide-path monitoring) and to support consideration of capital planning. Where references are made to capital buffers and resilience, these are intended to describe the direction of impacts and management response rather than to provide quantified capital adequacy outcomes in this section.

**Metrics connectivity.** Operational emissions metrics are presented for 2024, including **Scope 1 emissions of 2,634.908317 tCO₂e** and **Scope 2 emissions of 6,963.4977 tCO₂e (location-based)** and **3,060.5382 tCO₂e (market-based)**. The Scope 1 boundary includes the corporate fleet (fleet emissions are included). Scope 3 business travel emissions are reported as **289.8833 tCO₂e (2024)**. These metrics provide context for the entity’s own operations, while financed emissions and carbon intensity provide portfolio-level indicators relevant to climate-related risk and opportunity.

Targets are also included to support connected performance assessment. For example, target **TGT001** is an **absolute reduction** target for **Scope 1 and Scope 2**, with a **baseline year of 2020** and progress measured as **absolute tCO₂e**; progress in 2024 is **40.0%**. Additional target progress measures are presented using **tCO₂e per EUR million** (progress **25.0%**) and **percentage reduction versus baseline** (progress **13.3%**). These target disclosures connect operational emissions measurement to performance management.

### Measurement approaches, assumptions, judgement and data-quality characteristics

Measurement approaches and key assumptions influence how users interpret sustainability-related metrics and forward-looking analyses, particularly where estimates and modelling are used.

**Scenario analysis methods and assumptions.** The scenario analysis uses established climate scenarios and model outputs. For NGFS v4 Net Zero 2050, the approach is described as top-down sector pathways applied to the banking book, with carbon price assumptions mapped to NACE sectors and stranded asset estimates derived from sector-level fossil fuel capex exposure. For NGFS v4 Below 2°C (orderly), the scenario is aligned to a **1.8°C median temperature pathway**, with a carbon price trajectory from the **REMIND-MAgPIE model**. Financial impact is assessed through **loan-level probability of default sensitivity to carbon cost pass-through**, segmented by sector carbon intensity tier. These methodological descriptions are intended to make the forward-looking information supportable and understandable.

**Risk rating methodology.** The climate risk register’s risk rating is derived from a defined calculation: **likelihood score × severity score**, interpreted through a 5×5 matrix with explicit thresholds (low, medium, high, critical). This provides transparency on how qualitative risk descriptions are translated into a rating used for monitoring and escalation.

**Emissions measurement and market-based Scope 2.** Scope 2 emissions are disclosed on both a location-based and a market-based basis. The market-based Scope 2 figure reflects the entity’s contractual instruments for electricity supply (for example, renewable energy certificates and power purchase agreements) and the associated emission factors applied in the calculation.

**Data-quality characteristics and proxy basis.** The financed emissions disclosures include a data-quality summary that characterises the basis of underlying inputs. The distribution is described as **0.7% audited report**, **35.5% CDP disclosure**, **47.5% estimated using economic data**, and **16.3% proxy model**. Where proxy modelling is used, the estimate is prepared on a proxy basis as part of the overall financed emissions measurement approach.

### Comparative consistency and change monitoring

Comparability over time supports users’ ability to evaluate performance, risk profile and progress against targets, while change monitoring supports timely identification of shifts in risk exposure.

The disclosures provide multi-year information for selected measures, including climate capex and Tier 1 capital across **2022–2024**, enabling users to observe changes in investment levels and capital position. For climate-related risk monitoring, the risk register includes a **“changed since prior period”** indicator and defined monitoring frequencies (monthly, quarterly, semi-annual). This structure supports consistent tracking of risk status and highlights where the risk profile has evolved.

Comparative consistency is also supported through the use of defined units of measure and calculation approaches. For example, carbon intensity is expressed as **tCO₂e per EUR million**, and risk ratings are derived from a consistent likelihood–severity scoring approach. Together, these features are intended to support comparability, verifiability and understandability of the sustainability-related financial disclosures as a coherent whole.
