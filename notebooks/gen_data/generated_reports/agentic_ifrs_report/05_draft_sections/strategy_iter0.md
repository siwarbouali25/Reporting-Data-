## Strategy

This section explains how climate-related risks and opportunities are expected to affect the bank’s business model, strategy and financial planning across the **short term**, **medium term** and **long term**, and how the bank assesses the resilience of its strategy under climate-related scenario analysis.

### Climate-related risks and opportunities and their expected effects

The bank’s climate-related risk profile is driven primarily by exposures in the banking book and the sensitivity of collateral and counterparties to both **physical** and **transition** risk transmission channels.

#### Principal climate-related risks

The bank identifies the following climate-related risks as relevant to its strategy:

- **Physical risk — mortgage book flood exposure**: Residential and commercial mortgage collateral located in flood-prone zones faces rising acute physical risk. Property damage and devaluation could impair collateral coverage.
- **Physical risk — wildfire exposure in southern portfolio** (risk rating: **medium**): Wildfire risk is assessed as a physical risk driver for parts of the portfolio.
- **Transition risk — building energy performance regulation** (risk rating: **medium**): Minimum energy performance standards may require costly retrofits for low-EPC mortgage collateral, reducing values and increasing loan-to-value.
- **Stranded assets — fossil fuel sector exposure**: Demand shifts away from fossil fuels risk stranding reserves and assets held by oil, gas and coal counterparties, reducing their enterprise value.
- **Carbon pricing and policy tightening**: Tightening EU ETS and national carbon pricing raise operating costs for carbon-intensive borrowers, increasing default probability in cement, steel and chemicals.
- **Technology transition risk in automotive value chains**: Rapid electrification could strand internal-combustion vehicle manufacturers and suppliers in the lending book.
- **Physical risk affecting agriculture (medium term)**: Chronic heat and shifting precipitation reduce yields for agricultural counterparties, weakening repayment capacity over the medium term.
- **Reputational and stakeholder risk**: Stakeholder and NGO scrutiny of financed emissions and fossil financing could damage brand and trigger client attrition.

These risks can affect the bank through credit risk (counterparty repayment capacity), collateral value dynamics (including mortgage collateral revaluation), and broader franchise impacts (including reputational effects and client behaviour).

#### Principal climate-related opportunities

The bank identifies climate-related opportunities that are aligned with client demand and the transition of the real economy:

- **Sustainable bond underwriting (short term)**: Growth in underwriting activity for sustainable bonds.
- **Green loan growth (medium term)**: Expansion of green lending.
- **Transition advisory**: Fee income from climate risk advisory, transition planning support, and ESG due diligence services offered to large corporate clients.
- **Renewable energy project finance**: Project finance for solar, wind, and battery storage infrastructure aligned with REPowerEU and national renewable energy targets.

These opportunities are expected to support revenue diversification and deepen client relationships as counterparties seek financing and advisory support for transition plans and low-carbon investment.

### Effects on the value chain

This subsection explains where climate-related risks and opportunities arise across the bank’s value chain, with emphasis on the banking model in which the most material climate-related effects typically occur through financing activities.

- **Own operations**: Operational sites and travel generate Scope 1–2 emissions and face physical disruption. The bank’s value chain mapping also identifies **Corporate fleet & business travel** as an own-operations node with the same climate risk description.
- **Financing counterparties**: Climate-related risks are concentrated in financed activities, including:
  - Lending to **Manufacture of coke and refined petroleum**, which carries elevated transition risk from carbon pricing and demand shifts.
  - Exposure to **Manufacture of motor vehicles**, which is subject to moderate physical and transition risk.

The bank’s strategy therefore focuses on managing climate-related risk in the banking book while pursuing opportunities that support clients’ transition and the financing of renewable energy infrastructure.

### Strategic response and integration into decision-making

The bank’s strategic response is oriented around (i) managing climate-related risk concentrations and (ii) capturing transition-related business opportunities.

#### Portfolio and client strategy

Across the banking book, the bank’s strategic response emphasises:

- **Sector and portfolio steering**: Scenario resilience assessments identify the bank’s **sector exposure limits** and **active portfolio decarbonisation glide-path monitoring** as primary mechanisms supporting resilience under disorderly transition scenarios.
- **Client engagement**: Under orderly transition scenarios, the bank expects **green loan growth** and **client engagement on transition plans** to progressively reduce high-carbon sector concentration over the medium term.
- **Collateral-focused risk management**: For physical and building-related transition risks, resilience mechanisms referenced in scenario analysis include a **flood-zone overlay** and **EPC-based collateral monitoring**, reflecting the importance of location and energy performance characteristics in mortgage collateral risk.

#### Emissions and intensity orientation

The bank discloses a target intensity of **20.0 tCO₂e per €m**. In addition, the bank reports **financed emissions of 35,973,167.6878965 tCO₂e (2024)**, reflecting the strategic relevance of financed emissions to the bank’s climate-related risk profile and stakeholder expectations.

Progress indicators reported for 2024 include:

- **Scope 1 and 2**: 40.0% progress
- **Scope 3 Category 15**: 25.0% progress
- **Net zero (all scopes)**: 13.3% progress

These indicators are used to track progress across operational and financed emissions dimensions.

### Trade-offs and strategic choices

The bank’s climate strategy involves balancing risk reduction and opportunity capture across different parts of the portfolio:

- **Transition risk management vs. client franchise**: Stakeholder and NGO scrutiny of financed emissions and fossil financing could damage brand and trigger client attrition, reinforcing the strategic importance of financed-emissions management and portfolio steering.
- **Collateral value protection vs. borrower affordability**: Building energy performance regulation may require costly retrofits for low-EPC mortgage collateral, which can reduce collateral values and increase loan-to-value; this creates a strategic need to monitor EPC-related risk while maintaining prudent credit decisions.
- **Orderly vs. disorderly transition pathways**: Scenario analysis indicates that disorderly transition pathways can increase capital consumption from stranded asset impairments in the medium term, while orderly pathways are assessed as more manageable within existing buffers.

### Climate-related scenario analysis and resilience

The bank uses climate-related scenario analysis to assess strategic resilience across transition and physical risk pathways.

#### Scenario set and scope

- **Scope of analysis**: The analysis covers **all banking book exposures across DE, FR, IT, NL and ES**.
- **Orderly transition scenarios**: The bank applies **NGFS v4 Net Zero 2050** using a top-down sector-pathway approach. Carbon price assumptions are mapped from the **IEA NZE 2050** trajectory to counterparty NACE sectors, and stranded asset estimates are derived from sector-level fossil fuel capex exposure.
- **Orderly below-2°C scenario**: The bank applies **NGFS v4 Below 2°C (orderly)** aligned to a 1.8°C median temperature pathway, using a carbon price trajectory from the REMIND-MAgPIE model. Financial impact is assessed through loan-level probability of default sensitivity to carbon cost pass-through by sector carbon intensity tier.
- **Disorderly transition scenario**: The bank uses **NGFS v4 Divergent Net Zero** to capture technology pathway uncertainty, with carbon price assumptions varying by country based on national ETS and carbon tax trajectories. This scenario is used primarily to stress-test technology transition risk in the automotive and energy manufacturing lending sub-portfolio.
- **Delayed policy action scenario**: The bank uses **NGFS v4 NDC**, representing delayed but eventually strengthened policy action. Carbon pricing ramp-up is assumed from 2030 onward based on Paris Agreement NDC submission trajectories. Revenue-at-risk is computed from sector revenue sensitivity to carbon price at scenario-specified EUR/tCO₂e levels.
- **Hot house world (current policies) scenario**: The bank uses **NGFS v4 Current Policies** as an adverse baseline. Physical risk parameters are sourced from NGFS chronic and acute hazard layers and downscaled to counterparty postcode and collateral location, using RCP 8.5 physical risk projections for a 2050 horizon analysis.

#### Resilience assessment outcomes

- **Orderly transition resilience**: Under orderly transition scenarios, the portfolio demonstrates **adequate resilience**. Transition risk losses remain within **Pillar 2 capital buffer thresholds**. Green loan growth and client engagement on transition plans are expected to progressively reduce high-carbon sector concentration over the medium term.
- **Disorderly transition resilience**: Under disorderly transition scenarios, near-term resilience is assessed as **acceptable**, but **medium-term capital consumption from stranded asset impairments is material**. Sector exposure limits and active portfolio decarbonisation glide-path monitoring are identified as the primary resilience mechanisms. Additional capital buffers may be required post-2030 under the most adverse disorderly assumptions.
- **Hot house world resilience**: Under hot house world scenarios, **physical risk losses** represent the dominant long-term resilience challenge. Collateral revaluation risk in flood-exposed mortgage books and agricultural borrower default risk from chronic heat stress are identified as primary transmission channels. The bank’s flood-zone overlay and EPC-based collateral monitoring are described as resilience mechanisms deployed for the 2050 horizon.

- **€9,214.0m** (medium-term horizon)

These indicators are used to inform strategic assessment of transition impacts across the banking book.
