## Strategy

This Strategy section explains how climate-related risks and opportunities affect the bank’s business model and value chain, how the bank is responding through its strategic priorities and portfolio actions, and how scenario analysis informs resilience and financial planning across short-term, medium-term and long-term horizons.

### Strategic context and boundary

The bank’s climate-related strategy is developed and assessed on a **financial control** boundary. This boundary frames how climate-related risks and opportunities are identified and managed across the bank’s activities, including the banking book and operational footprint.

Climate-related considerations are most significant where the bank’s value creation is driven by financing activities. In addition, the bank recognises that its own operations (including sites and travel) both generate Scope 1–2 emissions and can be exposed to physical disruption.

### Climate-related risks and opportunities and their time horizons

The bank’s climate-related risk profile reflects both **physical risks** (acute and chronic) and **transition risks** (policy, technology, market and reputational dynamics). These risks can affect credit quality, collateral values, client demand, and the bank’s capital consumption through impairments and risk-weighted assets.

#### Key climate-related risks

The following climate-related risks are identified as relevant to the bank’s strategy and resilience:

- **Physical risk — mortgage book flood exposure**: Residential and commercial mortgage collateral located in flood-prone zones faces rising acute physical risk. Property damage and devaluation could impair collateral coverage. This risk is linked to scenario analysis (SCN0009).

- **Physical risk — wildfire exposure in southern portfolio (medium risk rating)**: Wildfire exposure is identified as a physical risk affecting parts of the portfolio, with a **medium** risk rating and a scenario analysis linkage (SCN0007). The bank’s risk rating approach uses a 5x5 matrix where likelihood and severity scores are multiplied and mapped to rating bands.

- **Transition risk — building energy performance regulation (medium risk rating)**: Minimum energy performance standards may require costly retrofits for low-EPC mortgage collateral, reducing values and increasing loan-to-value ratios. This risk is rated **medium** and linked to scenario analysis (SCN0011).

- **Stranded assets — fossil fuel sector exposure**: Demand shifts away from fossil fuels risk stranding reserves and assets held by oil, gas and coal counterparties, reducing their enterprise value.

- **Carbon pricing and policy tightening affecting carbon-intensive sectors**: Tightening EU ETS and national carbon pricing raise operating costs for carbon-intensive borrowers, increasing default probability in cement, steel and chemicals. This risk is linked to scenario analysis (SCN0007).

- **Technology transition risk in automotive value chains**: Rapid electrification could strand internal-combustion vehicle manufacturers and suppliers in the lending book. This risk is also linked to scenario analysis (SCN0007).

- **Chronic physical risk affecting agriculture (medium-term)**: Chronic heat and shifting precipitation reduce yields for agricultural counterparties, weakening repayment capacity over the **medium term**. This risk is linked to scenario analysis (SCN0005).

- **Reputational and conduct-related transition risk**: Stakeholder and NGO scrutiny of financed emissions and fossil financing could damage brand and trigger client attrition. This risk is identified as having changed since the prior period.

These risks are strategically relevant because they can transmit into financial performance through (i) borrower cash-flow stress and higher probability of default, (ii) collateral impairment and revaluation risk (notably in flood-exposed mortgage books and low-EPC collateral), and (iii) franchise impacts through stakeholder scrutiny and client behaviour.

#### Key climate-related opportunities

The bank’s strategy also recognises climate-related opportunities that can support revenue growth and client relevance across time horizons:

- **Sustainable bond underwriting (short-term)** (OPP-BANK01-02): Opportunity to support capital markets activity through sustainable bond underwriting in the **short term**.

- **Green loan growth (medium-term)** (OPP-BANK01-01): Opportunity to expand green lending in the **medium term**, supporting portfolio repositioning and client transition needs.

- **Transition advisory** (OPP-BANK01-03): Fee income from climate risk advisory, transition planning support, and ESG due diligence services offered to large corporate clients.

- **Renewable energy project finance** (OPP-BANK01-04): Project finance for solar, wind, and battery storage infrastructure aligned with REPowerEU and national renewable energy targets.

These opportunities are strategically relevant because they can (i) diversify and grow fee and interest income, (ii) support client transition planning and risk mitigation, and (iii) contribute to reducing high-carbon sector concentration over the medium term through portfolio rebalancing.

### Effects on the business model and value chain

This subsection explains how climate-related risks and opportunities affect the bank’s business model and value chain, focusing on where climate-related impacts are most likely to influence enterprise value.

#### Financing activities as the primary transmission channel

The bank’s value chain mapping identifies multiple nodes classified as **financing counterparties**, reflecting that climate-related impacts are predominantly transmitted through the banking book. Within this financing value chain:

- Lending to **manufacture of coke and refined petroleum** is identified as carrying elevated transition risk from carbon pricing and demand shifts.
- Exposure to **manufacture of motor vehicles** is identified as subject to moderate physical and transition risk.

These exposures align with the risk register themes of stranded assets in fossil fuel sectors, carbon pricing impacts on carbon-intensive industries, and technology transition risk in automotive manufacturing and supply chains.

#### Collateral and location-based physical risk

Physical risk is particularly relevant where the bank relies on collateral values and where counterparties are exposed to climate hazards:

- Flood risk affecting residential and commercial mortgage collateral can impair collateral coverage through property damage and devaluation.
- Wildfire exposure in parts of the portfolio can disrupt assets and economic activity.
- Chronic heat and shifting precipitation can reduce agricultural yields, weakening repayment capacity over the medium term.

These channels can affect credit risk outcomes and collateral valuation dynamics, and therefore influence portfolio composition, underwriting standards, and monitoring priorities.

#### Own operations

The bank’s own operations—operational sites and travel—generate Scope 1–2 emissions and face physical disruption. While the bank’s enterprise value is primarily driven by financing activities, operational disruption can still affect service continuity and operating costs.

### Strategic response: how the bank is positioning and responding

This subsection describes how the bank’s strategy responds to climate-related risks and opportunities through portfolio actions, client engagement, and the scaling of climate-related products and services.

#### Portfolio repositioning and client engagement

Scenario analysis indicates that, under orderly transition scenarios, **green loan growth and client engagement on transition plans** are expected to progressively reduce high-carbon sector concentration over the **medium term**. This strategic direction links the bank’s opportunity set (green loan growth and transition advisory) with its risk mitigation approach (reducing concentration in high-carbon sectors and supporting clients’ transition planning).

In disorderly transition scenarios, the bank identifies **sector exposure limits** and **active portfolio decarbonisation glide-path monitoring** as primary resilience mechanisms. These mechanisms are intended to manage the pace and concentration of transition risk in the banking book, particularly where stranded asset impairments could become material.

#### Scaling climate-related products and services

The bank’s opportunity set supports a strategy that combines financing and advisory capabilities:

- **Renewable energy project finance** supports the build-out of solar, wind and battery storage infrastructure aligned with REPowerEU and national renewable energy targets.
- **Sustainable bond underwriting** supports clients’ access to capital markets for sustainable financing.
- **Transition advisory** supports large corporate clients through climate risk advisory, transition planning support, and ESG due diligence services.

Together, these activities are positioned to support client transition needs while also contributing to the bank’s revenue mix through fee income and financing growth.

#### Managing trade-offs in strategic execution

The bank’s strategy reflects trade-offs inherent in managing climate-related risks while pursuing climate-related opportunities:

- **Risk reduction vs. client support**: Reducing high-carbon sector concentration and applying sector exposure limits can mitigate transition risk, while client engagement on transition plans and advisory services can support clients’ transition pathways.
- **Near-term resilience vs. medium-term capital consumption**: Disorderly transition scenarios indicate acceptable near-term resilience but material medium-term capital consumption from stranded asset impairments, implying a strategic need to balance growth and portfolio repositioning with capital planning.
- **Transition focus vs. physical risk preparedness**: While transition risk is prominent in orderly and disorderly pathways, hot house world scenarios identify physical risk losses as the dominant long-term resilience challenge, requiring sustained attention to location-based risk overlays and collateral monitoring.

### Scenario analysis and resilience

This subsection summarises how climate scenario analysis informs the bank’s assessment of resilience and the key transmission channels identified.

#### Scope and methodologies applied

The bank applies climate scenario analysis to the banking book using NGFS v4 scenarios, with analysis scope covering **all banking book exposures across DE, FR, IT, NL and ES**.

Key methodological elements include:

- **Orderly transition (NGFS v4 Net Zero 2050)**: Applied using a top-down sector-pathway approach. Carbon price assumptions are mapped from the IEA NZE 2050 trajectory to counterparty NACE sectors. Stranded asset estimates are derived from sector-level fossil fuel capex exposure.

- **Orderly transition (NGFS v4 Below 2°C)**: Applied using NGFS integrated assessment model outputs aligned to a 1.8°C median temperature pathway, with a carbon price trajectory from the REMIND-MAgPIE model. Financial impact is assessed through loan-level probability of default sensitivity to carbon cost pass-through by sector carbon intensity tier.

- **Disorderly transition (NGFS v4 Divergent Net Zero)**: Used to capture technology pathway uncertainty, with divergent sectoral transition speeds across jurisdictions and carbon price assumptions varying by country based on national ETS and carbon tax trajectories. This scenario is used primarily to stress-test technology transition risk in the automotive and energy manufacturing lending sub-portfolio.

- **Delayed policy action (NGFS v4 NDC)**: Represents delayed but eventually strengthened policy action, with carbon pricing ramp-up assumed from 2030 onward based on Paris Agreement NDC submission trajectories. Revenue-at-risk is computed from sector revenue sensitivity to carbon price at EUR per tonne of CO2e levels specified in the scenario.

- **Hot house world / current policies (NGFS v4 Current Policies)**: Used as the adverse baseline for physical risk. Physical risk parameters are sourced from NGFS chronic and acute hazard layers and downscaled to counterparty postcode and collateral location, with RCP 8.5 physical risk projections used for a 2050 horizon analysis.

#### Resilience under orderly transition scenarios

Under orderly transition scenarios, the portfolio demonstrates **adequate resilience**. Transition risk losses remain within **Pillar 2 capital buffer thresholds**. The bank expects that green loan growth and client engagement on transition plans will progressively reduce high-carbon sector concentration over the medium term.

The bank quantified revenue-at-risk under the NGFS v4 Net Zero 2050 scenario application, including:

- **EUR 9,689.2 million** revenue-at-risk (scenario SCN0001).
- **EUR 9,214.0 million** revenue-at-risk (medium-term horizon scenario application).

These quantified outputs are used to inform strategic prioritisation of portfolio actions and client engagement in sectors most sensitive to carbon pricing and transition dynamics.

#### Resilience under disorderly transition scenarios

Under disorderly transition scenarios, near-term resilience is assessed as **acceptable**, but **medium-term capital consumption from stranded asset impairments is material**. The bank identifies its current **sector exposure limits** and **active portfolio decarbonisation glide-path monitoring** as the primary resilience mechanisms.

The bank also notes that **additional capital buffers may be required post-2030 under the most adverse disorderly assumptions**, indicating that resilience is sensitive to the pace and disorderliness of the transition and the resulting impairment dynamics.

#### Resilience under hot house world scenarios

Under hot house world scenarios, **physical risk losses represent the dominant long-term resilience challenge**. The bank identifies the primary transmission channels as:

- **Collateral revaluation risk in flood-exposed mortgage books**.
- **Agricultural borrower default risk from chronic heat stress**.

For the 2050 horizon, the bank identifies a **flood-zone overlay** and **EPC-based collateral monitoring** as resilience mechanisms deployed to manage these long-term physical risk channels.

### Financial planning, resource allocation and progress monitoring

This subsection explains how climate-related considerations are reflected in financial planning and how the bank monitors progress using selected indicators.

#### Climate-related expenditure and financial capacity indicators

The bank reports climate-related capital expenditure and operating expenditure alongside key financial indicators:

- **2022**: climate capex **EUR 322.2 million**; climate opex **EUR 420.32 million**; total revenue **EUR 25,466.1 million**; net profit **EUR 5,759.9 million**; total assets **EUR 763,922.8 million**; Tier 1 capital **EUR 95,149.4 million**.
- **2023**: climate capex **EUR 373.91 million**; climate opex **EUR 509.21 million**; total revenue **EUR 27,779.7 million**; total assets **EUR 820,167.2 million**; Tier 1 capital **EUR 143,979.2 million**.
- **2024**: climate capex **EUR 476.95 million**; total revenue **EUR 24,136.2 million**; total assets **EUR 850,000.0 million**; Tier 1 capital **EUR 151,208.6 million**.

These indicators provide context for the scale of climate-related investment and operating activity relative to the bank’s overall financial position and performance.

#### Portfolio emissions and intensity orientation

The bank reports **financed emissions of 35,973,167.6878965 tCO2e in 2024**, reflecting the significance of financed emissions as a strategic consideration for a bank.

The bank also discloses a target intensity of **20.0 tCO2e per EUR million**, which is used as an orientation point for portfolio decarbonisation and monitoring.

#### Progress monitoring against targets

The bank tracks progress against climate-related targets, including:

- **Scope 1 and 2**: **40.0%** progress in 2024.
- **Scope 3 Category 15**: **25.0%** progress in 2024.
- **Net zero (all scopes)**: **13.3%** progress in 2024.

These progress indicators support strategic oversight by linking operational and financed emissions performance to the bank’s broader climate ambition.

### Strategic outlook

Across short-term, medium-term and long-term horizons, the bank’s strategy integrates climate-related risks and opportunities through (i) portfolio and client actions that support green loan growth, transition advisory and sustainable finance activity, (ii) resilience mechanisms including sector exposure limits and portfolio decarbonisation glide-path monitoring, and (iii) physical risk overlays and collateral monitoring to address long-term hazard-driven impacts. Scenario analysis across NGFS v4 pathways provides a structured basis for assessing resilience and for informing capital and portfolio planning under both transition and physical risk outcomes.
