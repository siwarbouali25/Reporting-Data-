## General requirements

This section describes the basis on which the Bank’s sustainability-related financial disclosures have been prepared, including the reporting period, presentation currency, comparative information, connected information, and the key methodologies, estimates and judgements applied to climate-related disclosures.

### Basis of preparation and fair presentation

The Bank’s sustainability-related financial disclosures are prepared to present fairly sustainability-related risks and opportunities that could reasonably be expected to affect the Bank’s prospects. Fair presentation is supported through disclosures that are intended to be complete, neutral and accurate, and that are prepared in a manner designed to be comparable, verifiable, timely and understandable.

The disclosures are structured to enable users to understand:

- connections between climate-related risks and opportunities that could reasonably be expected to affect the Bank’s prospects; and
- connections within the sustainability-related financial disclosures across governance, strategy, risk management, and metrics and targets.

Where quantitative measures are presented, the Bank uses consistent units of measure and provides accompanying methodology notes to support interpretability.

### Reporting period and fiscal year

These sustainability-related financial disclosures are prepared for the reporting year **2024**.

The Bank’s fiscal year end is **31 December 2024**.

### Presentation currency

The presentation currency used in these sustainability-related financial disclosures is **euro (EUR)**.

Where currency is used as a unit of measure (for example, for climate-related capital expenditure and Tier 1 capital), amounts are presented in **EUR millions**.

### Comparative information

Comparative information is provided for **2022** and **2023** where available and relevant to understanding performance and trends.

Selected climate-related financial and emissions metrics are presented across multiple years to support comparability, including:

- climate-related capital expenditure (EUR millions) for 2022–2024; and
- Tier 1 capital (EUR millions) for 2022–2024.

### Connected information

The Bank prepares its sustainability-related financial disclosures to enable users to understand connections across disclosures and between related items.

**Connections across governance, risk management and metrics.** Climate-related risks are monitored at defined frequencies and are reported to the Board on a semi-annual basis. Governance indicators (such as Board climate expertise and the frequency of Board and committee meetings) are presented alongside climate-related metrics (such as financed emissions, operational emissions and climate-related capital expenditure) to support an integrated understanding of how oversight and monitoring relate to performance measurement.

**Connections across risk identification, assessment and monitoring.** The climate risk register includes risk descriptions, risk categories, risk ratings, and monitoring frequencies. For example, acute physical risk to mortgage collateral in flood-prone zones is described as potentially impairing collateral coverage through property damage and devaluation, and is monitored monthly. Transition and reputational risks are also described in the register, including risks associated with rapid electrification affecting internal-combustion vehicle manufacturers and suppliers, and stakeholder scrutiny of financed emissions and fossil financing.

**Connections between scenario analysis and resilience assessment.** The Bank uses climate scenario analysis to assess portfolio resilience under orderly and disorderly transition pathways. Methodology notes describe the scenario sources and modelling approach, and resilience assessments describe the implications for portfolio resilience and capital consumption.

### Methodologies

This subsection summarises the principal methodologies used in preparing climate-related disclosures, focusing on scenario analysis, risk rating, and greenhouse gas accounting approaches that underpin reported metrics.

#### Climate scenario analysis methodology

The Bank applies climate scenarios to the banking book using methodologies that link scenario pathways to sector exposures.

- **NGFS v4 Net Zero 2050 (orderly transition) scenario.** The scenario is applied using a top-down sector-pathway approach. Carbon price assumptions are taken from the IEA Net Zero Emissions by 2050 trajectory and mapped to counterparty NACE sectors. Stranded asset estimates are derived from sector-level fossil fuel capital expenditure exposure. The analysis scope covers all lending book exposures across **Germany, France, Italy, the Netherlands and Spain**.

- **NGFS v4 Below 2°C (orderly) scenario.** The scenario is applied using NGFS integrated assessment model outputs aligned to a **1.8°C median temperature pathway**. The carbon price trajectory is sourced from the REMIND-MAgPIE model. Financial impact is assessed through loan-level probability of default sensitivity to carbon cost pass-through by sector carbon intensity tier.

Resilience assessments are presented for both orderly and disorderly transition scenarios:

- Under **orderly transition scenarios**, the portfolio is assessed as demonstrating adequate resilience, with transition risk losses remaining within Pillar 2 capital buffer thresholds.
- Under **disorderly transition scenarios**, near-term resilience is assessed as acceptable, while medium-term capital consumption from stranded asset impairments is assessed as material. Sector exposure limits and portfolio decarbonisation glide-path monitoring are described as primary resilience mechanisms, and additional capital buffers may be required post-2030 under the most adverse disorderly assumptions.

#### Climate risk rating methodology

Climate risk ratings in the risk register are derived from a **5×5 risk matrix** based on likelihood and severity scores. The overall score is calculated as the product of the likelihood score and the severity score.

Risk rating bands are defined as follows:

- **Low:** score less than 3
- **Medium:** score from 3 to 6
- **High:** score from 8 to 12
- **Critical:** score from 15 to 25

As an illustration of application, the risk “Physical risk — mortgage book flood exposure” is rated **critical** with a likelihood score of **4** and a severity score of **5**, consistent with a score of 20.

#### Greenhouse gas emissions accounting and market-based Scope 2

Operational emissions metrics are presented for 2024, including Scope 1, Scope 2 (location-based and market-based), and Scope 3 business travel.

For **Scope 2 market-based emissions**, the Bank’s market-based figure is sourced directly from utility invoice data that already reflects market-based accounting. Grid emission factors are adjusted for renewable energy certificates and power purchase agreements. Renewable energy certificate registry information is used for disclosure narrative (for example, certificate type, volume and registry) and is not subtracted again in the calculation, avoiding double-counting.

#### Financed emissions and intensity metrics

The Bank reports financed emissions for 2024 and a carbon intensity metric expressed as tonnes of CO₂e per EUR million.

In addition, the Bank applies methodological considerations for specific asset classes. For sovereign bonds, issuer enterprise value including cash and issuer revenue are not used because these are corporate concepts not applicable to sovereigns.

### Estimates and judgements

The preparation of climate-related disclosures involves estimates and judgements, particularly where forward-looking analysis is required.

- **Scenario analysis and resilience assessments** require judgement in selecting scenario pathways and translating scenario variables (such as carbon prices and temperature pathways) into sector and counterparty impacts. The Bank’s methodologies include mapping carbon price assumptions to counterparty NACE sectors and assessing probability of default sensitivity to carbon cost pass-through by sector carbon intensity tier.

- **Stranded asset estimation** under transition scenarios involves estimation based on sector-level fossil fuel capital expenditure exposure.

- **Risk assessment and prioritisation** involve judgement in assigning likelihood and severity scores within the defined risk matrix, which then determine the risk rating band.

### Data quality and key characteristics of information

The Bank’s sustainability-related financial disclosures are prepared with the objective of providing information that is comparable, verifiable, timely and understandable.

#### Emissions data quality profile

For emissions-related information, the Bank summarises the composition of data sources and estimation approaches used:

| Emissions data quality category | Share (%) |
|---|---:|
| Estimated using economic data | 47.5 |
| CDP disclosure-based | 35.5 |
| Proxy model-based | 16.3 |
| Audited report-based | 0.7 |

This profile is intended to help users understand the extent to which emissions information is derived from estimates, third-party disclosures, proxy modelling and audited sources.

#### Monitoring frequency and change tracking for climate risks

The climate risk register includes monitoring frequencies and whether a risk has changed since the prior period. Monitoring frequencies observed in the register include **monthly**, **quarterly**, and **semi-annual**. This supports timeliness and ongoing oversight of climate-related risks.

### Targets and progress measurement

Where targets are disclosed, progress is presented using the specified progress metric for each target.

- A target with baseline year **2020** covers **Scope 1 and Scope 2** emissions, uses **tCO₂e** as the metric and **absolute emissions (tCO₂e absolute)** as the progress metric. Progress reported for 2024 is **40.0%**.
- Additional target progress measures reported for 2024 include **25.0%** (progress metric: tCO₂e per EUR million) and **13.3%** (progress metric: percentage reduction versus baseline).

### Value chain boundary considerations

The Bank identifies material nodes within its value chain for the purposes of sustainability-related disclosures. Within the Bank’s own operations, “Corporate fleet & business travel” is identified as a material node. Value chain mapping also distinguishes upstream and downstream node types.

### Assurance

The Bank reports **limited** external assurance as part of its governance maturity indicators.
