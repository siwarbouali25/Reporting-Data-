## General requirements

This section describes the basis on which the Bank’s sustainability-related financial disclosures have been prepared, including the reporting period and currency, the approach to comparatives, how information is connected across disclosures, and the key methodologies, estimates and judgements applied to climate-related information.

### Basis of preparation and fair presentation

The Bank’s sustainability-related financial disclosures are prepared to present fairly sustainability-related risks and opportunities that could reasonably be expected to affect the Bank’s prospects. Fair presentation is supported through disclosures that are intended to be complete, neutral and accurate, and that are prepared in a manner designed to be comparable, verifiable, timely and understandable.

The disclosures are structured to enable users to understand connections:

- **Between sustainability-related matters** (for example, how acute physical risks to mortgage collateral and transition risks in specific sectors can both affect credit risk and portfolio resilience).
- **Within the sustainability-related financial disclosures** (for example, how governance arrangements for climate oversight relate to the Bank’s climate risk identification and monitoring processes, and to the metrics used to track performance).

Where currency is used as a unit of measure in these disclosures, the Bank uses the presentation currency applied in its related financial statements.

### Reporting period, fiscal year-end and reporting currency

The Bank’s reporting year is **2024**.

- **Fiscal year-end:** **31 December 2024**
- **Reporting currency:** **euro (EUR)**

### Comparative information

Comparative information is provided for **2022** and **2023** where relevant to support trend analysis and comparability.

Selected sustainability-related metrics are presented across multiple years, including climate-related capital expenditure and Tier 1 capital (see table below). Financed emissions are also presented for 2022 and 2024 in the evidence available.

### Connected information

The Bank prepares climate-related disclosures in a way that supports connected information across governance, risk management, strategy and metrics.

- **Governance and oversight cadence connected to risk monitoring:** Climate risk reporting to the Board is provided **semi-annually**, while the climate risk register includes risks monitored at **monthly**, **quarterly** and **semi-annual** frequencies depending on the risk.
- **Risk identification connected to scenario analysis:** The Bank’s climate scenario analysis is applied to the banking book and is used to assess portfolio resilience under orderly and disorderly transition pathways. This provides context for how identified transition risks (for example, stranded asset risk in internal-combustion vehicle value chains) could translate into credit impacts.
- **Metrics connected to risk profile:** The Bank discloses financed emissions and carbon intensity metrics alongside qualitative descriptions of key climate risks (for example, flood exposure in mortgage collateral and chronic climate impacts on agricultural counterparties).

### Methodologies

This subsection summarises the principal methodologies used to prepare climate-related information, focusing on scenario analysis, risk rating, and greenhouse gas accounting elements evidenced in the reporting dataset.

#### Climate scenario analysis methodology

The Bank applies climate scenarios to assess the resilience of its banking book under transition pathways.

**Orderly transition scenarios**

- The Bank applies the **NGFS v4 Net Zero 2050** scenario to the banking book using a **top-down sector-pathway approach**.
- Carbon price assumptions are mapped from the **IEA NZE 2050 trajectory** to counterparties using **NACE sector** mapping.
- Stranded asset estimates are derived from **sector-level fossil fuel capex exposure**.
- The analysis scope covers **all lending book exposures** across **Germany (DE), France (FR), Italy (IT), the Netherlands (NL) and Spain (ES)**.

The Bank also applies an **NGFS v4 Below 2°C (orderly)** scenario using NGFS integrated assessment model outputs aligned to a **1.8°C median temperature pathway**. The carbon price trajectory is sourced from the **REMIND-MAgPIE** model. Financial impact is assessed through **loan-level probability of default sensitivity** to carbon cost pass-through, differentiated by **sector carbon intensity tier**.

**Disorderly transition scenarios**

For disorderly transition scenarios, the Bank’s resilience assessment describes near-term resilience as acceptable, with **material medium-term capital consumption** from stranded asset impairments under the most adverse assumptions. The Bank identifies **sector exposure limits** and **portfolio decarbonisation glide-path monitoring** as primary resilience mechanisms, and notes that **additional capital buffers may be required post-2030** under the most adverse disorderly assumptions.

#### Climate risk rating methodology

Climate risk ratings in the climate risk register are derived from a **5×5 risk matrix** based on the product of a likelihood score and a severity score.

- The **risk score** is calculated as **likelihood × severity**.
- Risk bands are defined as:
  - **Low:** scores **below 3**
  - **Medium:** scores **3–6**
  - **High:** scores **8–12**
  - **Critical:** scores **15–25**

As an illustration of application, the risk “**Physical risk — mortgage book flood exposure**” is rated **critical**, with a **likelihood score of 4** and a **severity score of 5**, and is monitored **monthly**.

#### Greenhouse gas accounting: Scope 2 market-based reconciliation

For Scope 2 market-based emissions, the Bank’s Scope 2 market-based figure is sourced directly from utility invoice data that already reflects market-based accounting. Grid emission factors are adjusted for renewable energy certificates and power purchase agreements. Renewable energy certificate registry data is used for disclosure narrative (for example, certificate type, volume and registry) and is not subtracted again during preparation, avoiding double counting.

#### Financed emissions methodology note (sovereign bonds)

For sovereign bonds, issuer EVIC and issuer revenue are treated as not applicable because EVIC and revenue are corporate concepts and are not used for sovereigns.

### Key estimates and judgements

The preparation of climate-related disclosures involves estimates and judgements, particularly where forward-looking analysis or modelled information is used.

- **Scenario analysis translation to financial risk:** The Bank uses sector pathways, carbon price trajectories and sector mapping (including NACE sector mapping) to translate transition pathways into potential credit impacts, including probability of default sensitivities and stranded asset estimates.
- **Risk scoring and categorisation:** The assignment of risk ratings is based on the risk matrix methodology described above, requiring judgement in selecting likelihood and severity scores for each risk.
- **Portfolio resilience assessments:** Resilience statements under orderly and disorderly scenarios reflect the Bank’s assessment of the extent to which transition risk losses and capital consumption could be absorbed, including reference to Pillar 2 capital buffer thresholds in orderly scenarios.

### Data quality and information characteristics

The Bank’s sustainability-related financial disclosures are prepared to support decision-useful information characteristics, including comparability and verifiability.

#### Emissions data quality profile

The Bank summarises the basis of emissions data used in its reporting as follows:

| Emissions data basis | Share (%) |
|---|---:|
| Estimated (economic-based) | 47.5 |
| CDP disclosure | 35.5 |
| Proxy model | 16.3 |
| Audited report | 0.7 |

This profile indicates that a substantial portion of emissions information is derived from estimates and third-party disclosures, supplemented by proxy modelling and a smaller portion from audited reports.

#### External assurance

The Bank reports **limited** external assurance in relation to its governance maturity indicators.

### Value chain boundary considerations (climate-related)

The Bank identifies climate-related topics across its own operations and value chain. Within the value chain mapping evidenced, “**Corporate fleet & business travel**” is identified as a material node within the Bank’s own operations.

### Consistency of data and assumptions

The Bank prepares sustainability-related financial disclosures using data and assumptions intended to be consistent, to the extent possible, with those used in preparing the related financial statements. This includes the use of the same reporting currency (EUR) when currency is used as a unit of measure.
