## Risk Management

This section describes the processes used to identify, assess, prioritise and monitor climate-related risks, and how those processes are integrated into the organisation’s overall risk management.

### Risk identification: capturing climate-related risks across the value chain

Climate-related risks are identified and documented in a climate risk register. The register captures risks arising from the organisation’s own operations and from activities in the value chain.

Value-chain mapping is used to support identification by linking climate risk themes to specific segments. Examples of climate risk themes reflected in the value chain include:

- **Own operations (operational sites and travel):** operational sites and travel generate Scope 1–2 emissions and face physical disruption.
- **Financed activities (lending):** lending to mining and quarrying is associated with elevated transition risk from carbon pricing and demand shifts.
- **Counterparty physical risk (agriculture):** chronic heat and shifting precipitation can reduce yields for agricultural counterparties, weakening repayment capacity over the medium term.

To inform identification of physical climate hazards and exposures, the organisation uses **European Central Bank (ECB) climate indicators** as an input data source for physical risk exposures.

### Risk assessment: evaluating likelihood and severity using a structured methodology

Identified climate-related risks are assessed using a structured scoring approach that evaluates **likelihood** and **severity**. Likelihood is scored on a five-point scale (for example, likelihood scores recorded in the climate risk register include **3**, **4** and **5**).

Overall risk ratings are derived from a **5×5 risk matrix** in which the likelihood score is multiplied by a severity score to produce a combined score from 1 to 25. The combined score is then translated into rating bands as follows:

- **Critical:** combined score **15–25**
- **High:** combined score **8–12**
- **Medium:** combined score **3–6**
- **Low:** combined score **1–2**

This methodology supports consistent assessment of the nature and likelihood of climate-related risks and provides a basis for comparing risks across the register.

### Use of climate-related scenario analysis in risk identification and assessment

Scenario analysis is used to inform the identification and assessment of climate-related risks. Individual risks in the climate risk register are linked to scenario analysis references, enabling traceability between scenario work and the risks recorded and monitored.

Scenario references linked to risks in the register include **SCN0005**, **SCN0007**, **SCN0009** and **SCN0011**. These references are used to connect specific risks to the relevant scenario analysis outputs when assessing how climate conditions and transition pathways could affect risk drivers.

### Risk prioritisation: translating scores into actionable focus

Climate-related risks are prioritised using the risk rating derived from the 5×5 matrix. This enables the organisation to focus management attention and monitoring effort on risks with higher combined likelihood and severity.

Within the climate risk register, risks are categorised using the rating bands described above. Examples of risks recorded as **medium** include:

- Chronic heat and shifting precipitation reducing yields for agricultural counterparties and weakening repayment capacity over the medium term.
- Other risks in the register also carry a **medium** rating, reflecting combined scores that fall within the medium band.

In addition to the rating band, the register records the **likelihood score** for each risk, supporting prioritisation decisions by distinguishing between risks with similar overall ratings but different likelihood profiles.

### Monitoring: cadence-based review and escalation through the register

Climate-related risks are monitored through the climate risk register using defined monitoring frequencies. Monitoring cadence varies by risk, reflecting the nature of the risk and the need for timely updates.

Monitoring frequencies evidenced in the register include:

- **Monthly monitoring**
- **Quarterly monitoring**
- **Semi-annual monitoring**

This cadence-based approach supports ongoing oversight of risk drivers and enables updates to risk assessments when conditions change.

The register also tracks whether a risk entry has **changed since the prior reporting period**. Some risks are flagged as having changed since the prior period, while others are recorded as unchanged. This change tracking supports disciplined review of the risk profile over time and helps focus attention on risks where the assessment, drivers or context have shifted.

### Integration into overall risk management (ERM)

Climate-related risks are integrated into the organisation’s overall risk management process through the climate risk register. For a substantial portion of the risks evidenced, the register indicates that the risk is **integrated into enterprise risk management**. This integration supports consistent treatment of climate-related risks alongside other risk types, including the use of common assessment tools (such as the risk matrix), consistent monitoring expectations, and incorporation into broader risk reporting and oversight.

The register also evidences that integration is applied at the individual-risk level (with some risks marked as integrated and at least one risk marked as not integrated), supporting a differentiated approach to embedding climate-related risks into ERM based on the characteristics and management pathway of each risk.

### How the lifecycle operates in practice

The organisation’s climate risk management lifecycle operates as an end-to-end process:

1. **Identify** climate-related risks using the climate risk register, supported by value-chain mapping and external physical risk inputs (including ECB climate indicators).
2. **Assess** risks by scoring likelihood and severity and deriving a combined score using the 5×5 matrix.
3. **Prioritise** risks by translating combined scores into rating bands (low, medium, high, critical) and using those ratings to focus management attention.
4. **Monitor** risks through defined review cadences (monthly, quarterly or semi-annual) and by tracking whether risks have changed since the prior reporting period.
5. **Integrate** climate-related risks into overall risk management through ERM integration flags within the register and the use of consistent assessment and monitoring disciplines.

Together, these processes provide a structured basis for identifying, assessing, prioritising and monitoring climate-related risks across operations and the value chain, and for integrating those risks into the organisation’s overall risk management framework.
