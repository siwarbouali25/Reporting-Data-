### Strategy

#### Overview

Eurolux Universal Bank AG’s climate strategy is structured to integrate climate-related risks and opportunities into its value chain, risk management, transition planning, resource allocation and scenario analysis. The strategy focuses on the lending and investment portfolio, where climate-related financial risks and opportunities are most material, and on the Bank’s own operations.

Physical and transition risks are identified and assessed through a dedicated climate risk register and forward-looking scenario analysis. These insights inform portfolio steering, sectoral exposure management, client engagement and the development of green and transition finance offerings. Resource allocation decisions, including climate-related capital and operating expenditure, are aligned with these strategic priorities.

Scenario analysis using NGFS v4 pathways is used to test the resilience of the Bank’s strategy under orderly, disorderly and hot house climate futures. Climate-related targets and progress are summarised in the Metrics and targets section and provide directional guidance for decarbonisation of own operations and financed emissions.

#### Sustainability risks and opportunities across the value chain

This subsection describes where climate-related risks and opportunities arise across the Bank’s value chain and over which time horizons they may affect prospects.

The Bank’s primary climate-related risks arise in its lending activities to corporate borrowers and households, with additional exposures in its own operations and selected upstream suppliers.

Key transition risks include:

- Stranded assets in fossil fuel sector exposure (high risk, long term), with an estimated financial impact of EUR 136.5 million, reflecting potential demand shifts and valuation declines.
- Technology-driven transition risk from the shift to electric vehicles in auto lending (high risk, short term), with an estimated impact of EUR 165.2 million.
- Policy-driven transition risk from carbon pricing on industrial loans (high risk, medium term), with an estimated impact of EUR 67.5 million.
- Reputational risk from scrutiny of financed emissions (medium risk, medium term), with an estimated impact of EUR 65.5 million.

Physical risks are concentrated in:

- Mortgage book flood exposure (high risk, long term), with an estimated impact of EUR 24.8 million.
- Wildfire exposure in parts of the southern portfolio (medium risk, short term), with an estimated impact of EUR 22.7 million.

Across the value chain, material downstream financing counterparties include corporate borrowers in high-carbon and climate-sensitive sectors such as manufacture of coke and refined petroleum (EUR 3,903.65 million exposure), mining of coal and lignite (EUR 3,547.58 million), mining of metal ores (EUR 2,749.58 million) and other mining and quarrying (EUR 2,102.44 million). These nodes are primarily exposed to transition policy risk. Other large exposures, such as manufacture of motor vehicles (EUR 3,871.6 million) and education (EUR 2,962.25 million), are subject to combinations of physical and transition risk.

The Bank’s own operations, including buildings and corporate fleet and travel, contribute to Scope 1 and 2 emissions and relevant Scope 3 categories such as business travel, and face physical disruption and transition market risk. Upstream suppliers are relevant where they provide energy, technology and services that influence the Bank’s operational footprint.

Opportunities arise mainly in downstream products and services. The Bank has identified estimated revenue opportunities from:

- Green and sustainability-linked loan growth aligned with the EU Taxonomy (EUR 180.0 million, medium-term estimate, medium confidence).
- Sustainable bond underwriting and distribution (EUR 95.0 million, short-term estimate, medium confidence).
- Transition advisory and ESG due diligence services (EUR 42.0 million, medium-term estimate, medium confidence).
- Renewable energy project finance for solar, wind and battery storage (EUR 130.0 million, long-term estimate, medium confidence).
- Operational energy efficiency in own facilities (EUR 18.0 million, short-term estimate, medium confidence).

These opportunity estimates, totalling EUR 465.0 million, are indicative estimates with stated confidence levels and are not assured revenue.

The Bank defines short term as 0–2 years, medium term as 2–5 years and long term as beyond 5 years. These horizons are aligned with internal planning cycles and capital management horizons.

#### Strategic management of climate-related risks and opportunities

This subsection explains how the Bank manages identified climate-related risks and opportunities through its strategy and risk management processes.

Climate-related risks are integrated into the Bank’s risk management framework via a climate risk register that distinguishes physical acute, physical chronic, transition market, transition policy, transition technology and transition reputational risks. High- and medium-rated risks are prioritised for management actions.

Key management mechanisms include:

- Scenario-based risk management: Climate scenario stress testing is integrated into the internal capital adequacy assessment process to assess transition technology risk, including the shift to electric vehicles in auto lending.
- Collateral and hazard overlays: Collateral revaluation and flood mapping overlays are used for selected portfolios, including wildfire-exposed assets, to reflect climate-related risk in collateral values and risk appetite.
- Sector exposure management: Sector exposure limits and enhanced due diligence are applied to industrial sectors sensitive to carbon pricing, such as mining and heavy manufacturing, to manage transition policy risk.
- Portfolio decarbonisation monitoring: A portfolio decarbonisation glide path is monitored to address reputational risk from financed emissions and to guide portfolio steering over time. This monitoring is informed by portfolio-level metrics, including total financed emissions of approximately 36.0 million tonnes of CO2 equivalent and a carbon intensity of around 1,155 tonnes of CO2 equivalent per million euro of exposure.
- Client engagement: Engagement and transition-plan covenants with borrowers are used in flood-exposed mortgage portfolios and other relevant segments to encourage risk mitigation and adaptation at counterparty level.

On the opportunity side, the Bank’s strategy includes expansion of green and sustainability-linked lending, growth in sustainable bond underwriting, development of transition advisory services and increased renewable energy project finance. These activities are intended to support clients’ transition pathways while diversifying the Bank’s revenue base.

Climate-related targets provide directional guidance. The Bank has set:

- A 2030 Scope 1 and 2 absolute reduction target under the 1.5°C framework, with 40.0% progress reported for 2024.
- A financed emissions intensity reduction target for financed emissions to 2030 under a net-zero banking framework, with 25.0% progress.
- A net-zero target across all scopes by 2050 under a net-zero banking framework, with 13.3% progress.

These targets inform strategic planning and portfolio steering. The Bank does not currently disclose the detailed assumptions and dependencies underlying the transition pathway in this section. Quantified or documented trade-off analysis for climate-related strategy decisions, such as explicit financial trade-offs between short-term returns and long-term decarbonisation or adaptation objectives, has not yet been disclosed.

#### Climate-related effects on business model, value chain and decision-making

This subsection outlines how climate-related risks and opportunities influence the Bank’s business model, value chain and decision-making.

The Bank’s business model is centred on lending, capital markets and advisory services. Climate-related risks and opportunities affect this model primarily through changes in sectoral credit risk, collateral values, client demand for products and services, and regulatory expectations.

In the lending portfolio, high-carbon sector exposure of 35.5% (EUR 11,059.49 million) and fossil fuel exposure of 22.15% (EUR 6,899.59 million) influence decisions on sectoral risk appetite, pricing and tenor. The Bank’s green loan book of EUR 3,335.75 million, representing 10.71% of total loans, reflects a strategic shift towards lower-carbon and transition-aligned activities. Financed emissions of approximately 35,973,168 tonnes of CO2 equivalent and a carbon intensity of about 1,154.8 tonnes of CO2 equivalent per million euro of exposure are used in portfolio reviews, reputational risk assessments and decarbonisation glide-path monitoring.

Across the value chain, climate considerations affect:

- Downstream financing counterparties: Sectoral strategies for mining, fossil fuels, automotive and other carbon-intensive industries are informed by transition risk assessments, scenario analysis and sector exposure limits.
- Retail and mortgage customers: Physical risk assessments, including flood and wildfire exposure, inform collateral valuation, underwriting standards and borrower engagement.
- Own operations: Energy efficiency and emissions reduction initiatives in buildings and fleet influence facility management and procurement decisions.

Decision-making processes incorporate climate-related information through:

- Integration of climate scenarios into capital planning and risk appetite setting.
- Use of climate risk metrics, such as financed emissions, carbon intensity and sectoral exposures, in portfolio reviews.
- Consideration of climate-related opportunities in product development and client coverage strategies.

The Bank’s strategic planning recognises that climate-related risks and opportunities may lead to changes in portfolio composition over time, including a gradual reduction in high-carbon exposures and growth in green and transition finance. The Bank does not currently disclose a separate, detailed business-model impact assessment beyond these portfolio and value-chain effects.

#### Financial effects and resource allocation

This subsection describes how climate-related risks and opportunities influence the Bank’s financial position, performance, cash flows and resource allocation.

Resource allocation

The Bank tracks climate-related capital and operating expenditure as indicators of strategic resource allocation:

- Climate-related capital expenditure in 2024 amounted to EUR 476.95 million.
- Climate-related operating expenditure in 2024 amounted to EUR 219.32 million.

These amounts include investments and expenses related to, for example, energy efficiency in own operations, climate risk data and modelling capabilities, and development of green and transition finance products. A detailed activity-level breakdown is not disclosed in this section. These figures are indicators of strategic prioritisation and do not represent separate line items in the primary financial statements; they are embedded within existing categories such as property and equipment, intangible assets, personnel expenses and administrative expenses.

Current and anticipated financial effects

Climate-related risks may affect the Bank’s financial position and performance through the following transmission channels:

- Credit risk: Potential increases in expected credit losses on loans to sectors exposed to transition policy and technology risk (for example, fossil fuels, mining, automotive) and to physical risk (for example, flood-exposed mortgages, wildfire-prone regions).
- Market and valuation risk: Potential revaluation of collateral and financed assets in high-carbon sectors, including stranded asset risk in fossil fuel exposures.
- Net interest income and fee income: Changes in client demand for green loans, sustainable bonds and transition advisory services may influence interest and fee income. The Bank has identified estimated revenue opportunities of EUR 465.0 million across green lending, sustainable bond underwriting, transition advisory, renewable energy project finance and operational energy efficiency. These are forward-looking estimates with medium confidence and should not be interpreted as assured future revenue.
- Operating expenses: Investments in climate-related capabilities and operational efficiency may affect cost structures in the short term, with potential longer-term cost savings from energy efficiency.

The Bank has not yet quantified the impact of climate-related risks and opportunities on specific current-period financial statement line items, such as movements in expected credit losses, impairments or individual income or expense categories.

Scenario-based financial effects

Scenario analysis provides modelled estimates of potential financial impacts under different climate pathways. These include:

- A maximum modelled physical risk loss equivalent to 32.0% of capital in 2050 under a Current Policies hot house scenario.
- A maximum modelled transition risk loss equivalent to 23.1% of capital in 2025 under a Divergent Net Zero disorderly scenario.
- A maximum modelled stranded assets estimate of EUR 33,575.2 million in 2050 under a Net Zero 2050 orderly scenario, indicating potential valuation pressure and credit loss risk on fossil fuel-related exposures.
- A maximum modelled revenue at risk of EUR 35,509.1 million in 2025 under a Nationally Determined Contributions hot house scenario.

These scenario outputs are modelled estimates used for strategic planning and risk management and should not be interpreted as realised losses or as forecasts. The Bank does not currently disclose detailed mapping of these effects to specific financial statement line items or planned sources of funding to implement its climate strategy.

#### Climate resilience and scenario analysis

This subsection explains how the Bank assesses the resilience of its strategy and business model using climate-related scenario analysis.

Scenario framework and scope

The Bank uses NGFS v4 scenarios to assess climate-related risks and opportunities across its lending book. The analysis covers exposures in key European jurisdictions, including Germany, France, Italy, the Netherlands and Spain, and considers three scenario types:

- Orderly transition scenarios, including Net Zero 2050 and Below 2°C.
- Disorderly transition scenarios, including Divergent Net Zero and Delayed Transition.
- Hot house world scenarios, including Nationally Determined Contributions and Current Policies.

Time horizons include short term (2025), medium term (2030) and long term (2050), aligned with the Bank’s strategic and capital planning horizons.

Key assumptions and methodology

Key modelling assumptions include:

- Carbon prices: A range of EUR 1.2–287.1 per tonne of CO2 equivalent across scenarios, reflecting different policy stringency and timing.
- Temperature outcomes: Scenario-consistent global temperature outcomes of approximately 1.5°C, 1.8°C, 2.5°C and 3.0°C above pre-industrial levels.
- Technology readiness: Varying levels of technology readiness (low, medium, high) across scenarios, particularly for low-carbon technologies in power, transport and industry.

Methodology by scenario type

- Orderly (for example, Net Zero 2050): A top-down sector pathway approach is applied to the banking book, mapping sectoral emissions and activity pathways to counterparty sectors using NACE classifications. Carbon price trajectories are aligned with an international net-zero energy scenario. Stranded asset estimates are derived from sector-level fossil fuel capital expenditure exposure.
- Disorderly (for example, Divergent Net Zero): The analysis focuses on technology transition risk in automotive and energy manufacturing portfolios, with divergent sectoral transition speeds and country-specific carbon prices based on national emissions trading systems and carbon taxes.
- Hot house (for example, Nationally Determined Contributions, Current Policies): The analysis emphasises physical risk, with carbon pricing ramp-up delayed or limited. Revenue at risk is estimated from sector revenue sensitivity to carbon prices and physical impacts.

Key modelled outputs

The scenario analysis yields the following key modelled estimates:

- Maximum physical risk loss equivalent to 32.0% of capital in 2050 under a Current Policies hot house scenario.
- Maximum transition risk loss equivalent to 23.1% of capital in 2025 under a Divergent Net Zero disorderly scenario.
- Maximum stranded assets estimate of EUR 33,575.2 million in 2050 under a Net Zero 2050 orderly scenario, indicating potential valuation pressure and credit loss risk on fossil fuel-related exposures.
- Maximum revenue at risk of EUR 35,509.1 million in 2025 under a Nationally Determined Contributions hot house scenario.

Scenario-specific resilience

Orderly scenarios: Under orderly transition scenarios, transition risk losses remain within internal capital buffer thresholds. Green loan growth, client engagement on transition plans and portfolio decarbonisation monitoring are expected to reduce high-carbon sector concentration over the medium term. The Bank’s strategy is assessed as adequately resilient to orderly transition pathways within the defined assumptions and capital planning horizons.

Disorderly scenarios: Under disorderly scenarios, near-term resilience is acceptable but medium-term capital consumption from stranded asset impairments and higher credit losses is material. Sector exposure limits, enhanced due diligence and portfolio decarbonisation monitoring are key resilience mechanisms. Additional capital buffers may be required after 2030 under the most adverse disorderly assumptions.

Hot house scenarios: Under hot house world scenarios, physical risk becomes the dominant long-term challenge. Collateral revaluation risk in flood-exposed mortgage books and increased default risk among agricultural and other climate-sensitive borrowers are key transmission channels. Flood-zone overlays, hazard mapping and energy performance certificate-based collateral monitoring are used to manage these risks. The Bank has not provided sufficient detail in this section to assess broader operational adaptation capacity beyond these mechanisms.

Adaptation capacity

Across scenarios, adaptation capacity is informed by:

- Financial resources: Climate-related capital expenditure of EUR 476.95 million and operating expenditure of EUR 219.32 million, which indicate the Bank’s willingness to invest in climate-related capabilities and operational efficiency.
- Portfolio flexibility: Sector exposure limits, green loan growth, decarbonisation glide-path monitoring and client engagement mechanisms, which provide levers to adjust portfolio composition over time.
- Product and service development: Expansion of green and transition finance products and advisory services, which may support clients’ adaptation and transition efforts and diversify the Bank’s income sources.

These elements contribute to the Bank’s ability to adjust its strategy and portfolio under different climate scenarios, subject to the uncertainties inherent in long-term climate and policy pathways.

#### Strategy limitations and evidence boundaries

This subsection summarises key boundaries and limitations relevant to the Strategy disclosures.

Business model: The Strategy section describes climate-related risk, opportunity, portfolio and value-chain effects. The Bank does not currently disclose a separate, detailed business-model impact assessment that quantifies how climate-related factors may change the overall business model structure beyond these portfolio-level insights.

Trade-offs: The Bank has not yet disclosed quantified or documented trade-off analysis for climate-related strategy decisions, such as explicit financial trade-offs between short-term returns and long-term decarbonisation or adaptation objectives.

Targets: Only target summary information is used in this section, including target type, scope, status, target year, framework and progress. Baseline details, interim milestones, validation bodies, gross or net status and planned use of carbon credits are not described here.

Transition plan: The Bank’s climate-related strategy and targets indicate a transition pathway, but key assumptions and dependencies underlying a comprehensive transition plan, such as specific policy, technology and customer behaviour assumptions, are not specified in this section.

Scenarios: Scenario financial outputs, including physical risk losses, transition risk losses, stranded asset estimates and revenue at risk, are modelled estimates used for strategic planning and risk management. They should not be interpreted as realised losses, forecasts or precise predictions of future financial performance.

Opportunities: Revenue impacts associated with climate-related opportunities, including green lending, sustainable bond underwriting, transition advisory, renewable energy project finance and operational energy efficiency, are estimates with stated confidence levels. They should not be interpreted as assured future revenue.

Resilience: Observations on climate resilience are scenario-specific and reflect the Bank’s assessment under the defined NGFS v4 pathways, assumptions and time horizons. They do not constitute a general statement of overall bank-wide resilience to all possible climate-related outcomes.
