### Strategy

#### Overview

Eurolux Universal Bank AG’s climate-related strategy is structured to manage material physical and transition risks across its value chain, capture defined climate-related financing opportunities, and align portfolio steering with its transition and net zero ambitions. The strategy integrates risk identification from the climate risk register with sector-level portfolio analysis, high-carbon and fossil-fuel exposure metrics, and the expansion of green and sustainability-linked products.

This architecture applies a value-chain perspective encompassing upstream funding and suppliers, own operations, and downstream financing counterparties. It supports the incorporation of climate factors into credit analysis, sector exposure management, and client engagement, as well as resource allocation decisions evidenced by dedicated climate-related capital and operating expenditure. Climate-related targets—including a 2030 absolute reduction target for Scope 1 and 2 emissions aligned to an SBTi 1.5°C pathway framework per target summary, a financed emissions intensity objective, and a 2050 net zero goal—frame the Bank’s longer-term transition planning. Scenario analysis based on NGFS v4 orderly, disorderly, and hot house scenarios informs strategic decision-making by quantifying modelled portfolio losses, stranded asset risks, and revenue at risk across short-, medium-, and long-term horizons.

#### Sustainability risks and opportunities across the value chain

The 2024 climate risk register identifies eight material climate-related risks: three physical and five transition risks, with ratings from medium to high and time horizons spanning short term (0–2 years), medium term (2–5 years), and long term (more than 5 years). Key physical risks include flood exposure in the mortgage portfolio and wildfire exposure in the southern lending book, both classified as acute physical risks with high or medium risk ratings and potential impacts up to EUR 24.8 million. These risks principally affect downstream retail and corporate lending, as well as collateral values in flood- and fire-prone regions.

Transition risks are concentrated in carbon-intensive corporate segments. High-priority items include technology-driven transition risk from the shift to electric vehicles in auto lending (short term, modelled impact EUR 165.2 million), market-driven stranded asset risk in fossil fuel sector exposure (long term, EUR 136.5 million), and policy-driven carbon pricing impacts on industrial loans (medium term, EUR 67.5 million). Reputational risk from scrutiny of financed emissions is rated medium with a potential impact of EUR 65.5 million. These risks are embedded in a portfolio with 35.5% exposure to high-carbon sectors (EUR 11,059.49 million) and 22.15% exposure to fossil fuel-related activities (EUR 6,899.59 million), highlighting concentration in downstream financing counterparties.

Risks and opportunities are concentrated in specific value-chain nodes. The largest quantified downstream exposures include: Manufacture of coke and refined petroleum (EUR 3,903.65 million), Manufacture of motor vehicles (EUR 3,871.6 million), Mining of coal and lignite (EUR 3,547.58 million), Education (EUR 2,962.25 million), Mining of metal ores (EUR 2,749.58 million), and Other mining and quarrying (EUR 2,102.44 million). Own operations (buildings, fleet, and business travel) face chronic physical risk and transition pressures linked to operational emissions. Upstream suppliers are affected through evolving expectations on data and environmental performance, though some nodes are assessed qualitatively rather than quantitatively.

The Bank has identified climate-related opportunities, mainly in downstream client financing and advisory, with estimated potential revenue impacts of EUR 465.0 million over varying time horizons. These are treated as estimates with medium confidence, not assured benefits.

#### Strategic management of climate-related risks and opportunities

Eurolux Universal Bank AG manages climate-related risks and opportunities through integrated portfolio steering, engagement with clients in carbon-intensive sectors, and development of a differentiated green and transition finance offering. Transition planning is framed by three key targets: an absolute reduction target for Scope 1 and 2 emissions aligned to an SBTi 1.5°C pathway framework per target summary (40.0% progress as of 2024), a financed emissions intensity reduction target for Category 15 Scope 3 emissions under the Net-Zero Banking Alliance framework with a 2030 horizon (25.0% progress), and a 2050 net zero target covering all scopes (13.3% progress). Validation status, baselines, and milestones are not available in the source data.

Risk management mechanisms evidenced in the risk register and scenario analysis include: climate scenario stress testing integrated into the internal capital adequacy assessment process; sector exposure limits and enhanced due diligence for industrial sectors exposed to carbon pricing; collateral revaluation and flood mapping overlays for property-related physical and stranded asset risk; and portfolio decarbonisation glide-path monitoring to respond to reputational and policy-driven pressures on financed emissions. Engagement with borrowers is used to discuss transition plans and, where relevant, integrate climate-related covenants into lending structures.

On the opportunity side, the Bank is steering growth in green and sustainability-linked loans (EUR 3,335.75 million, 10.71% of the loan book), sustainable capital market products such as green and social bond underwriting, transition advisory, and renewable energy project finance. These activities are intended to reallocate balance-sheet deployment towards lower-carbon and transition-enabling assets while managing high-carbon exposures. The available evidence does not specify the detailed assumptions or dependencies underlying the transition plan.

#### Climate-related effects on business model, value chain and decision-making

Climate-related risks and opportunities are increasingly influencing Eurolux Universal Bank AG’s lending and financing activities, with a particular focus on downstream corporate borrowers. The Bank’s total loan portfolio of EUR 31,150.64 million includes high-carbon sector exposure of 35.5% (EUR 11,059.49 million) and fossil fuel-related exposure of 22.15% (EUR 6,899.59 million). These concentrations, combined with modelled stranded asset risk and physical risk exposures, are shaping sectoral risk appetite and credit decision-making, notably for fossil fuel extraction, mining, and energy-intensive manufacturing.

The value-chain mapping exercise indicates that own operations (buildings and fleet) are being addressed through targets for Scope 1 and 2 emissions and resource efficiency initiatives, while upstream suppliers are expected to meet higher climate and data standards. Downstream, sector-by-sector analysis of climate exposure for corporate borrowers in manufacturing, mining, education, and other sectors informs credit analysis, pricing, and collateral requirements, particularly in regions exposed to floods, wildfires, or transition-driven demand shifts. Climate factors, including carbon pricing trajectories and technology transition risk, are incorporated into scenario-based analyses of portfolio quality and potential capital impacts.

Decision-making incorporates these insights through mechanisms such as sector exposure limits, enhanced due diligence for carbon-intensive borrowers, and monitoring of financed emissions intensity against the 2030 and 2050 targets. Green and sustainability-linked financing propositions provide alternative pathways for clients transitioning their business models, influencing product design and origination strategies. The available evidence describes risk, opportunity, portfolio, and value-chain effects, but does not include a separate, detailed business-model impact assessment or quantified analysis of specific trade-offs between climate-related considerations and other strategic objectives.

#### Financial effects and resource allocation

Climate-related risks and opportunities are associated with both direct financial effects and forward-looking, modelled impacts on Eurolux Universal Bank AG’s balance sheet and income profile. For the reporting period, the Bank reports dedicated climate-related capital expenditure of EUR 476.95 million and climate-related operating expenditure of EUR 219.32 million. These amounts represent resource allocation indicators towards climate-related initiatives; the evidence does not provide a detailed activity-level breakdown or a precise mapping to individual income statement captions. Relevant accounting impacts are expected to arise through changes in net interest income, fee income, credit loss allowances, and operating expenses as climate-related actions are implemented.

On the risk side, modelled financial impacts from the risk register highlight potential losses from specific climate drivers: EUR 165.2 million associated with the shift to electric vehicles in auto lending, EUR 136.5 million from stranded assets in fossil fuel sector exposure, and EUR 67.5 million from carbon pricing on industrial loans, across different time horizons. These figures inform capital planning and risk appetite but are not realised losses. Scenario analysis provides modelled estimates of the maximum share of capital at risk from physical and transition risks and potential stranded assets.

On the opportunity side, estimated cumulative revenue impacts from identified climate-related opportunities total EUR 465.0 million, with examples including EUR 180.0 million from green and sustainability-linked loan growth, EUR 95.0 million from sustainable bond underwriting, EUR 42.0 million from transition advisory, EUR 130.0 million from renewable energy project finance, and EUR 18.0 million from operational energy efficiency measures. These figures are treated as scenario-based estimates with medium confidence and are not assured future revenue. They guide strategic prioritisation of product development and capital deployment rather than constituting firm financial forecasts.

The available evidence does not provide sufficient detail to assess whether any identified climate risks or opportunities create a significant risk of material adjustment within the next annual reporting period to the carrying amounts of assets and liabilities. Planned sources of funding to implement the strategy are not specified in the available documentation.

#### Climate resilience and scenario analysis

Eurolux Universal Bank AG conducts climate-related scenario analysis using the NGFS v4 framework, covering orderly, disorderly, and hot house scenario families across short-term (2025), medium-term (2030), and long-term (2050) horizons. The scenario set includes Net Zero 2050, Below 2°C, Divergent Net Zero, Delayed Transition, Nationally Determined Contributions, and Current Policies. The analysis applies a top-down sector pathway methodology to the lending book across key European jurisdictions (Germany, France, Italy, the Netherlands, and Spain), with sectoral shocks translated into credit risk parameters and revenue sensitivities.

Methodology summaries by scenario type indicate that: under orderly scenarios, such as Net Zero 2050, sectoral decarbonisation follows a relatively smooth path; carbon price trajectories are derived from an external net-zero energy scenario and mapped to NACE sectors; and stranded asset estimates for 2050 (e.g., EUR 33,575.2 million in Net Zero 2050) are derived from sector-level fossil fuel capex exposure. Disorderly scenarios, such as Divergent Net Zero, assume uneven technology uptake, with technology readiness levels varying between low, medium, and high by sector and country, and carbon prices differing according to national emissions trading and carbon tax policies. Hot house scenarios, including Nationally Determined Contributions and Current Policies, assume higher temperature outcomes (up to around 3.0°C), delayed policy tightening, and a stronger emphasis on physical rather than transition risk.

Key input ranges across scenarios include carbon prices between EUR 1.2 and EUR 287.1 per tonne of CO2e and temperature outcomes spanning approximately 1.5°C to 3.0°C, with renewable energy shares and macroeconomic pathways varying by scenario type. Modelled financial outputs include: a maximum physical risk loss of 32.0% of capital in 2050 under the Current Policies hot house scenario; a maximum transition risk loss of 23.1% of capital in 2025 under the Divergent Net Zero disorderly scenario; a maximum stranded assets estimate of EUR 33,575.2 million in 2050 under the Net Zero 2050 orderly scenario; and a maximum revenue at risk of EUR 35,509.1 million in 2025 under the Nationally Determined Contributions hot house scenario. These are modelled estimates used for stress testing and should not be interpreted as realised or certain future losses.

Resilience assessments are scenario-specific and based on NGFS v4 modelled analysis. Under orderly scenarios, transition risk losses remain within internal Pillar 2 capital buffer thresholds; however, buffer threshold values and quantitative reconciliation are not included in available documentation. Mechanisms such as green loan growth, client engagement on transition plans, and decarbonisation pathway monitoring are expected to support gradual reduction in high-carbon sector concentrations over the medium term. Under disorderly scenarios, the Bank experiences materially higher near-term capital consumption from transition risk, particularly via stranded asset impairments in fossil fuel and heavy industry exposures; resilience in these cases is associated with sector exposure limits, tightened underwriting in high-risk sectors, and the potential need for additional capital buffers after 2030 under the most adverse combinations of assumptions. In hot house scenarios, physical risk dominates over longer horizons, with portfolio resilience depending on the effectiveness of flood-zone overlays, collateral revaluation processes, and energy performance certificate-based collateral monitoring for mortgage and agricultural lending portfolios.

Adaptation capacity is informed by both financial resources and portfolio flexibility mechanisms. Climate-related capex of EUR 476.95 million and opex of EUR 219.32 million indicate the scale of resources being directed to climate-related initiatives, including operational efficiency improvements and systems for risk measurement and management. Portfolio flexibility is reflected in the ability to adjust sectoral exposures (e.g., high-carbon sectors with 35.5% current exposure and 22.15% to fossil fuel-related activities), to scale green loans (currently 10.71% of the loan book), and to use decarbonisation glide-path metrics, collateral overlays, and enhanced due diligence to steer new business and refinancing. The evidence does not provide enough detail to assess full operational adaptation capacity across all business units, but it identifies mechanisms such as exposure limits, scenario stress testing, and client engagement that are intended to support adaptation of the business and lending portfolio over time.

#### Strategy limitations and evidence boundaries

The Strategy disclosures in this section are based on the strategy-specific documentation, risk register, scenario analysis, value-chain mapping, climate opportunity assessment, portfolio metrics, and target summaries available for the year ended 31 December 2024. With respect to business model effects, the available evidence describes climate-related risks, opportunities, portfolio composition, and value-chain exposures, but does not include a separate, detailed business-model impact assessment for Eurolux Universal Bank AG. Similarly, the source data do not provide quantified or documented trade-off analysis for strategy decisions, including explicit trade-offs between climate-related objectives and other financial or strategic considerations.

Target information in this section is derived from summary records only, covering target type, scope, status, target year, framework, and progress to date. Detailed target assumptions, intermediate milestones, validation evidence, and baselines are not available in the source data. For the transition plan, the documentation referenced here identifies governance decisions, net zero and interim target years, and certain portfolio steering mechanisms, but does not specify the full set of underlying assumptions or dependencies, such as detailed policy, technology, or client behaviour pathways.

All scenario-related financial outputs, including capital losses, stranded asset estimates, and revenue at risk, are modelled estimates based on the NGFS v4 scenario set and should not be interpreted as realised or guaranteed future losses. Similarly, estimated revenue impacts from climate-related opportunities (totalling EUR 465.0 million) represent internal estimates with stated confidence levels and should not be interpreted as assured or committed future revenue. Resilience observations in this section are scenario-specific and do not constitute a general statement of overall bank-wide resilience under all possible future climate or macroeconomic conditions.
