### Strategy

#### Climate-related risks and opportunities

Eurolux Universal Bank AG identifies climate-related risks and opportunities using its climate risk register, key performance indicators, targets, and climate scenario analysis. The 2024 risk register records eight material climate-related risks: three physical risks and five transition risks, spanning physical acute, physical chronic, transition market, transition policy, transition reputational, and transition technology categories. Risks are assessed over short-term (0–2 years), medium-term (2–5 years), and long-term (5+ years) horizons, with risk ratings of medium or high.

**Physical risks**

- **Physical acute risk:** “Physical risk — mortgage book flood exposure” is rated high over the long term (5+ years), with a potential financial impact of EUR 24.8 million. This risk is linked to scenario analysis (SCN0004) and is being addressed through engagement and transition-plan covenants with borrowers, as well as collateral revaluation and flood mapping overlays.
- **Physical chronic risk:** Chronic risks, such as gradual temperature and precipitation changes, affect sectors including “Corporate borrowers — Manufacture of motor vehicles” and “Corporate borrowers — Education,” classified as having physical chronic climate exposure. These risks are expected to impact operating costs, asset values, and default risk over the medium and long term. Quantitative financial impacts beyond identified top risks are not yet fully modelled.

**Transition risks**

- **Transition technology risk:** “Transition risk — EV shift in auto lending” is a high-rated risk over the short term (0–2 years), with a potential financial impact of EUR 165.2 million. This reflects the risk that regulatory and consumer shifts toward electric vehicles could reduce the value of combustion-engine-focused auto loan portfolios. Mitigation includes integrating climate scenario stress testing into the Internal Capital Adequacy Assessment Process (ICAAP).
- **Transition market risk:** “Stranded assets — fossil fuel sector exposure” is a high-rated risk over the long term (5+ years), with a potential financial impact of EUR 136.5 million. This risk reflects the possibility that changes in energy demand, technology, and regulation may impair the economic viability and collateral value of fossil fuel-related assets. Mitigation actions include collateral revaluation and flood mapping overlays.
- **Transition policy risk:** “Transition risk — carbon pricing on industrial loans” is a high-rated risk over the medium term (2–5 years), with an estimated financial impact of EUR 67.5 million. This reflects potential increases in operating costs and credit risk for industrial borrowers subject to higher carbon prices or stricter emissions regulations. Mitigation actions include sector exposure limits and enhanced due diligence for affected sectors.
- **Transition reputational risk:** “Reputational risk — financed emissions scrutiny” is a medium-rated risk over the medium term, with a potential financial impact of EUR 65.5 million. This risk arises from stakeholder and regulatory scrutiny of the bank’s financed emissions profile and decarbonisation progress. The bank’s response focuses on portfolio decarbonisation glide-path monitoring and scenario analysis (SCN0013).

**Climate-related opportunities**

The bank has identified climate-related opportunities in products, services, and resource efficiency, supported by quantitative estimates that are treated as forward-looking estimates rather than guaranteed revenue. Across five key opportunity areas, the total estimated revenue impact is EUR 465.0 million over the respective time horizons, with medium confidence:

- **Green loan growth:** Expansion of the green and sustainability-linked loan book aligned with the EU Taxonomy, driven by corporate transition financing demand (medium-term, EUR 180.0 million).
- **Sustainable bond underwriting:** Growth in green and social bond underwriting and distribution as sovereigns and corporates accelerate ESG capital market activity (short-term, EUR 95.0 million).
- **Transition advisory:** Climate risk advisory, transition planning support, and ESG due diligence services for large corporate clients (medium-term, EUR 42.0 million).
- **Renewable energy project finance:** Project finance for solar, wind, and battery storage infrastructure aligned with REPowerEU and national renewable targets (long-term, EUR 130.0 million).
- **Operational energy efficiency:** Reduction in energy costs through facility EPC upgrades and REC procurement, expected to reduce Scope 2 emissions and operational expenditure (short-term, EUR 18.0 million).

These opportunity values are estimates and should not be described as guaranteed revenue.

The bank’s climate targets include a Scope 1 and 2 reduction target under an SBTi 1.5°C framework (target TGT001), a Scope 3 Category 15 intensity reduction, and a 2050 net zero target under the Net-Zero Banking Alliance (targets TGT002 and TGT003). As of 2024, progress is 40.0% (Scope 1 and 2), 25.0% (Scope 3 Category 15), and 13.3% (all scopes) toward the respective 2030 and 2050 milestones.

#### Effects on business model and value chain

The bank’s business model is based on universal banking activities, including lending, underwriting, and advisory services. Detailed business model narratives are not fully documented in the available evidence; therefore, this section focuses on observable lending, operational, and value chain channels where quantification exists.

The value chain summary identifies 16 value chain nodes, of which 12 are material, covering downstream customers, financing counterparties, own operations, and upstream suppliers. Quantified exposure is available for key financing counterparty nodes, while some own-operations and supplier nodes are mapped qualitatively where financial exposure is unavailable. This represents a boundary for the completeness of value chain quantification.

**Downstream financing counterparties**

- **Manufacture of coke and refined petroleum:** Transition policy risk, EUR 3,903.65 million exposure (Scope 3 Category 15). Sensitive to carbon pricing, regulatory changes, and demand shifts for fossil-derived products.
- **Mining of coal and lignite:** Transition policy risk, EUR 3,547.58 million exposure (Scope 3 Category 15). Exposed to transition policies and market shifts reducing demand for coal and lignite.
- **Mining of metal ores and other mining and quarrying:** Transition policy risk, EUR 2,749.58 million and EUR 2,102.44 million exposures (Scope 3 Category 15). Exposed to carbon regulation and market shifts.
- **Manufacture of motor vehicles:** Physical chronic exposure, EUR 3,871.6 million. Chronic physical risks and technology-driven transition risks (e.g., EV shift) affect this node.
- **Education:** Physical chronic exposure, EUR 2,962.25 million. Climate impacts on buildings and infrastructure may influence credit risk.

These quantified nodes illustrate how physical and transition risks propagate through the financing value chain into credit, market, and reputational risk channels. High-carbon and fossil fuel-related sectors contribute to elevated financed emissions and concentrated transition risk, consistent with the portfolio-level high-carbon exposure of 35.5% (EUR 11,059.49 million) and fossil fuel exposure of 22.15% (EUR 6,899.59 million).

**Own operations and upstream nodes**

- **Own banking operations — buildings:** Exposed to physical chronic risk (e.g., temperature and energy efficiency impacts). EPC upgrades and energy procurement strategies contribute to resource efficiency and reduced Scope 2 emissions.
- **Corporate fleet and business travel:** Transition market exposure (Scope 3 Category 6). Influenced by fuel prices, low-emission vehicle mandates, and stakeholder expectations.

Some own-operations and supplier nodes are mapped qualitatively without financial exposure, which is a boundary for value chain analysis completeness.

**Business model effects**

- **Lending and credit:** Physical and transition risks affect credit pricing, tenor, collateral standards, and covenants.
- **Capital markets and advisory:** Opportunities in green bond underwriting and transition advisory influence fee-based income and sustainable finance positioning.
- **Operational cost base:** Energy efficiency initiatives in own buildings and fleet management support cost reduction and progress against Scope 1 and 2 targets.

Due to incomplete business model impacts evidence, these effects are described based on available value chain and portfolio metrics and may not capture all business lines in detail.

#### Effects on strategy and decision-making

Climate-related risks and opportunities inform the bank’s strategic priorities, target-setting, and resource allocation. Based on available documentation, this influence is evident in risk management integration, target frameworks, and product development. No documented strategy trade-off decisions are available, which is a boundary for describing quantified trade-off analysis.

**Strategic integration of climate risks**

- **Risk register and ICAAP:** High-rated climate-related risks, such as the EV shift in auto lending and carbon pricing on industrial loans, are integrated into the risk register and linked to climate scenario stress testing within the ICAAP. This informs capital planning and sector risk appetite decisions across time horizons.
- **Sector exposure limits and due diligence:** Medium-term transition policy risk related to carbon pricing is addressed by sector exposure limits and enhanced due diligence for industrial and high-carbon sectors, influencing credit underwriting standards and covenants.
- **Portfolio decarbonisation glide-path monitoring:** The bank monitors its financed emissions profile and progress against decarbonisation targets, informing product development (e.g., green loans) and engagement with high-carbon clients.
- **Resource allocation:** The bank has allocated EUR 476.95 million in climate-related capital expenditure (capex) and EUR 219.32 million in climate-related operational expenditure (opex), supporting EPC upgrades, REC procurement, and risk appetite measures for high-carbon exposures.

**Portfolio financial position and relevance**

- **Green loans:** EUR 3,335.75 million (10.71% of total loans) are classified as green loans, supporting the bank’s transition strategy and reducing exposure to transition risk.
- **Financed emissions:** The bank’s financed emissions total 35,973,167.69 tCO2e, with a carbon intensity of 1,154.81 tCO2e per EUR million. These metrics inform the bank’s decarbonisation targets and sectoral risk management, particularly in high-carbon and fossil fuel sectors.
