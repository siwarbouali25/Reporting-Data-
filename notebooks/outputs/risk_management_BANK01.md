### Risk management

#### Sustainability-related risk management overview

For 2024, Eurolux Universal Bank AG structures sustainability-related risk management around a climate risk register that records identified physical and transition climate risks together with their assessment, monitoring and mitigation characteristics. The 2024 register contains 8 climate-related risks, comprising 3 physical risks and 5 transition risks. These risks are categorised as physical acute, physical chronic, transition market, transition policy, transition reputational and transition technology.

Each registered risk is assigned a rating of high or medium and mapped to short- (0–2 years), medium- (2–5 years) or long-term (more than 5 years) time horizons. Financial impact is estimated for each risk in millions of euros, enabling a view of which climate-related exposures are considered most material. In 2024, transition risks associated with the shift to electric vehicles in auto lending, potential stranded fossil fuel assets and prospective carbon pricing on industrial loans are recorded as the top transition risks by financial impact, while flood exposure in the mortgage book represents the highest-impact physical risk in the register.

Monitoring frequencies recorded in the register are either quarterly or semi-annual, providing an indication of how often risk owners are expected to review and update the underlying risk information. Six of the eight risks are flagged as integrated into the Bank’s broader enterprise risk management (ERM) processes, corresponding to an ERM integration share of 75%. For these risks, mitigation actions are recorded in the risk register and, where evidenced, linked to existing risk processes such as the Internal Capital Adequacy Assessment Process (ICAAP).

Scenario analysis is used as a forward-looking input for selected risks rather than uniformly across all risk types. The risk register evidences scenario links to four scenario identifiers (SCN0002, SCN0004, SCN0011 and SCN0013), which in turn draw on 18 climate scenarios based on the NGFS v4 framework, including orderly, disorderly and hot-house world pathways (for example Below 2°C, Net Zero 2050, Delayed Transition and Current Policies) with horizon years of 2025, 2030 and 2050. These scenario references inform the forward-looking assessment of selected risks but are not recorded as realised financial losses.

Compared with the prior period, 4 of the 8 risks are marked as changed, primarily reflecting updates to transition risk assessments, time horizons and mitigation approaches. This indicates that climate-related risks are revisited and, where necessary, re-assessed as part of ongoing risk management.

#### Upstream sustainability risks

For a commercial bank, upstream sustainability risks primarily concern access to funding markets, dependence on suppliers and external service providers, and the potential impact of climate-related developments on capital availability. In the case of Eurolux Universal Bank AG, the source data for 2024 is centred on the climate risk register and associated scenario analysis, which focus on exposures arising from the Bank’s lending, collateral and sectoral risk profile rather than on suppliers or funding counterparties.

The climate risk register does not distinguish a separate upstream risk taxonomy for suppliers, outsourcing arrangements, utilities or capital providers. Nor does it attribute specific climate-related risks to particular funding structures or to the Bank’s own access to capital. As a result, upstream sustainability-related risks are not separately identified, rated or monitored in the same structured way as downstream credit and market-related climate risks.

Nevertheless, certain transition risks recorded in the register have an indirect bearing on upstream considerations. For example, “Stranded assets — fossil fuel sector exposure” and “Reputational risk — financed emissions scrutiny” could influence perceptions of the Bank’s risk profile among investors and wholesale funding providers if they were to affect financial performance or stakeholder confidence. These connections are implicit and are not documented as distinct upstream risk processes.

Given these boundaries, the Bank’s 2024 disclosures on climate-related risk management are driven primarily by exposures in its financing activities rather than by a separately articulated upstream sustainability risk framework.

#### Risk management across internal operations

Internal operations can be exposed to climate-related risks through business continuity, premises and data-centre locations, employee health and safety during extreme weather, and internal data and reporting processes. The 2024 climate risk register for Eurolux Universal Bank AG, however, is focused on risks arising from client and collateral exposures in the real economy.

The recorded physical risks—mortgage book flood exposure, wildfire exposure in part of the portfolio and heat stress affecting agricultural borrowers—are all defined through client and collateral channels rather than internal facilities or operational processes. Similarly, the transition risks—including those linked to the shift to electric vehicles, carbon pricing, building energy performance regulation and reputational scrutiny of financed emissions—are described in terms of impacts on lending portfolios, sector exposures and client segments.

The available risk register does not provide distinct entries for internal operational climate risks such as disruption to branch networks, data centres or internal processes, nor does it document specific controls or procedures for internal climate-related operational risk. While certain mitigation actions, such as collateral revaluation and flood mapping overlays, may rely on internal data and analytical capabilities, these are recorded in the register as mitigation actions addressing credit and collateral risk in the lending portfolio, not as controls for internal facilities or operations.

On this basis, internal operations are not presented as a separate category within the 2024 climate risk register, and climate-related risk management is described in this report primarily from the perspective of the Bank’s external lending and financing exposures.

#### Downstream sustainability risks

For Eurolux Universal Bank AG, material downstream sustainability-related risks arise predominantly through its role as a lender and financial intermediary. The 2024 climate risk register indicates that physical and transition climate risks are primarily linked to loan portfolios, collateral values, sector exposures and financed emissions.

Physical risks in the lending book are recorded as:

- **Physical risk — mortgage book flood exposure** (physical acute, high rating, long-term horizon, estimated financial impact of EUR 24.8 million). This risk is monitored quarterly and is linked to a specific climate scenario reference (SCN0004). Mitigation actions recorded in the risk register include engagement and transition-plan covenants with borrowers, with the risk also flagged as integrated into ERM processes.

- **Physical risk — wildfire exposure in southern portfolio** (physical acute, medium rating, short-term horizon, estimated financial impact of EUR 22.7 million). The risk relates to certain geographic segments of the lending portfolio, with a semi-annual monitoring frequency and a scenario analysis link (SCN0002). Mitigation actions recorded in the risk register include collateral revaluation and the application of flood mapping overlays to collateral assessment. This risk is not flagged as ERM-integrated in the source data.

- **Physical risk — heat stress on agricultural borrowers** (physical chronic, medium rating, short-term horizon, estimated financial impact of EUR 20.6 million). This risk focuses on agricultural lending and is monitored semi-annually. It references scenario SCN0011 and records mitigation through climate scenario stress testing integrated into ICAAP, with ERM integration flagged.

Transition risks in downstream activities are represented by five entries:

- **Transition risk — EV shift in auto lending** (transition technology, high rating, short-term horizon, estimated financial impact of EUR 165.2 million). This risk captures potential market shifts in auto lending portfolios as electric vehicle adoption accelerates. It is monitored quarterly, linked to scenario SCN0011 and integrated into ERM. The mitigation action recorded in the risk register is climate scenario stress testing integrated into ICAAP.

- **Stranded assets — fossil fuel sector exposure** (transition market, high rating, long-term horizon, estimated financial impact of EUR 136.5 million). This risk reflects potential devaluation of fossil fuel-related assets and exposures. It is monitored quarterly and integrated into ERM. The risk register records mitigation actions such as collateral revaluation and flood mapping overlay, applied to relevant exposures.

- **Transition risk — carbon pricing on industrial loans** (transition policy, high rating, medium-term horizon, estimated financial impact of EUR 67.5 million). This risk addresses the potential impact of more stringent carbon pricing on industrial borrowers. It is monitored quarterly, ERM-integrated and mitigated through sector exposure limits and enhanced due diligence, as recorded in the register.

- **Reputational risk — financed emissions scrutiny** (transition reputational, medium rating, medium-term horizon, estimated financial impact of EUR 65.5 million). This risk relates to stakeholder scrutiny of the Bank’s financed emissions profile. It is monitored semi-annually, references scenario SCN0013 and is ERM-integrated. The mitigation action recorded in the risk register is portfolio decarbonisation glide-path monitoring.

- **Transition risk — building energy performance regulation** (transition policy, medium rating, long-term horizon, estimated financial impact of EUR 21.6 million). This risk concerns the impact of evolving energy performance requirements on real estate collateral and borrowers. It is monitored semi-annually and mitigated through green retrofit financing incentives; ERM integration is not flagged for this risk.

These downstream risks are managed through a combination of monitoring frequencies, scenario references and mitigation actions recorded in the risk register. The distinction between high- and medium-rated risks, along with their time horizons, provides insight into which segments of the lending portfolio are considered most exposed in the near term versus the longer term.

Climate-related KPIs reported by the Bank, including 2024 financed emissions of approximately 35.97 million tCO₂e, a carbon intensity of 1,154.8131 tCO₂e per EUR million, high-carbon sector exposure of 35.5% and fossil fuel exposure of 22.15%, provide contextual indicators that are relevant for monitoring transition and reputational risks linked to financed emissions and sectoral exposures. These indicators are used as contextual information and are not documented as formal risk appetite limits in the source data.

#### Climate risk management

The climate risk register constitutes the core of Eurolux Universal Bank AG’s climate risk management process for 2024. It provides a structured approach to the identification, assessment, prioritisation, monitoring and mitigation of climate-related risks across physical and transition dimensions.

**Identification and classification**

The register records 8 distinct climate-related risks, split into 3 physical and 5 transition risks. Physical risks are further classified into acute and chronic categories, reflecting short-term event-driven impacts (such as floods and wildfires) and longer-term, gradual changes (such as heat stress). Transition risks are classified into market, policy, reputational and technology categories, capturing changes in market demand, regulatory developments, stakeholder expectations and technological shifts.

**Assessment and prioritisation**

Each risk entry includes:

- A qualitative description of the risk (for example, EV shift in auto lending, stranded fossil fuel assets, carbon pricing on industrial loans, flood exposure in the mortgage book).
- A risk category (physical acute, physical chronic, transition market, transition policy, transition reputational or transition technology).
- A risk rating of high or medium.
- An assigned time horizon: short-term (0–2 years), medium-term (2–5 years) or long-term (more than 5 years).
- An estimated financial impact expressed in millions of euros.

The most material risks by estimated financial impact in 2024 are transition-related, led by the EV shift in auto lending (EUR 165.2 million), stranded fossil fuel sector exposure (EUR 136.5 million) and carbon pricing on industrial loans (EUR 67.5 million). The main physical risk by financial impact is mortgage book flood exposure (EUR 24.8 million).
