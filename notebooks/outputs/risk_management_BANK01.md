### Risk management

#### Sustainability-related risk management overview

Eurolux Universal Bank AG uses a dedicated climate risk register as the primary basis for describing climate-related risk management in this report. For the year ended 31 December 2024, the register records eight climate-related risks that are considered relevant for this disclosure, comprising three physical risks and five transition risks. The register categorises these risks as acute physical, chronic physical, transition market, transition policy, transition reputational and transition technology. Each recorded risk is assigned a rating (high or medium), a time horizon (short term: 0–2 years; medium term: 2–5 years; long term: more than 5 years), an indicative financial impact and a monitoring frequency. The risks relate mainly to potential effects on credit, market and reputational risk channels.

The climate risk register is the central source used to describe the Bank’s climate risk profile and related management activities in this section. Six of the eight recorded climate risks are flagged as integrated into the Bank’s enterprise risk management, indicating that these specific risks are taken into account within broader risk oversight and reporting. Four risks are recorded as having changed since the prior period, showing that the register is updated when assessments are revised.

The register records quarterly or semi-annual monitoring for the identified risks. Five of the eight risks are explicitly linked to climate-related scenario analysis, using four distinct scenario references. For these five risks, forward-looking climate scenarios are used as inputs to assess potential future conditions; these references do not represent realised losses. The scenario analysis supporting these risk-level links is drawn from a broader library of 18 climate-related scenarios based on the NGFS_v4 framework, which includes orderly, disorderly and hot-house world pathways and time horizons to 2025, 2030 and 2050. The existence of this broader library does not imply that all scenarios are applied to every risk; only the risks with explicit links use scenario analysis as part of their assessment.

Mitigation actions are recorded at the level of individual risks and include: climate scenario stress testing integrated into the internal capital adequacy assessment process, collateral revaluation and flood mapping overlays, engagement and transition-plan covenants with borrowers, green retrofit financing incentives, portfolio decarbonisation glide-path monitoring, and sector exposure limits with enhanced due diligence. Where climate scenario stress testing is recorded as integrated into the internal capital adequacy assessment, this indicates a connection between the relevant climate risks and capital planning. The Bank’s reported climate-related key performance indicators, including financed emissions, carbon intensity, high-carbon sector exposure and fossil fuel exposure, provide additional context for transition and reputational risks, particularly those related to financed emissions scrutiny and high-carbon sector exposures.

#### Upstream sustainability risks

For Eurolux Universal Bank AG, upstream sustainability-related risks would typically relate to funding sources, key suppliers, external service providers and access to capital markets. In the 2024 reporting period, the main evidence base for climate-related risk management is the climate risk register, which is focused on risks arising from the Bank’s financing activities, collateral exposures and borrower characteristics, rather than on a separate upstream climate risk taxonomy.

The risks recorded in the register predominantly reflect downstream and balance-sheet exposures, such as mortgage book flood exposure, wildfire exposure in specific portfolios, stranded assets in the fossil fuel sector and transition risks affecting industrial and auto lending. No distinct upstream climate-related risk entries, such as supplier-related climate disruptions or climate-linked constraints on wholesale funding, are recorded in the documentation used for this report. As a result, upstream sustainability-related climate risks are not described as a separate process here, and any upstream implications of climate change are addressed indirectly through the Bank’s broader enterprise risk management and funding strategies rather than through specific climate risk register entries.

#### Risk management across internal operations

Internal operational climate risks for a commercial bank may include physical impacts on branches and offices, data centres and critical infrastructure, as well as transition-related impacts on internal processes, systems and human capital. In the 2024 climate risk register, the physical risks recorded—mortgage book flood exposure, wildfire exposure in the southern portfolio and heat stress affecting agricultural borrowers—are framed through their impact on borrowers, collateral and credit quality rather than on the Bank’s own premises or operational continuity.

The register does not separately record climate-related risks that are purely operational in nature, such as damage to the Bank’s own facilities or disruptions to internal operations. There is also no separate set of recorded mitigation actions that relate exclusively to internal operational climate risks. The mitigation actions in the register focus on credit and portfolio measures, including collateral revaluation and flood mapping overlays, climate scenario stress testing integrated into the internal capital adequacy assessment, and green retrofit financing incentives, which are directed at managing financial exposures.

On this basis, climate risk management across internal operations is reflected indirectly through the Bank’s broader risk management and business continuity arrangements, while the 2024 climate risk register concentrates on risks that affect the Bank’s financial assets and client portfolios.

#### Downstream sustainability risks

Downstream sustainability-related risks are the primary focus of Eurolux Universal Bank AG’s climate risk register, reflecting the Bank’s role as a lender and financial intermediary. The 2024 register shows that material climate-related exposures arise mainly through:

- Lending to sectors and borrowers exposed to transition risks, including technology shifts, policy changes and market re-pricing.
- Collateral values in regions exposed to acute and chronic physical climate hazards.
- Financed emissions and high-carbon sector exposures, which can drive both transition and reputational risks.

Key downstream transition risks recorded in the register include:

- Transition risk from the shift to electric vehicles in auto lending (transition technology, high risk, short-term horizon, indicative financial impact of about EUR 165 million, monitored quarterly). The mitigation action recorded is climate scenario stress testing integrated into the internal capital adequacy assessment, linking this risk to capital planning for the relevant exposures.
- Stranded asset risk related to fossil fuel sector exposure (transition market, high risk, long-term horizon, indicative financial impact of about EUR 137 million, monitored quarterly). The mitigation action recorded is collateral revaluation and flood mapping overlays, used to reassess collateral values and location-specific physical exposures associated with fossil fuel-related assets.
- Transition risk from carbon pricing on industrial loans (transition policy, high risk, medium-term horizon, indicative financial impact of about EUR 68 million, monitored quarterly). This risk has mitigation actions recorded as sector exposure limits and enhanced due diligence applied to industrial lending portfolios.
- Reputational risk from scrutiny of financed emissions (transition reputational, medium risk, medium-term horizon, indicative financial impact of about EUR 66 million, monitored semi-annually). The mitigation action recorded is portfolio decarbonisation glide-path monitoring, applied to track the trajectory of financed emissions in relation to the Bank’s portfolio.

Downstream physical risks are also prominent:

- Physical risk from flood exposure in the mortgage book (acute physical, high risk, long-term horizon, indicative financial impact of about EUR 25 million, monitored quarterly). This risk is linked to climate-related scenario analysis and has mitigation actions recorded as engagement and transition-plan covenants with affected mortgage borrowers.
- Physical risk from wildfire exposure in the southern portfolio (acute physical, medium risk, short-term horizon, indicative financial impact of about EUR 23 million, monitored semi-annually). The mitigation action recorded is collateral revaluation and flood mapping overlays, used to reassess collateral values in wildfire-prone areas.
- Physical risk from heat stress affecting agricultural borrowers (chronic physical, medium risk, short-term horizon, indicative financial impact of about EUR 21 million, monitored semi-annually). This risk has mitigation actions recorded as climate scenario stress testing integrated into the internal capital adequacy assessment, applied to assess potential impacts on agricultural borrowers and related credit exposures.

The Bank’s climate-related indicators—financed emissions of approximately 36 million tonnes of carbon dioxide equivalent, a carbon intensity of about 1,155 tonnes of carbon dioxide equivalent per million euro, high-carbon sector exposure of 35.5% and fossil fuel exposure of 22.15%—provide contextual information for these downstream risks. They are particularly relevant to transition market and reputational risks associated with fossil fuel sector exposure and financed emissions scrutiny and are used as monitoring context rather than as formal risk appetite thresholds.

#### Climate risk management

Climate risk management at Eurolux Universal Bank AG, as presented in this report, is based on the structure and content of the 2024 climate risk register and its links to climate-related scenario analysis and enterprise risk management. The register covers eight climate-related risks, split into three physical risks and five transition risks, and records a consistent set of attributes for each risk:

- Risk category: physical acute, physical chronic, transition market, transition policy, transition reputational or transition technology.
- Risk rating: high or medium.
- Time horizon: short term (0–2 years), medium term (2–5 years) or long term (more than 5 years).
- Indicative financial impact, expressed in million euro.
- Monitoring frequency: quarterly or semi-annual.
- Link to climate-related scenario analysis, where applicable.
- Mitigation actions.
- Indicator of integration into enterprise risk management.
- Indicator of whether the risk has changed since the prior period.

The register records both physical and transition risks, capturing drivers such as extreme weather events, chronic climate trends, regulatory changes, technology shifts, market re-pricing and reputational pressures. The three physical risks recorded relate to flood exposure in the mortgage book, wildfire exposure in the southern portfolio and heat stress on agricultural borrowers. The five transition risks recorded relate to the shift to electric vehicles in auto lending, stranded assets in the fossil fuel sector, carbon pricing on industrial loans, building energy performance regulation and scrutiny of financed emissions.

The register assigns risk ratings and indicative financial impact estimates to each risk. High-rated risks with larger indicative financial impacts, such as the shift to electric vehicles in auto lending (about EUR 165 million) and stranded fossil fuel assets (about EUR 137 million), are recorded as monitored quarterly and, in most cases, flagged as integrated into enterprise risk management. Medium-rated risks, such as building energy performance regulation and heat stress on agricultural borrowers, are recorded with semi-annual monitoring frequencies and their respective time horizons. Four of the eight risks are recorded as having changed since the prior period, indicating that the recorded assessments have been updated for these risks.

Monitoring arrangements are captured through the monitoring frequencies and scenario links recorded in the register. Quarterly monitoring is recorded for the shift to electric vehicles in auto lending, stranded fossil fuel assets, carbon pricing on industrial loans and mortgage book flood exposure. Semi-annual monitoring is recorded for wildfire exposure, building energy performance regulation, financed emissions scrutiny and heat stress on agricultural borrowers. Five of the eight risks are linked to climate-related scenario analysis through four distinct scenario references. These risk-level links draw on a broader set of 18 climate scenarios based on the NGFS_v4 framework, which includes orderly, disorderly and hot-house world pathways and time horizons to 2025, 2030 and 2050. Selected risks are linked to these scenarios to support forward-looking assessment of potential climate-related impacts; the scenario outputs are modelled estimates and not records of actual losses, and the broader scenario library is not applied uniformly to all risks.

Risk management and mitigation actions are recorded at the individual risk level:

- For the shift to electric vehicles in auto lending and heat stress on agricultural borrowers, the mitigation action recorded is climate scenario stress testing integrated into the internal capital adequacy assessment, applied to these specific risks.
- For stranded fossil fuel sector exposure and wildfire exposure in the southern portfolio, the mitigation action recorded is collateral revaluation and flood mapping overlays, used to reassess collateral values and location-specific physical risk for these exposures.
- For mortgage book flood exposure, the mitigation action recorded is engagement and transition-plan covenants with borrowers in the mortgage portfolio.
- For building energy performance regulation, the mitigation action recorded is green retrofit financing incentives, used to support borrowers in improving building energy performance.
- For financed emissions scrutiny, the mitigation action recorded is portfolio decarbonisation glide-path monitoring, applied to monitor the trajectory of financed emissions in relation to the Bank’s portfolio.
- For carbon pricing on industrial loans, the mitigation actions recorded are sector exposure limits and enhanced due diligence, applied to industrial lending portfolios.

Integration into overall risk management is indicated by the enterprise risk management integration flag in the register. Six of the eight climate risks, representing 75% of the recorded climate risks, are marked as integrated into enterprise risk management, indicating that these specific risks are taken into account within the Bank’s broader risk oversight processes. This includes, for certain risks, the use of climate scenario stress testing integrated into the internal capital adequacy assessment as a mitigation action, which links climate-related scenario analysis for those risks to capital planning. The remaining two risks are not flagged as integrated, so integration is partial and focused on selected risks.

The climate risk register and associated scenario links therefore provide the primary basis for describing how climate-related risks are identified, categorised, rated, monitored and managed, and the extent to which these recorded risks inform the Bank’s overall risk management.

#### Risk management limitations and evidence boundaries

The climate risk management disclosures for Eurolux Universal Bank AG in this report are based on the 2024 climate risk register, the associated climate-related scenario information and selected climate-related key performance indicators. While these sources provide structured evidence of recorded risk identification, ratings, monitoring frequencies, mitigation actions and partial integration into enterprise risk management, there are important limitations.

First, formal climate risk appetite thresholds, detailed risk policy documents specific to climate risk, and formal escalation thresholds or triggers are not evidenced in the available documentation. As a result, this section does not describe quantitative climate risk appetite limits, formal escalation routes or detailed policy frameworks for climate risk.

Second, detailed descriptions of formal controls and procedures, including step-by-step processes for incorporating climate-related factors into credit underwriting, loan pricing, collateral management, provisioning or expected credit loss estimation, are not available in the source data. The mitigation actions described—such as collateral revaluation and flood mapping overlays, climate scenario stress testing integrated into the internal capital adequacy assessment, engagement and transition-plan covenants with borrowers, green retrofit financing incentives, portfolio decarbonisation glide-path monitoring and sector exposure limits with enhanced due diligence—are therefore presented as risk-specific actions recorded in the register, rather than as a complete set of formal control activities or as evidence of systematic incorporation into all credit decision-making processes.

Third, the value chain perspective in this section is constrained by the focus of the climate risk register on downstream lending and collateral exposures. There is limited evidence on distinct upstream climate-related risk processes (for suppliers, service providers or funding sources) and on internal operational climate risks that affect the Bank’s own facilities and operations. Consequently, upstream and internal operational climate risk management are described at a high level, and the primary emphasis is on downstream risks arising from lending, financed emissions and sector exposures.

Finally, climate-related scenario analysis is used as a forward-looking input for selected risks, with five of the eight risks linked to four distinct scenario references. The underlying scenarios are based on the NGFS_v4 framework and represent modelled estimates of potential future climate and transition pathways. These modelled outputs are subject to uncertainty and should not be interpreted as predictions or as evidence of actual losses. The Bank may refine its methodologies, data sources and scenario selection over time, which could affect the comparability of future disclosures with those presented for 2024.
