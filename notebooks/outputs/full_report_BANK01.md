### General Requirements

#### Understanding the Bank’s approach towards implementing IFRS S1 and IFRS S2 requirements

This report has been prepared by Eurolux Universal Bank AG as a dedicated sustainability-related and climate-related financial disclosure, structured in line with the core concepts and architecture of IFRS S1 and IFRS S2. The report focuses on the sustainability-related and climate-related risks and opportunities that could reasonably be expected to affect the Bank’s prospects, including its cash flows, access to finance and cost of capital over the short, medium and long term.

The disclosure is organised to reflect the four core thematic areas commonly associated with IFRS Sustainability Disclosure Standards: Governance, Strategy, Risk management, and Metrics and targets. These thematic sections are preceded by this General Requirements section, which sets out the basis of preparation, reporting boundaries and materiality approach for the sustainability-related financial disclosures.

The primary purpose of this report is to provide current and potential investors, creditors and other users of general purpose financial reports with decision-useful information on how sustainability-related and climate-related factors interact with Eurolux Universal Bank AG’s business model and value chain, and how these factors may influence the Bank’s financial position and performance over relevant time horizons. The report is intended to be read in conjunction with the Bank’s related financial statements and other public disclosures in order to obtain a more complete view of the Bank’s overall risk profile and strategy.

The approach described in this report represents an alignment with the principles and disclosure objectives of IFRS S1 and IFRS S2, taking into account the European sustainability reporting environment and sustainability reporting requirements that may apply to banks operating in Europe. The Bank expects that its approach to sustainability-related financial reporting will continue to evolve in line with emerging regulatory expectations, market practices and improvements in data quality and methodologies.

Parts of the report rely on modelled estimates, internally generated analytical outputs and available source data, particularly for climate scenario analysis and financed emissions.

#### Fair presentation

Eurolux Universal Bank AG aims to present its sustainability-related and climate-related disclosures in a manner that fairly reflects the material sustainability-related risks and opportunities that could reasonably be expected to affect the Bank’s prospects. In this context, fair presentation is understood as providing information that is balanced and transparent about methodologies, estimates and known limitations, rather than asserting completeness or the absence of material error.

The information in this report has been prepared using the following principles:

- reliance on the available internal documentation, risk registers, exposure mapping and climate scenario outputs relevant to the reporting entity;
- use of quantitative indicators where underlying data are available, complemented by qualitative disclosures where data or methodologies are still developing;
- consistent use, to the extent possible, of the Bank’s reporting currency (euro, EUR) for monetary amounts to align with the unit of measure used in the related financial statements; and
- application of internal definitions of short term, medium term and long term that are consistent with the Bank’s strategic planning horizons, as further described in the Strategy and Risk management sections.

Where data, methodologies or internal records are incomplete or still maturing, this report may not fully satisfy all attributes of completeness, neutrality and freedom from material error as envisaged in IFRS Sustainability Disclosure Standards. In particular:

- detailed methodological descriptions for some metrics and targets, including certain financed emissions indicators, are not available in the current evidence base;
- operational greenhouse gas emissions data (for Scope 1, Scope 2 and relevant Scope 3 categories) are not comprehensively documented in the available records; and
- organisational consolidation boundaries and explicit alignment with the boundaries used in the Bank’s financial statements are not evidenced and are therefore not asserted.

The Bank does not claim external assurance over the entirety of this report. Available evidence indicates that independent assurance, where obtained, relates only to selected components such as Scope 1 and Scope 2 greenhouse gas emissions. Assurance over financed emissions or other sustainability metrics is not asserted.

Given these factors, user judgement is required when interpreting trend analyses and comparing this information with other institutions’ disclosures or with the Bank’s future reporting, especially as methodologies, data sources and regulatory expectations change over time.

#### Materiality assessment

The sustainability-related and climate-related disclosures in this report are based on a materiality approach that is primarily risk- and portfolio-driven. Rather than being derived from a formal stakeholder materiality survey, the Bank’s approach reflects the information currently available in internal risk registers, exposure mapping and climate scenario outputs.

Key elements of this approach include:

- Identification of sustainability-related and climate-related risk drivers: the Bank uses its climate risk register and available scenario analysis to identify transition risks (for example, policy, legal and market changes affecting sectors such as mining, refined petroleum, and air transport) and physical risks (for example, physical chronic risk affecting certain sectors and own operations).
- Portfolio and value chain screening: exposures within the Bank’s portfolio and value chain are mapped using sector classifications (including NACE codes) and financial exposure data. Nodes with higher financial exposure and identified climate risk characteristics, such as lending to mining, refined petroleum, energy-intensive manufacturing, transport and selected services sectors, are considered priority areas for disclosure.
- Risk and opportunity linkage to financial effects: where supported by available evidence, the Bank considers the potential for identified risks and opportunities to influence credit risk, market risk, operational risk and business model resilience, taking into account relevant time horizons (short term, medium term and long term) and the Bank’s strategic planning cycles.
- Metrics and targets focus: metrics and targets are selected where they are directly linked to identified risk concentrations, transition or physical risk exposures and key aspects of the Bank’s climate-related strategy, including financed emissions and sectoral exposure indicators.

Due to evidence boundaries, the materiality approach described here does not incorporate a structured, survey-based assessment of stakeholder priorities, and no specific stakeholder groups or consultation processes are asserted. Instead, material topics reflected in this report have been derived from:

- the available climate risk register and risk management documentation;
- sectoral exposure and financed emissions data;
- scenario analysis outputs and related internal assessments; and
- governance and management information regarding the oversight of sustainability-related and climate-related risks and opportunities.

As the Bank’s data, methodologies and governance processes continue to develop, the materiality approach may be further refined, including potential integration of more formalised stakeholder input, enhanced quantitative thresholds and more granular assessment of emerging sustainability-related topics, subject to available evidence and internal governance approval.

### Governance

#### Overview

For the year ended 31 December 2024, Eurolux Universal Bank AG’s climate-related governance is organised across the Board of Directors, the ESG & Sustainability Committee, executive management and supporting control processes. Based on available evidence, climate-related governance is reflected in Board oversight, ESG & Sustainability Committee activity, executive management responsibility, semi-annual Board reporting, ERM integration indicators, climate checks for major transactions and a climate risk register.

The Board has overall responsibility for overseeing climate-related risks and opportunities as part of the bank’s business strategy and risk profile. Oversight is reflected in the proportion of Board meetings at which climate appears on the agenda, semi-annual climate risk reporting and documented 2024 Board and committee decisions.

At committee level, the ESG & Sustainability Committee provides more detailed oversight of climate-related matters, including review of progress against climate-related objectives and monitoring of key climate-related risks and associated management actions. Management responsibility for identifying, assessing and managing climate-related risks and opportunities is assigned to the Climate Risk Management Committee, which reports to the Board on a semi-annual basis.

Supporting processes are reflected in ERM integration indicators, climate risk reporting to the Board and the use of climate checks for major transactions. The bank uses a climate risk register that, in 2024, recorded 8 climate-related risks with defined categories, ratings, time horizons and mitigation actions. Board-level climate expertise, a skills development programme and the inclusion of climate and ESG-related metrics in executive remuneration further anchor accountability for climate-related matters.

#### The role of the Board of Directors

The Board of Directors of Eurolux Universal Bank AG comprises 10 members, of whom 68.5% are recorded as independent for 2024. Source data indicates that climate-related matters are considered within the Board’s wider responsibilities for strategy, risk appetite and oversight of the bank’s performance and resilience.

In 2024, the Board held 8 meetings (2023: 7; 2022: 4). Climate-related topics appeared on the agenda at 72.6% of these meetings (2023: 73.1%; 2022: 69.8%), indicating regular Board-level engagement with climate-related risks and opportunities. Board oversight of climate-related matters is evidenced through:

The Board also oversees progress against climate-related objectives and targets, including consideration of related performance metrics within remuneration policies. This is reflected in the inclusion of ESG and climate-related metrics in CEO and executive remuneration and in the Board’s consideration of recommendations from the ESG & Sustainability Committee on climate-related matters.

#### Management responsibility for climate-related risks and opportunities

In 2024, management responsibility for climate-related risks and opportunities is assigned to the Climate Risk Management Committee. This management-level committee is responsible for coordinating the identification, assessment, monitoring and management of climate-related risks across the bank, and for preparing climate risk information for semi-annual reporting to the Board.

The Climate Risk Management Committee uses several key mechanisms:

- Climate risk register: The bank uses a climate risk register that, in 2024, recorded 8 climate-related risks. These risks are classified across physical acute, physical chronic, transition market, transition policy, transition reputational and transition technology categories. Each risk is assigned a rating (high or medium), a time horizon (short 0–2 years, medium 2–5 years or long 5+ years) and an associated monitoring frequency, typically quarterly or semi-annual.
- ERM integration: Source data indicates that 6 of the 8 recorded climate-related risks are integrated into the bank’s enterprise risk management framework. For these risks, ERM integration indicators show inclusion within group risk reporting and consideration in capital and liquidity planning processes.
- Scenario analysis identifiers: For selected climate-related risks, the risk register records scenario analysis identifiers that link individual risks to particular climate scenarios. These identifiers provide a reference between risk assessments and the bank’s climate scenario analysis work, without evidencing additional internal assessment processes beyond those identifiers.
- Mitigation actions: For each risk, the register records high-level mitigation actions. For 2024, examples of actions associated with risks in the register include:
 - integrating climate scenario stress testing into the Internal Capital Adequacy Assessment Process (ICAAP);
 - applying collateral revaluation with flood mapping overlays for relevant portfolios;
 - incorporating engagement and transition-plan covenants with borrowers;
 - offering green retrofit financing incentives;
 - monitoring portfolio decarbonisation glide paths; and
 - applying sector exposure limits and enhanced due diligence in selected sectors.
- Monitoring frequency and changes: The risk register records monitoring frequencies at quarterly or semi-annual intervals, and source data shows that 4 of the 8 risks recorded were updated or changed since the prior period, reflecting ongoing management attention.

The Climate Risk Management Committee prepares semi-annual reports for the Board summarising key developments in climate-related risks, changes in risk ratings, mitigation progress and ERM integration indicators. These reports draw on the climate risk register and are a primary channel through which the Board is informed about climate-related risk exposures and management responses.

Climate-related considerations in major transactions are reflected through climate checks for such transactions. Source data indicates that these checks are applied and are considered by management when presenting major transactions to the Board or relevant committees. Documentation reviewed does not evidence formal, quantitative escalation thresholds or detailed trigger-based mechanics for when climate issues must be escalated, beyond their capture in the climate risk register and the general requirement to consider climate factors in significant transaction approvals.

Across the period 2022–2024, different management committees are recorded as having responsibility for ESG or climate-related topics (Group Sustainability Committee in 2022, ESG Executive Committee in 2023 and Climate Risk Management Committee in 2024). Source data records these names by year but does not evidence continuity, formal renaming or a direct evolution between these committees.

### Strategy

#### Overview

Eurolux Universal Bank AG’s climate-related strategy is structured to manage material physical and transition risks across its value chain, capture defined climate-related financing opportunities, and align portfolio steering with its transition and net zero ambitions. The strategy integrates risk identification from the climate risk register with sector-level portfolio analysis, high-carbon and fossil-fuel exposure metrics, and the expansion of green and sustainability-linked products.

This architecture applies a value-chain perspective encompassing upstream funding and suppliers, own operations, and downstream financing counterparties. It supports the incorporation of climate factors into credit analysis, sector exposure management, and client engagement, as well as resource allocation decisions evidenced by dedicated climate-related capital and operating expenditure. Climate-related targets—including a 2030 absolute reduction target for Scope 1 and 2 emissions aligned to an SBTi 1.5°C pathway framework per target summary, a financed emissions intensity objective, and a 2050 net zero goal—frame the Bank’s longer-term transition planning. Scenario analysis based on NGFS v4 orderly, disorderly, and hot house scenarios informs strategic decision-making by quantifying modelled portfolio losses, stranded asset risks, and revenue at risk across short-, medium-, and long-term horizons.

#### Sustainability risks and opportunities across the value chain

The 2024 climate risk register identifies eight material climate-related risks: three physical and five transition risks, with ratings from medium to high and time horizons spanning short term (0–2 years), medium term (2–5 years), and long term (more than 5 years). Key physical risks include flood exposure in the mortgage portfolio and wildfire exposure in the southern lending book, both classified as acute physical risks with high or medium risk ratings and potential impacts up to EUR 24.8 million. These risks principally affect downstream retail and corporate lending, as well as collateral values in flood- and fire-prone regions.

Transition risks are concentrated in carbon-intensive corporate segments. High-priority items include technology-driven transition risk from the shift to electric vehicles in auto lending (short term, modelled impact EUR 165.2 million), market-driven stranded asset risk in fossil fuel sector exposure (long term, EUR 136.5 million), and policy-driven carbon pricing impacts on industrial loans (medium term, EUR 67.5 million). Reputational risk from scrutiny of financed emissions is rated medium with a potential impact of EUR 65.5 million. These risks are embedded in a portfolio with 35.5% exposure to high-carbon sectors (EUR 11,059.49 million) and 22.15% exposure to fossil fuel-related activities (EUR 6,899.59 million), highlighting concentration in downstream financing counterparties.

Risks and opportunities are concentrated in specific value-chain nodes. The largest quantified downstream exposures include: Manufacture of coke and refined petroleum (EUR 3,903.65 million), Manufacture of motor vehicles (EUR 3,871.6 million), Mining of coal and lignite (EUR 3,547.58 million), Education (EUR 2,962.25 million), Mining of metal ores (EUR 2,749.58 million), and Other mining and quarrying (EUR 2,102.44 million). Own operations (buildings, fleet, and business travel) face chronic physical risk and transition pressures linked to operational emissions. Upstream suppliers are affected through evolving expectations on data and environmental performance, though some nodes are assessed qualitatively rather than quantitatively.

The Bank has identified climate-related opportunities, mainly in downstream client financing and advisory, with estimated potential revenue impacts of EUR 465.0 million over varying time horizons. These are treated as estimates with medium confidence, not assured benefits.

#### Strategic management of climate-related risks and opportunities

Eurolux Universal Bank AG manages climate-related risks and opportunities through integrated portfolio steering, engagement with clients in carbon-intensive sectors, and development of a differentiated green and transition finance offering.  - Scope and coverage: All relevant scopes (Scopes 1, 2 and 3), covering all Kyoto greenhouse gases for the whole entity. The target is recorded as being set on a net basis and is associated in the target record with the operational gross reduction target described above.
 - Metric and baseline: Absolute tonnes of CO₂ equivalent, with a baseline year of 2020 and a baseline parameter recorded at 120,000 tonnes of CO₂ equivalent.
 - Target and time horizon: A 100% reduction relative to the 2020 baseline parameter by 2050, recorded as a net‑zero objective.

The target records also include review frequencies: triennial for the operational emissions target, annual for the financed emissions intensity target and biennial for the net‑zero target. The Bank uses these targets and associated progress indicators to guide its strategic planning, to inform its assessment of climate‑related risks and opportunities, and to support internal monitoring of transition pathways. However, the presence of third‑party validation fields in the target records pertains to the design and framing of the targets and does not constitute verification of current‑year emissions, target progress, alignment with climate pathways, or the likelihood of future target achievement.

#### Metrics and targets limitations and evidence boundaries

The metrics and targets disclosed in this section are subject to several limitations arising from data availability, methodology documentation and verification status:

- Operational greenhouse gas emissions: Detailed records for 2024 Scope 1, Scope 2 and Scope 3 emissions are not available in the source data. As a result, the Bank does not report quantified operational emissions for the current year, and the operational emissions baselines and milestones described in the climate targets section are target‑record parameters only and not current‑year emissions.
- Financed emissions methodology: While absolute financed emissions and portfolio carbon intensity have been reported for 2024, detailed calculation methodology, data quality assessments, attribution choices, sectoral breakdowns and underlying assumptions are not described in the available evidence. Users of this report should therefore be cautious in making detailed comparisons with other institutions or with future reporting periods that may adopt updated methods.
- Portfolio exposure metrics: High‑carbon and fossil‑fuel exposure figures are based on internal classifications and sector definitions. The precise sectoral mapping, thresholds and criteria used to define “high‑carbon sectors”, “fossil‑fuel exposure” and “green loans” are not fully described in the available documentation and may evolve over time.
- Verification status: The 2024 metrics presented in this section, including financed emissions, carbon intensity, portfolio exposures, green loans, and climate‑related capital and operating expenditure, have not been independently verified. Any references to third‑party validation relate solely to target‑record attributes for the design of climate targets and do not extend to current‑year emissions, portfolio metrics, target progress indicators, characterisation of climate pathway alignment or the likelihood of future target achievement.
- Methodology records: Detailed methodology records and data quality documentation for the calculation of each metric, including specific models, data sources, estimation techniques and limitations, are not available in the source data. Consequently, the Bank is unable in this report to provide granular disclosures on methods and assumptions beyond the high‑level descriptions included above.

The Bank recognises these limitations and intends to continue enhancing its climate‑related metrics and targets, including improving data coverage, refining definitions and methodologies, and strengthening internal controls over climate‑related data, so that future disclosures can provide more comprehensive and comparable information to users.
