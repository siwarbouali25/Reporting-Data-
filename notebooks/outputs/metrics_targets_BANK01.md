### Metrics and targets

#### Overview

For the financial year 2024, Eurolux Universal Bank AG uses a set of quantitative climate-related metrics and targets to monitor material climate risks and opportunities across its balance sheet and operations. These metrics focus on portfolio-financed emissions, exposure to high‑carbon and fossil‑fuel sectors, the growth of green lending, and climate-related capital and operating expenditure. They are complemented by medium- and long-term greenhouse gas reduction targets that cover both operational and financed emissions.

The metrics and targets disclosed in this section are intended to support an integrated view of how climate-related risks and opportunities affect, and are expected to affect, the Bank’s financial position, performance and cash flows. They also provide a basis for monitoring progress against the Bank’s stated climate ambitions and for informing strategic decisions on capital allocation, risk appetite and product development. Connections to governance, strategy and risk management are described in the relevant sections of this report; this section focuses on the quantitative indicators and target parameters used for monitoring.

#### Sustainability-related metrics and targets

Eurolux Universal Bank AG has identified a core set of climate-related key performance indicators that are used internally to assess and manage climate-related risks and opportunities and that are reported externally for 2024. These indicators are primarily portfolio-based, reflecting the Bank’s role as a commercial lender and financial intermediary, and are expressed in relation to the Bank’s total assets and loan book.

Key 2024 climate-related metrics are as follows:

- Total assets: EUR 850,000 million.
- Total loans: EUR 31,150.64 million.

High‑carbon and fossil‑fuel exposure metrics:

- Exposure to high‑carbon sectors: EUR 11,059.49 million, representing 35.5% of the total loan portfolio.
- Exposure to fossil‑fuel activities: EUR 6,899.59 million, representing 22.15% of the total loan portfolio.

Green lending and transition-support metrics:

- Green loans: EUR 3,335.75 million, representing 10.71% of the total loan portfolio.

These exposure and green lending metrics are used to monitor the relative weight of climate‑sensitive sectors in the portfolio, to inform credit risk analysis and sector strategies, and to track the reallocation of financing towards lower‑carbon and transition‑enabling activities over time.

In addition, the Bank tracks climate-related capital and operating expenditure as indicators of resource allocation to climate risk management, decarbonisation and transition-enabling initiatives:

- Climate-related capital expenditure in 2024: EUR 476.95 million.
- Climate-related operating expenditure in 2024: EUR 219.32 million.

These expenditure metrics are used to monitor the scale of investment in climate-related initiatives, including internal decarbonisation projects, data and systems enhancements, and the development of climate-related products and services. They also inform the Bank’s assessment of the current and anticipated financial effects of climate-related risks and opportunities, including the integration of climate considerations into financial planning and investment decisions.

The Bank’s climate-related targets, which are described in detail in the “Climate targets and progress” subsection, are linked to these metrics through absolute and intensity-based greenhouse gas reduction objectives for operational and financed emissions, as well as a long-term net‑zero ambition.

#### Greenhouse gas emissions

For the year ended 31 December 2024, detailed operational greenhouse gas emissions records for Scope 1, Scope 2 and Scope 3 are unavailable. As a result, Eurolux Universal Bank AG does not report quantified 2024 operational emissions in this section and instead focuses on portfolio-financed emissions and related intensity metrics as the primary quantitative indicators of climate-related risk and opportunity.

#### Managing exposure towards financed emissions

Given the nature of its business, Eurolux Universal Bank AG considers financed emissions to be a central metric for assessing its exposure to climate-related transition risk and for monitoring progress against its portfolio decarbonisation objectives.

For 2024, source data indicate:

- Total financed emissions associated with the loan portfolio: 35,973,167.69 tCO₂e.
- Total loans: EUR 31,150.64 million.
- Portfolio carbon intensity: 1,154.81 tCO₂e per EUR million of lending.

These figures represent the Bank’s reported estimate of greenhouse gas emissions associated with its lending activities for the year, expressed both in absolute terms and as an intensity metric relative to the size of the loan book. The intensity metric is used internally to monitor changes in the emissions profile of the portfolio over time, taking into account growth or contraction in lending volumes, and to assess progress against the Bank’s financed emissions intensity reduction target described below.

The financed emissions metrics are used in several ways:

- To inform sector and client-level credit analysis, particularly in high‑emitting industries, by providing an additional lens on transition risk alongside traditional credit risk parameters.
- To support the setting and monitoring of portfolio-level decarbonisation targets, including the Bank’s intensity-based target for financed emissions.
- To guide engagement with clients in high‑emitting sectors, including discussions on transition plans, capital expenditure programmes and resilience to evolving climate policies and market dynamics.
- To support internal scenario analysis and stress testing, where financed emissions and portfolio intensity are used as inputs to assess potential impacts of climate-related policy, technology and market shifts on credit quality and expected loss.

The 2024 financed emissions and intensity figures have not been independently verified. Detailed calculation methodology, including data sources, estimation techniques and any sectoral or asset-class exclusions, is not described in the available documentation and is therefore not disclosed in this section.

#### Climate-related financial metrics and resource allocation

Eurolux Universal Bank AG uses a set of climate-related financial metrics to assess how climate-related risks and opportunities are influencing its financial position, performance and resource allocation decisions.

High‑carbon and fossil‑fuel exposure

The Bank monitors its exposure to sectors and activities that are particularly sensitive to transition risk:

- High‑carbon sector exposure: EUR 11,059.49 million, representing 35.5% of the total loan portfolio.
- Fossil‑fuel exposure: EUR 6,899.59 million, representing 22.15% of the total loan portfolio.

These metrics are used to assess concentration risk in sectors that may be affected by changes in climate policy, technology and market preferences, and to inform risk appetite, sector limits and client engagement strategies. They also provide a basis for evaluating the potential impact of climate-related risks on expected credit losses, including through their influence on probability of default, exposure at default and loss given default for borrowers in affected sectors.

Green lending and transition finance

To capture the financial effects of climate-related opportunities and the Bank’s role in supporting the transition to a lower‑carbon economy, the following metrics are tracked:

- Green loans: EUR 3,335.75 million, representing 10.71% of the total loan portfolio.

This metric reflects lending that is classified by the Bank as green, based on internal criteria and/or applicable regulatory or market taxonomies, and is used to monitor the growth of products and services that support climate mitigation, adaptation and other environmental objectives. It also informs strategic planning for new business areas and product development.

Climate-related capital and operating expenditure

The Bank’s internal investment in climate-related initiatives is reflected in:

- Climate-related capital expenditure: EUR 476.95 million in 2024.
- Climate-related operating expenditure: EUR 219.32 million in 2024.

These amounts represent reported expenditure on projects and activities that support the management of climate-related risks and the pursuit of climate-related opportunities, including investments in data, systems, processes, and internal decarbonisation measures. They are used to assess the scale of resources allocated to climate-related priorities and to inform future investment and funding plans.

The financial metrics described in this subsection have not been independently verified. Detailed methodologies for classifying exposures as high‑carbon, fossil‑fuel or green, and for identifying climate-related capital and operating expenditure, are not described in the available documentation and are therefore not disclosed here.

#### Climate targets and progress

Eurolux Universal Bank AG has established a suite of climate-related targets that cover operational emissions, financed emissions and a long-term net‑zero ambition. The targets are expressed using absolute and intensity-based greenhouse gas metrics and are intended to guide the Bank’s transition pathway over the short, medium and long term. All target parameters described below are taken from internal target records and are presented as target-record parameters rather than as current-year operational emissions or verified performance outcomes.

For transparency, the principal targets can be summarised as follows:

- Target 1 – Operational emissions reduction (Scope 1 and 2):
  - Metric and scope: absolute greenhouse gas emissions in tonnes of CO₂ equivalent, covering Scope 1 and Scope 2 emissions.
  - Baseline and target: the target record specifies a 2020 baseline of 137,849 tCO₂e and a 2030 target of a 42% reduction in these emissions on a gross basis, covering all Kyoto greenhouse gases and applying to the whole entity.
  - Interim milestones: the target record includes interim absolute emissions parameters of 120,480 tCO₂e for 2025 and 97,321.4 tCO₂e for 2028, expressed as target-record milestones rather than forecast operational emissions.
  - Framework and validation: the target record identifies the SBTi 1.5°C pathway as the target framework. The same record indicates that third‑party validation is recorded, with SBTi identified as the validation body.
  - Progress indicator: the target record uses absolute tCO₂e as the progress metric and reports 40.0% progress as at 2024. This percentage is an internal target-record indicator and has not been independently verified.

- Target 2 – Financed emissions intensity reduction (Scope 3, category 15 – financed emissions):
  - Metric and scope: greenhouse gas emissions intensity in tonnes of CO₂ equivalent per EUR million of lending, covering Scope 3 category 15 financed emissions.
  - Baseline and target: the target record specifies a 2022 baseline intensity of 20.0 tCO₂e per EUR million of lending and a 2030 target of a 30% reduction in this intensity, covering all Kyoto greenhouse gases and applying to the whole entity.
  - Interim milestones: the target record includes interim intensity parameters of 18.2 tCO₂e per EUR million of lending for 2025 and 15.8 tCO₂e per EUR million of lending for 2028, expressed as target-record milestones.
  - Framework and validation: the target record identifies the Net‑Zero Banking Alliance as the target framework. The same record indicates that third‑party validation is recorded, with UNEP FI identified as the validation body.
  - Progress indicator: the target record uses tCO₂e per EUR million as the progress metric and reports 25.0% progress as at 2024. This percentage is an internal target-record indicator and has not been independently verified.

- Target 3 – Long-term net‑zero greenhouse gas emissions:
  - Metric and scope: net greenhouse gas emissions in tonnes of CO₂ equivalent, covering all scopes and all Kyoto greenhouse gases, applying to the whole entity.
  - Baseline and target: the target record specifies a 2020 baseline of 120,000 tCO₂e and a 2050 target of a 100% reduction in net emissions. The target is recorded as a net target and is associated in the target record with the gross operational emissions reduction target described above.
  - Framework and validation: the target record identifies the Net‑Zero Banking Alliance as the target framework. The same record indicates that third‑party validation is recorded, with UNEP FI identified as the validation body.
  - Use of carbon credits: the target record notes a planned use of carbon credits equivalent to 8.2% of the reduction, described as technology-based removal credits. This parameter is recorded at target level and does not represent a current commitment to procure specific volumes of credits in any given year.
  - Progress indicator: the target record uses percentage reduction versus baseline as the progress metric and reports 13.3% progress as at 2024. This percentage is an internal target-record indicator and has not been independently verified.

For ease of reference, the main target parameters can be summarised as:

- Operational Scope 1 and 2 absolute emissions reduction: 42% reduction by 2030 from a 2020 baseline of 137,849 tCO₂e, with interim target-record milestones for 2025 and 2028, using absolute tCO₂e as the progress metric, and with the SBTi 1.5°C pathway identified as the framework.
- Financed emissions intensity reduction: 30% reduction in tCO₂e per EUR million of lending by 2030 from a 2022 baseline of 20.0 tCO₂e per EUR million, with interim target-record milestones for 2025 and 2028, and with the Net‑Zero Banking Alliance identified as the framework.
- Net‑zero emissions: 100% reduction in net emissions across all scopes by 2050 from a 2020 baseline of 120,000 tCO₂e, with the Net‑Zero Banking Alliance identified as the framework and a recorded planned use of technology-based removal credits.

The target records also indicate review frequencies (triennial for the operational target, annual for the financed emissions intensity target and biennial for the net‑zero target). These review frequencies describe how often the Bank expects to revisit the target parameters. The target records include internal fields indicating that the targets are considered consistent with a temperature pathway; these fields are presented here as attributes of the target records and do not constitute an external assessment of pathway alignment.

The third‑party validation information described above is limited to the target records themselves. It does not extend to the Bank’s 2024 emissions metrics, to the reported progress percentages, or to any assessment of future target achievement. The Bank’s 2024 emissions and financial metrics, and the progress indicators reported against these targets, have not been independently verified.

Available documentation for this section does not describe which governance body oversees the setting of these targets, who the internal owners are, or whether and how related performance metrics are linked to remuneration. As a result, this section does not make statements about target oversight or remuneration linkage.

#### Metrics and targets limitations and evidence boundaries

The metrics and targets disclosed in this section are subject to the following limitations and evidence boundaries:

- Operational greenhouse gas emissions: detailed 2024 operational Scope 1, Scope 2 and Scope 3 emissions records are unavailable. Consequently, no quantified operational emissions are reported for 2024, and the operational emissions baseline and milestone values disclosed in the target descriptions are target-record parameters only and should not be interpreted as reported 2024 operational emissions.
- Financed emissions methodology: while 2024 financed emissions and portfolio carbon intensity figures are reported, detailed calculation methodology, data quality assessments, sector or asset-class coverage, and key assumptions are not described in the available documentation. Users of this report should therefore treat these figures as indicative estimates rather than fully specified, methodologically documented metrics.
- Exposure and green lending classifications: the criteria and taxonomies used to classify exposures as high‑carbon, fossil‑fuel or green, and to identify climate-related capital and operating expenditure, are not detailed in the available documentation. As a result, comparability with other institutions’ disclosures may be limited.
- Verification status: the 2024 metrics presented in this section, including financed emissions, carbon intensity, exposure metrics, green loans, and climate-related capital and operating expenditure, have not been independently verified. Progress percentages reported against the climate targets are internal target-record indicators and have not been independently verified.
- Target-record attributes: all baseline values, target values, interim milestones, progress percentages, framework references, review frequencies, and information on third‑party validation and planned use of carbon credits are taken from internal target records. These attributes describe how the Bank has defined its targets; they do not constitute verification of current-year emissions, confirmation of pathway alignment, or assurance that the targets will be met.
- Methodology and data quality: detailed information on the methods used to calculate the disclosed metrics, including data sources, estimation techniques, limitations and significant assumptions, is not provided in the available documentation. This may affect the ability of users to fully assess data quality and to compare the Bank’s metrics with those of other institutions or with future reporting periods.

Eurolux Universal Bank AG expects that, over time, enhancements to data availability, internal systems and methodologies will support more granular, comparable and methodologically transparent climate-related metrics and targets disclosures.
