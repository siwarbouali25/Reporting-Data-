## Executive Summary

This sustainability and climate disclosure report presents Eurolux Universal Bank AG’s (“the Bank”) approach to identifying, managing and reporting sustainability-related and climate-related risks and opportunities that could reasonably be expected to affect its cash flows, access to finance or cost of capital. The report is structured around Governance, Strategy, Risk management, and Metrics and targets, drawing on IFRS S1 and IFRS S2 as guiding frameworks. It is prepared on the basis of available documentation and data for the Bank, supplemented in some areas by structured analytics, and does not purport to represent full legal or regulatory compliance with those standards.

The Bank’s governance of climate-related matters is characterised by clear Board oversight, dedicated committee structures and defined management responsibilities. The Board of Directors oversees climate-related risks and opportunities as part of its overall mandate, with climate topics featuring regularly on the Board agenda. An ESG & Sustainability Committee supports the Board through more detailed review and escalation of climate-related issues. At the management level, the Climate Risk Management Committee coordinates the identification, assessment and monitoring of climate-related risks and opportunities across the Bank and reports to the Board on a semi-annual basis. A climate risk register, climate-related expertise at Board level and the integration of ESG and climate criteria into executive remuneration demonstrate that climate considerations are embedded within the Bank’s governance and incentive structures.

The Bank’s climate-related strategy focuses on managing material physical and transition risks across its lending portfolio and value chain, while pursuing defined transition and net-zero objectives. The strategy is supported by NGFS v4-based climate scenario analysis over short-, medium- and long-term horizons and is informed by a climate risk register that, in 2024, recorded eight material climate-related risks. Physical risks are concentrated in mortgage and regional corporate portfolios, including flood and wildfire exposures affecting collateral values and borrower creditworthiness. Transition risks arise primarily from policy, technology and market developments affecting sectors such as fossil fuels, industrials and auto lending, as well as reputational risks linked to financed emissions. At the same time, the Bank identifies opportunities in green and sustainability-linked lending, sustainable bond underwriting, transition advisory services, renewable energy project finance and operational energy efficiency, which inform business development and resource allocation decisions.

Risk management processes are designed to integrate climate-related risk into the Bank’s broader enterprise risk management framework. The climate risk register assigns ratings, time horizons and estimated financial impacts to each identified climate risk and specifies monitoring frequencies and mitigation actions. A majority of registered risks are explicitly linked to enterprise risk management processes and, where relevant, to the Internal Capital Adequacy Assessment Process. Scenario analysis, based on NGFS v4 pathways, is used as a forward-looking tool to inform the assessment of selected climate risks across orderly, disorderly and hot-house world scenarios. While the principal focus of the 2024 risk register is on exposures in the Bank’s financing activities rather than on suppliers or funding structures, certain transition and reputational risks have the potential to influence perceptions among investors and funding providers.

The Bank’s metrics and targets framework centres on portfolio-related greenhouse gas emissions, exposure to high-carbon and fossil-fuel sectors, green lending, and climate-related capital and operating expenditure. For 2024, the Bank reports total financed emissions from loans of 35,973,167.69 tCO₂e and a portfolio-level carbon intensity of 1,154.81 tCO₂e per million euro of lending, reflecting the emissions profile of a loan portfolio of EUR 31,150.64 million. High-carbon sectors account for 35.5% of the loan book (EUR 11,059.49 million), with fossil-fuel exposure representing 22.15% (EUR 6,899.59 million). Green loans represent 10.71% of the portfolio (EUR 3,335.75 million). Climate-related capital expenditure is reported at EUR 476.95 million and climate-related operating expenditure at EUR 219.32 million, highlighting the Bank’s allocation of resources to manage climate-related risks and to support climate-related opportunities. These indicators are complemented by climate targets covering absolute Scope 1–2 emissions reductions, financed emissions intensity reductions and an entity-wide net-zero objective; however, quantified operational Scope 1, Scope 2 and broader Scope 3 emissions for 2024 are not available in the underlying evidence and are therefore not presented.

The disclosures in this report are grounded in the best available internal data and documentation for Eurolux Universal Bank AG, supplemented where necessary by structured estimates that are constrained by the Bank’s known risk profile and financial context. The Bank acknowledges that the information presented is not exhaustive, that some metrics draw on generated approximations, and that external assurance, where obtained, is limited to Scope 1 and Scope 2 emissions rather than the full scope of reported sustainability and climate indicators. Within these boundaries, the Bank aims to provide balanced, decision-useful information on how climate-related risks and opportunities are governed, embedded in strategy, managed through risk processes and monitored through defined metrics and targets.

### Strategy

#### Overview

Eurolux Universal Bank AG’s climate-related strategy is structured to identify and manage material physical and transition risks across its lending portfolio and value chain, pursue defined transition and net-zero targets, and allocate financial resources to support portfolio alignment and client transition. The strategy integrates a dedicated climate risk register, portfolio steering tools, and NGFS v4-based scenario analysis across short-, medium-, and long-term horizons. High-carbon and fossil-fuel exposures, as well as green lending volumes, are monitored to inform sectoral priorities and client engagement. Transition planning is anchored in summary targets for Scope 1–2 emissions, financed emissions intensity, and a 2050 net-zero ambition, operationalised through sector exposure limits, collateral overlays, and decarbonisation glide-path monitoring. Climate-related opportunities in green loans, sustainable capital markets, and advisory services are assessed alongside risks to guide business development and resource allocation. Scenario analysis outcomes inform capital planning, risk appetite, and the calibration of resilience measures.

#### Sustainability risks and opportunities across the value chain

The Bank’s climate risk profile comprises both physical and transition risks across its value chain, with eight material risks identified for 2024—three physical and five transition risks. Physical risks are concentrated in mortgage and regional corporate portfolios, notably “Physical risk — mortgage book flood exposure” (high risk, long-term, estimated impact EUR 24.8 million) and “Physical risk — wildfire exposure in southern portfolio” (medium risk, short-term, EUR 22.7 million), affecting collateral values and borrower creditworthiness, including own operations’ buildings and facilities. Transition risks arise primarily from policy, technology, and market shifts, including “Transition risk — EV shift in auto lending” (short-term, EUR 165.2 million), “Stranded assets — fossil fuel sector exposure” (long-term, EUR 136.5 million), and “Transition risk — carbon pricing on industrial loans” (medium-term, EUR 67.5 million). Reputational risk from scrutiny of financed emissions is also identified.

Across the value chain, the most material quantified exposures relate to downstream financing counterparties in high-carbon sectors: Manufacture of coke and refined petroleum (EUR 3,903.65 million), Mining of coal and lignite (EUR 3,547.58 million), Mining of metal ores (EUR 2,749.58 million), and Other mining and quarrying (EUR 2,102.44 million), all with elevated transition policy risk. Corporate borrowers in Manufacture of motor vehicles (EUR 3,871.6 million) and Education (EUR 2,962.25 million) are exposed to chronic physical and transition risks. Portfolio-level indicators show high-carbon sector exposure of 35.5% (EUR 11,059.49 million) and fossil-fuel exposure of 22.15% (EUR 6,899.59 million), alongside financed emissions of 35,973,167.69 tCO2e and a carbon intensity of 1,154.81 tCO2e per EUR million. Own operations and upstream suppliers face physical disruption and transition market risks linked to buildings, corporate fleet, and business travel.

Climate-related opportunities are identified across products, services, and resource efficiency. These include green and sustainability-linked loan growth (estimated revenue impact EUR 180.0 million, medium-term, medium confidence), sustainable bond underwriting (EUR 95.0 million, short-term), transition advisory services (EUR 42.0 million, medium-term), renewable energy project finance (EUR 130.0 million, long-term), and operational energy efficiency (EUR 18.0 million, short-term). These figures represent estimated revenue impacts with medium confidence and are not assured outcomes.

#### Strategic management of climate-related risks and opportunities

The Bank manages climate-related risks and opportunities through an integrated framework combining risk management, portfolio steering, and transition planning. The climate risk register informs prioritisation across short- (0–2 years), medium- (2–5 years), and long-term (5+ years) horizons, with high- and medium-rated risks linked to specific mitigation actions. For example, transition technology risk in auto lending is addressed through climate scenario stress testing integrated into the Internal Capital Adequacy Assessment Process (ICAAP), while transition policy risk on industrial loans is managed via sector exposure limits and enhanced due diligence. Stranded asset risk in fossil fuel sectors is addressed through collateral revaluation and flood mapping overlays, which are also applied to physical risk exposures such as flood and wildfire-prone collateral. Reputational risk from financed emissions is managed through portfolio decarbonisation glide-path monitoring, aligned with the Bank’s Scope 3 (Category 15) intensity-reduction target under the NZBA framework.

Transition planning is anchored in three summary targets: an absolute Scope 1–2 reduction target to 2030 (SBTi 1.5°C, 40% progress in 2024), a Scope 3 Category 15 intensity-reduction target to 2030 (NZBA, 25% progress), and a net-zero target across all scopes by 2050 (NZBA, 13.3% progress). These targets guide portfolio alignment, client engagement, and the expansion of green finance, including a green loan book of EUR 3,335.75 million (10.71% of total loans). Climate-related opportunities are managed through dedicated product development in green loans, sustainable bond underwriting, transition advisory, and renewable energy project finance, with risk-adjusted pricing and due diligence informed by scenario analysis and sector pathways. The available documentation does not specify the detailed assumptions or dependencies underlying the transition plan.

#### Climate-related effects on business model, value chain and decision-making

Climate considerations are increasingly embedded in the Bank’s lending and portfolio decisions, particularly in sectors with elevated transition and physical risk. High-carbon and fossil-fuel exposures (35.5% and 22.15% of the loan book, respectively) are subject to sector exposure limits, enhanced due diligence, and collateral overlays, influencing origination, renewal, and refinancing decisions for corporate borrowers in fossil fuel extraction, refining, and energy-intensive manufacturing. The identified risk of stranded assets in fossil fuel sectors and the EV-driven transition risk in auto lending are reflected in portfolio steering and client engagement on transition plans and covenants.

The Bank’s value chain management incorporates climate risk into relationships with financing counterparties, own operations, and, to a more limited extent, upstream suppliers. For financing counterparties, climate risk assessments are applied to large exposures in mining, refining, motor vehicles, and other sectors with significant policy and technology risk. Own operations, including buildings and corporate fleet, are managed with a focus on energy efficiency and emissions reduction, consistent with the Scope 1–2 reduction target.

Decision-making is informed by NGFS v4 scenario analysis, which feeds into ICAAP, risk appetite setting, and capital planning, and by monitoring progress against climate targets and green loan growth. Climate-related opportunities in green lending, sustainable bond underwriting, and advisory services influence product strategy and client coverage priorities. The available documentation describes these portfolio and value-chain effects but does not include a separate, detailed assessment of climate-related impacts on the overall business model architecture or quantified trade-offs between climate objectives and other strategic priorities.

#### Financial effects and resource allocation

Climate-related financial effects are reflected in both risk and opportunity dimensions. On the risk side, the climate risk register quantifies potential financial impacts for selected risks, including EUR 165.2 million for transition technology risk in auto lending, EUR 136.5 million for stranded assets in fossil fuel sectors, EUR 67.5 million for carbon pricing on industrial loans, and EUR 24.8 million and EUR 22.7 million for flood and wildfire physical risks, respectively. These figures inform provisioning, capital planning, and risk appetite but are not presented as scenario-wide loss estimates.

Hot house scenarios, including NDC and Current Policies, assume delayed and uneven policy action, with carbon pricing ramping up from 2030 and temperature outcomes in the higher range of the NGFS v4 set (up to around 3.0°C). Key scenario assumptions include carbon price ranges from EUR 1.2 to EUR 287.1 per tCO2e, technology readiness levels ranging from low to high across sectors, and temperature outcomes between approximately 1.5°C and 3.0°C, depending on the scenario family. Macroeconomic and energy system dynamics are embedded in the NGFS v4 pathways, including increasing renewable energy shares in orderly and disorderly transitions. The scenario methodology covers lending book exposures across DE, FR, IT, NL, and ES, and applies to the 2025, 2030, and 2050 horizons.

Monitoring frequencies recorded in the register are either quarterly or semi-annual, providing an indication of how often risk owners are expected to review and update the underlying risk information. Six of the eight risks are flagged as integrated into the Bank’s broader enterprise risk management (ERM) processes, corresponding to an ERM integration share of 75%. For these risks, mitigation actions are recorded in the risk register and, where evidenced, linked to existing risk processes such as the Internal Capital Adequacy Assessment Process (ICAAP).

Scenario analysis is used as a forward-looking input for selected risks rather than uniformly across all risk types. The risk register evidences scenario links to four scenario identifiers (SCN0002, SCN0004, SCN0011 and SCN0013), which in turn draw on 18 climate scenarios based on the NGFS v4 framework, including orderly, disorderly and hot-house world pathways (for example Below 2°C, Net Zero 2050, Delayed Transition and Current Policies) with horizon years of 2025, 2030 and 2050. Compared with the prior period, 4 of the 8 risks are marked as changed, primarily reflecting updates to transition risk assessments, time horizons and mitigation approaches. This indicates that climate-related risks are revisited and, where necessary, re-assessed as part of ongoing risk management.
