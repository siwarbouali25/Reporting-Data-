### Governance

#### Overview

For the year ended 31 December 2024, Eurolux Universal Bank AG’s climate-related governance is organised across the Board of Directors, the ESG & Sustainability Committee, executive management and supporting control processes. Based on available evidence, climate-related governance is reflected in Board oversight, ESG & Sustainability Committee activity, executive management responsibility, semi-annual Board reporting, ERM integration indicators, climate checks for major transactions and a climate risk register.

The Board has overall responsibility for overseeing climate-related risks and opportunities as part of the bank’s business strategy and risk profile. Oversight is reflected in the proportion of Board meetings at which climate appears on the agenda, semi-annual climate risk reporting and documented 2024 Board and committee decisions.

At committee level, the ESG & Sustainability Committee provides more detailed oversight of climate-related matters, including review of progress against climate-related objectives and monitoring of key climate-related risks and associated management actions. Management responsibility for identifying, assessing and managing climate-related risks and opportunities is assigned to the Climate Risk Management Committee, which reports to the Board on a semi-annual basis.

Supporting processes are reflected in ERM integration indicators, climate risk reporting to the Board and the use of climate checks for major transactions. The bank uses a climate risk register that, in 2024, recorded 8 climate-related risks with defined categories, ratings, time horizons and mitigation actions. Board-level climate expertise, a skills development programme and the inclusion of climate and ESG-related metrics in executive remuneration further anchor accountability for climate-related matters.

#### The role of the Board of Directors

The Board of Directors of Eurolux Universal Bank AG comprises 10 members, of whom 68.5% are recorded as independent for 2024. Source data indicates that climate-related matters are considered within the Board’s wider responsibilities for strategy, risk appetite and oversight of the bank’s performance and resilience.

In 2024, the Board held 8 meetings (2023: 7; 2022: 4). Climate-related topics appeared on the agenda at 72.6% of these meetings (2023: 73.1%; 2022: 69.8%), indicating regular Board-level engagement with climate-related risks and opportunities. The Board receives climate risk reporting on a semi-annual basis, including information derived from the climate risk register and ERM integration indicators.

Board oversight of climate-related matters is evidenced through:

- agenda coverage of climate-related topics across the year;
- semi-annual reporting from management on climate risk, including material risk developments; and
- documented climate-related decisions in 2024 taken by the full Board, as further detailed in the Governance decisions, controls and evidence boundaries subsection.

The Board’s role includes overseeing how climate-related risks are considered in major transactions. Source data indicates that climate checks are applied to major transactions, which are reported up to the Board as part of transaction approval processes, although the documentation reviewed does not evidence formal escalation thresholds or a quantitative trigger framework.

The Board also oversees progress against climate-related objectives and targets, including consideration of related performance metrics within remuneration policies. This is reflected in the inclusion of ESG and climate-related metrics in CEO and executive remuneration and in the Board’s consideration of recommendations from the ESG & Sustainability Committee on climate-related matters.

#### Board committees and climate-related oversight

The ESG & Sustainability Committee is the primary Board committee supporting the Board in overseeing climate-related risks and opportunities. Available documentation evidences the committee’s activity and meeting frequency but does not include a separate committee charter or terms of reference specific to climate.

In 2024, the ESG & Sustainability Committee met 7 times (2023: 6; 2022: 5), with climate-related topics included within its remit. The increasing meeting frequency over the period 2022–2024 indicates that the committee has provided more regular oversight of ESG and climate-related topics over time.

Within the Board’s governance framework, the ESG & Sustainability Committee:

- reviews climate-related risk and opportunity assessments and associated management actions;
- considers progress against climate-related objectives and indicators prepared by management;
- reviews proposals related to climate-related methodologies, tools or frameworks before their escalation to the Board; and
- provides recommendations to the Board on climate-related matters, including those with implications for executive performance metrics and disclosures.

Committee oversight of climate-related issues is further evidenced by documented climate-related decisions in 2024 taken by the ESG & Sustainability Committee, some of which were subsequently considered or endorsed by the full Board. These decisions, and their specific topics and dates, are summarised in the Governance decisions, controls and evidence boundaries subsection.

Documentation reviewed does not provide a climate-specific committee mandate or an explicit description of how climate-related responsibilities are articulated within a formal terms-of-reference document. However, the recorded frequency of meetings and decision topics indicate that the ESG & Sustainability Committee has a sustained role in climate-related oversight.

#### Management responsibility for climate-related risks and opportunities

In 2024, management responsibility for climate-related risks and opportunities is assigned to the Climate Risk Management Committee. This management-level committee is responsible for coordinating the identification, assessment, monitoring and management of climate-related risks across the bank, and for preparing climate risk information for semi-annual reporting to the Board.

The Climate Risk Management Committee uses several key mechanisms:

- Climate risk register: The bank uses a climate risk register that, in 2024, recorded 8 climate-related risks. These risks are classified across physical acute, physical chronic, transition market, transition policy, transition reputational and transition technology categories. Each risk is assigned a rating (high or medium), a time horizon (short 0–2 years, medium 2–5 years or long 5+ years) and an associated monitoring frequency, typically quarterly or semi-annual.
- ERM integration: Source data indicates that 6 of the 8 recorded climate-related risks are integrated into the bank’s enterprise risk management framework. For these risks, ERM integration indicators show inclusion within group risk reporting and consideration in capital and liquidity planning processes.
- Scenario analysis identifiers: For selected climate-related risks, the risk register records scenario analysis identifiers that link individual risks to particular climate scenarios. These identifiers provide a reference between risk assessments and the bank’s climate scenario analysis work, without evidencing additional internal assessment processes beyond those identifiers.
- Mitigation actions: For each risk, the register records high-level mitigation actions. For 2024, examples of actions associated with risks in the register include:
  - integrating climate scenario stress testing into the Internal Capital Adequacy Assessment Process (ICAAP);
  - applying collateral revaluation with flood mapping overlays for relevant portfolios;
  - incorporating engagement and transition-plan covenants with borrowers;
  - offering green retrofit financing incentives;
  - monitoring portfolio decarbonisation glide paths; and
  - applying sector exposure limits and enhanced due diligence in selected sectors.
- Monitoring frequency and changes: The risk register records monitoring frequencies at quarterly or semi-annual intervals, and source data shows that 4 of the 8 risks recorded were updated or changed since the prior period, reflecting ongoing management attention.

The Climate Risk Management Committee prepares semi-annual reports for the Board summarising key developments in climate-related risks, changes in risk ratings, mitigation progress and ERM integration indicators. These reports draw on the climate risk register and are a primary channel through which the Board is informed about climate-related risk exposures and management responses.

Climate-related considerations in major transactions are reflected through climate checks for such transactions. Source data indicates that these checks are applied and are considered by management when presenting major transactions to the Board or relevant committees. Documentation reviewed does not evidence formal, quantitative escalation thresholds or detailed trigger-based mechanics for when climate issues must be escalated, beyond their capture in the climate risk register and the general requirement to consider climate factors in significant transaction approvals.

Across the period 2022–2024, different management committees are recorded as having responsibility for ESG or climate-related topics (Group Sustainability Committee in 2022, ESG Executive Committee in 2023 and Climate Risk Management Committee in 2024). Source data records these names by year but does not evidence continuity, formal renaming or a direct evolution between these committees.

#### Skills, competencies and remuneration

Board-level oversight of climate-related matters is supported by climate-related expertise among Board members and a skills development programme. For 2024, 37.5% of Board members are recorded as having climate-related expertise (2023: 35.5%; 2022: 33.5%), indicating a gradual increase over the three-year period. In addition, a skills development programme for Board members is in place, which includes climate and broader ESG topics, although the documentation reviewed does not provide a detailed curriculum.

Available documentation does not evidence a formalised process for assessing the adequacy of Board skills and competencies specifically in relation to climate-related risks and opportunities. The bank instead relies on a combination of recorded climate-related expertise percentages and participation in the skills development programme as indicators of Board capability in this area.

Climate-related performance metrics are incorporated into executive remuneration. For 2024, 9.3% of the CEO’s compensation is linked to ESG-related metrics (2023: 7.8%; 2022: 6.3%), reflecting an increasing weighting of ESG considerations over time. For the wider executive population, 15.1% of remuneration is linked to climate-related metrics in 2024 (2023: 15.3%; 2022: 8.7%).

These metrics, which form part of the bank’s performance management framework, are used by the Board and the ESG & Sustainability Committee when overseeing the implementation of climate-related strategies and monitoring progress against climate-related objectives. Documentation reviewed indicates that climate and ESG-related metrics are considered when calibrating executive remuneration outcomes, but does not provide detailed formulae or a breakdown of all underlying performance indicators.

#### Governance decisions, controls and evidence boundaries

In 2024, the Board and its committees took several significant climate-related decisions:

- On 24 January 2024, the full Board approved the 2024 ESG report for publication. Topics discussed in connection with this decision included carbon credit-related approvals and review of the bank’s transition plan.
- On 15 April 2024, the ESG & Sustainability Committee approved a climate scenario analysis methodology. This decision was considered by the full Board at subsequent meetings on 10 June, 17 July and 29 August 2024. In these discussions, topics included executive remuneration ESG key performance indicators, progress against net-zero objectives, physical risk updates, TCFD-related review and transition plan review.
- On 15 April, 20 May, 19 July and 26 November 2024, the ESG & Sustainability Committee and the full Board approved a carbon credit procurement budget for 2024. Topics in these meetings included executive remuneration ESG key performance indicators, net-zero progress, physical risk updates, climate scenario analysis, TCFD-related review and transition plan review.
- On 7 October 2024, the full Board endorsed an updated transition plan, following consideration of climate-related risks, green finance growth and TCFD-related review.
- On 1 November 2024, the full Board endorsed a revision of interim net-zero targets, based on an assessment of net-zero progress presented to it.

These decisions are in addition to the semi-annual climate risk reporting referenced earlier and provide evidence of Board and committee oversight of:

- the bank’s transition planning and net-zero objectives;
- climate scenario analysis methodologies used to assess climate-related risks and opportunities;
- the calibration of executive remuneration ESG key performance indicators;
- the use of carbon credits via an approved procurement budget; and
- climate-related disclosures, including the ESG report.

Controls and procedures used by management to support the oversight of climate-related risks and opportunities include:

- the climate risk register, capturing 8 climate-related risks in 2024 with categories, ratings, time horizons, monitoring frequencies, scenario analysis identifiers and mitigation actions;
- integration of 6 of these climate-related risks into the enterprise risk management framework, as indicated by ERM integration indicators;
- semi-annual climate risk reporting from the Climate Risk Management Committee to the Board; and
- climate checks for major transactions, providing a mechanism for climate-related considerations to be taken into account in significant credit and investment decisions.

Assurance-related controls are limited to specific areas. An external limited assurance engagement was performed by EY in accordance with ISAE 3000 over the bank’s Scope 1 and Scope 2 greenhouse gas emissions for 2024, as reported elsewhere in this report. Based on available evidence, this assurance covers only Scope 1 and Scope 2 emissions. Financed emissions (Scope 3, category 15), which for 2024 are recorded at 35,973,167.69 tCO₂e, are outside the stated assurance scope.

Regarding alignment with external frameworks, source data flags the bank’s climate-related disclosures as aligned with TCFD recommendations and IFRS S2. However, available documentation does not evidence a separate governance control process specifically designed to verify or assure such alignment beyond existing governance of disclosures and Board approval of the ESG report.

There are several important evidence boundaries:

- Formal mandates and terms of reference: Available documentation evidences the activity and meeting frequency of the Board and the ESG & Sustainability Committee, but does not include a climate-specific Board or committee charter, nor detailed terms of reference describing climate-related responsibilities.
- Trade-offs: Board decision evidence identifies the climate-related decisions listed above but does not describe quantified or specific Board-level trade-offs, such as between profitability, capital allocation, implementation cost, risk appetite or competing strategic priorities, in relation to these decisions.
- Skills adequacy assessment: While Board climate expertise percentages and a skills development programme are recorded, documentation reviewed does not evidence a formal process for assessing the adequacy of Board skills and competencies in relation to climate-related risks and opportunities.
- Escalation thresholds: For the climate risk register and related processes, documentation reviewed does not evidence formal escalation thresholds or trigger-based escalation mechanics that would automatically escalate climate-related issues to the Board or committees beyond the semi-annual reporting cycle and standard governance pathways.
- Management committee continuity: Source data records different management committee names responsible for ESG or climate-related matters over 2022–2024, but does not evidence whether these committees represent a continued or renamed body or separate structures across the years.
- Alignment control processes: Although source data flags TCFD and IFRS S2 alignment, there is no separate documented governance control process whose explicit purpose is to verify or assure alignment with those frameworks beyond existing review and approval processes for reports and methodologies.

These evidence boundaries should be considered when interpreting the scope and depth of the governance disclosures for Eurolux Universal Bank AG’s climate-related risks and opportunities for the year ended 31 December 2024.
