### Governance

#### Overview

Eurolux Universal Bank AG organises climate-related governance across the Board of Directors, the ESG & Sustainability Committee and executive management. Climate-related governance is reflected in Board oversight, ESG & Sustainability Committee activity, executive management responsibility, semi-annual Board reporting, integration indicators with the enterprise risk management (ERM) framework, climate checks for major transactions and the use of a climate risk register.

In 2024, the Board comprised 10 members, of whom 68.5% were independent. The Board met eight times during the year, with climate-related topics on the agenda at 72.6% of meetings. Climate risk reporting was provided to the Board on a semi-annual basis.

The Board is informed by an ESG & Sustainability Committee at Board level and a Climate Risk Management Committee at management level. The ESG & Sustainability Committee met seven times in 2024. Management used a climate risk register that recorded eight climate-related risks, of which six were flagged as integrated into the ERM framework. The Bank has climate checks for major transactions.

Board and committee oversight of climate-related risks and opportunities is reflected in documented climate-related decisions in 2024. Skills and incentives are supported by the Board’s climate expertise profile, a skills development programme and climate- and ESG-linked remuneration for the Chief Executive Officer and other executives.

#### The role of the Board of Directors

The Board of Directors oversees climate-related risks and opportunities as part of its overall oversight of the Bank’s business model, strategy and risk profile. In 2024, the Board comprised 10 members, with 68.5% classified as independent. The Board met eight times during the year ended 31 December 2024, compared with seven meetings in 2023 and four meetings in 2022.

Climate-related topics featured regularly on the Board agenda. Climate-related items were included at 72.6% of Board meetings in 2024, compared with 73.1% in 2023 and 69.8% in 2022. This agenda coverage, together with semi-annual climate risk reporting and documented climate-related decisions in 2024, reflects the Board’s oversight of climate-related risks, opportunities and targets.

Semi-annual climate risk reporting to the Board provides an overview of climate-related risks recorded in the climate risk register, including their status and monitoring frequency. Through its consideration of this reporting and its climate-related decisions in 2024, the Board oversees the setting and monitoring of climate-related targets and the integration of climate-related considerations into strategy and major decisions.

The Board takes climate-related risks and opportunities into account when overseeing the Bank’s strategy and major transactions by: (i) receiving semi-annual climate risk reporting; (ii) considering climate-related topics at a high proportion of its meetings; and (iii) taking climate-related decisions in 2024. The Bank’s climate-related Board decisions do not describe specific quantified trade-offs such as explicit balances between profitability, capital allocation, implementation cost, risk appetite or competing strategic priorities.

#### Board committees and climate-related oversight

The Board is informed in its climate-related oversight by the ESG & Sustainability Committee. This committee provides a forum for more detailed consideration of climate-related risks, opportunities, targets and disclosures.

The ESG & Sustainability Committee met seven times in 2024, compared with six meetings in 2023 and five meetings in 2022. This meeting frequency reflects regular engagement with climate-related topics at committee level. Committee oversight of climate-related matters includes the discussion and decision of climate-related topics in 2024.

The Bank’s governance information sets out the ESG & Sustainability Committee’s activity and meeting frequency but does not include a separate climate-specific committee charter or terms of reference.

#### Management responsibility for climate-related risks and opportunities

Management responsibility for climate-related risks and opportunities in 2024 is assigned to the Climate Risk Management Committee. In prior years, the management committee name recorded for climate-related responsibilities was Group Sustainability Committee in 2022 and ESG Executive Committee in 2023.

Climate risk reporting to the Board on climate-related risks was provided on a semi-annual basis. The Bank has climate checks for major transactions so that climate-related factors are considered in significant transaction decisions.

The Bank uses a climate risk register that, in 2024, recorded eight climate-related risks. These risks are recorded across physical and transition categories, including physical acute, physical chronic, transition market, transition policy, transition reputational and transition technology risks. The register records risk ratings (high or medium) and time horizons (short term of 0–2 years, medium term of 2–5 years and long term of more than 5 years). Monitoring frequencies recorded in the register include quarterly and semi-annual monitoring.

ERM integration indicators in the climate risk register show that six of the eight recorded climate-related risks are flagged as integrated into the enterprise risk management framework. Four of the eight climate-related risks were recorded as changed compared with the prior period. The register records identifiers for climate scenario analysis for selected risks and records mitigation actions for risks, including:

- climate scenario stress testing integrated into the Internal Capital Adequacy Assessment Process (ICAAP);
- collateral revaluation and flood mapping overlays;
- engagement and transition-plan covenants with borrowers;
- green retrofit financing incentives;
- portfolio decarbonisation glide-path monitoring; and
- sector exposure limits and enhanced due diligence.

The climate risk register includes specific recorded examples of climate-related risks with quantified financial impacts. Examples include:

- a transition technology risk related to the shift to electric vehicles in auto lending, rated high, with a short-term time horizon, quarterly monitoring, ERM integration, a link to climate scenario analysis and an estimated potential financial impact of approximately EUR 165 million;
- a transition market risk related to potential stranded assets in fossil fuel sector exposures, rated high, with a long-term time horizon, quarterly monitoring, ERM integration and an estimated potential financial impact of approximately EUR 137 million;
- a transition policy risk related to carbon pricing on industrial loans, rated high, with a medium-term time horizon, quarterly monitoring, ERM integration and an estimated potential financial impact of approximately EUR 68 million;
- a physical acute risk related to mortgage book flood exposure, rated high, with a long-term time horizon, quarterly monitoring, ERM integration, a link to climate scenario analysis and an estimated potential financial impact of approximately EUR 25 million; and
- a transition reputational risk related to scrutiny of financed emissions, rated medium, with a medium-term time horizon, semi-annual monitoring, ERM integration, a link to climate scenario analysis and an estimated potential financial impact of approximately EUR 66 million.

These recorded risks illustrate how climate-related risks are captured in the climate risk register by category, rating and time horizon, with associated monitoring frequencies, links to climate scenario analysis where relevant, mitigation actions and ERM integration indicators. The Bank does not currently disclose formal escalation thresholds or trigger-based escalation mechanics for climate-related risks.

#### Skills, competencies and remuneration

The Board’s climate-related skills and competencies are reflected in the proportion of Board members with climate-related expertise and in the Bank’s skills development activities. In 2024, 37.5% of Board members were recorded as having climate-related expertise, compared with 35.5% in 2023 and 33.5% in 2022. The Bank operates a skills development programme that includes climate-related topics for Board members and senior management. The Bank does not currently disclose a formal Board skills adequacy assessment process specific to climate-related oversight.

Remuneration structures incorporate climate- and ESG-related performance metrics. The Chief Executive Officer’s compensation includes an ESG-linked component, which represented 9.3% of total CEO compensation in 2024, compared with 7.8% in 2023 and 6.3% in 2022. For the executive population as a whole, climate-linked remuneration represented 15.1% of total executive remuneration in 2024, compared with 15.3% in 2023 and 8.7% in 2022.

These remuneration metrics show that climate- and ESG-related performance measures are included in executive incentive structures.

#### Governance decisions, controls and evidence boundaries

In 2024, the Board and its committees took a number of specific climate-related decisions that illustrate how climate-related risks, opportunities and targets are overseen and integrated into strategy, risk management and remuneration. The decisions described below are selected examples from the Bank’s climate-related Board and committee activity during the year.

Key 2024 climate-related decisions included:

- On 24 January 2024, the Full Board approved the 2024 ESG report for publication, following consideration of climate-related disclosures and associated topics including a carbon credit procurement proposal and the transition plan.
- On 15 April 2024, the ESG & Sustainability Committee approved a climate scenario analysis methodology. This methodology was subsequently considered by the Full Board at meetings on 10 June, 17 July and 29 August 2024, in the context of climate-related topics including executive remuneration ESG key performance indicators, progress against net-zero objectives, physical risk updates, climate-related disclosure frameworks and the transition plan.
- On 15 April, 20 May and 19 July 2024, the ESG & Sustainability Committee considered climate-related topics including executive remuneration ESG key performance indicators, progress against net-zero objectives, physical risk updates, climate scenario analysis and climate-related disclosure frameworks. On 26 November 2024, the Full Board approved a carbon credit procurement budget, taking into account these committee deliberations.
- On 7 October 2024, the Full Board endorsed an updated transition plan, following consideration of topics including green finance growth and climate-related disclosure frameworks.
- On 1 November 2024, the Full Board endorsed a revision of interim net-zero targets, following consideration of progress against those targets.

These decisions show how the Board and the ESG & Sustainability Committee oversee climate-related targets, methodologies, transition planning, carbon credit procurement and climate-related disclosures, and how they incorporate climate-related considerations into strategy, major decisions and executive remuneration.

Controls and procedures supporting this governance include:

- semi-annual climate risk reporting to the Board, providing an overview of climate-related risks recorded in the climate risk register;
- the use of a climate risk register that, in 2024, recorded eight climate-related risks, with six of these risks flagged as integrated into the enterprise risk management framework and four recorded as changed compared with the prior period;
- recorded monitoring frequencies (quarterly and semi-annual) for climate-related risks, together with identifiers for climate scenario analysis and recorded mitigation actions such as climate scenario stress testing integrated into ICAAP, collateral revaluation and flood mapping overlays, engagement and transition-plan covenants with borrowers, green retrofit financing incentives, portfolio decarbonisation glide-path monitoring and sector exposure limits with enhanced due diligence; and
- climate checks for major transactions, indicating that climate-related considerations are applied to major transaction decisions.

The Bank does not currently disclose formal escalation thresholds or trigger-based escalation mechanics for climate-related risks. The Bank’s climate-related Board and committee decisions do not describe specific quantified trade-offs such as explicit balances between profitability, capital allocation, implementation cost, risk appetite or competing strategic priorities.

External assurance over climate-related information is limited in scope. For the year ended 31 December 2024, EY provided limited assurance in accordance with ISAE 3000 over the Bank’s Scope 1 and Scope 2 greenhouse gas emissions. Financed emissions and other Scope 3 categories, including the Bank’s 2024 financed emissions of approximately 36 million tonnes of carbon dioxide equivalent, are outside the scope of this external assurance.

The Bank flags its climate-related disclosures as aligned with the recommendations of the Task Force on Climate-related Financial Disclosures and with IFRS S2. The Bank does not currently disclose a separate governance control process specifically designed to verify or assure alignment with these frameworks beyond Board and committee oversight, management processes and the limited assurance described above.
