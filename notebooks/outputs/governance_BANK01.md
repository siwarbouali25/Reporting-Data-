### Governance

#### Overview

Eurolux Universal Bank AG has established climate-related governance that is reflected in Board oversight, ESG & Sustainability Committee activity, executive management responsibility, semi-annual Board reporting, ERM integration indicators, climate checks for major transactions and a climate risk register. Governance indicators the Bank reports include the percentage of Board meetings with climate on the agenda, the proportion of Board members with climate expertise and the proportion of executive remuneration linked to ESG and climate-related performance.

The Bank uses a climate risk register that, in 2024, recorded 8 climate-related risks, with ERM integration indicators for a subset of these risks and links to climate scenario analysis identifiers. Climate risk reporting is provided to the Board on a semi-annual basis, drawing on this register to provide an overview of material climate-related risks. Management responsibility for climate-related risks and opportunities is assigned to the Climate Risk Management Committee, which is also responsible for providing climate-related input into major transactions where climate checks were performed in 2024.

Board and committee oversight of climate-related matters in 2024 is reflected in documented climate-related decisions taken during the year. These decisions, together with the governance indicators and management processes described in this section, provide the basis for the Bank’s climate-related governance disclosures and connect to the strategy, risk management, and metrics and targets sections of this report.

#### The role of the Board of Directors

The Board of Directors of Eurolux Universal Bank AG comprises 10 members, of whom 68.5% are independent. In the year ended 31 December 2024, the Board met 8 times, compared with 7 meetings in 2023 and 4 meetings in 2022. Climate-related topics were included on the Board agenda in 72.6% of meetings in 2024, compared with 73.1% in 2023 and 69.8% in 2022. This agenda coverage reflects the Board’s ongoing oversight of climate-related risks and opportunities as part of its broader responsibilities.

Climate risk reporting is provided to the Board on a semi-annual basis. This reporting provides an overview of material climate-related risks drawn from the Bank’s climate risk register and informs the Board’s oversight of climate-related risks and opportunities. The Board’s oversight of climate-related targets and performance is further reflected in documented climate-related decisions in 2024, which include decisions taken by the full Board and by the ESG & Sustainability Committee reporting to the Board.

The Board oversees the setting and monitoring of climate-related targets through its consideration of management reports and its documented climate-related decisions in 2024. Climate-related performance is also reflected in governance indicators the Bank reports, including the proportion of executive remuneration linked to ESG and climate-related metrics. The Board’s oversight of climate-related risks and opportunities is connected to the Bank’s strategy and risk management through the semi-annual reporting from management and the use of ERM integration indicators in the climate risk register.

#### Board committees and climate-related oversight

The Board is supported in its climate-related oversight by the ESG & Sustainability Committee. This committee met 7 times in 2024, compared with 6 meetings in 2023 and 5 meetings in 2022, reflecting regular committee-level consideration of ESG and climate-related matters. The ESG & Sustainability Committee reports to the Board and provides a forum for more detailed discussion of climate-related risks, opportunities, targets and performance indicators before matters are considered by the full Board where appropriate.

Committee activity in 2024 included the consideration of climate-related topics and the preparation of recommendations to the Board, as reflected in documented climate-related decisions taken during the year. These decisions cover areas such as climate-related methodologies, plans and resource allocations, and are recorded as decisions of the ESG & Sustainability Committee and, where applicable, of the full Board.

The Bank’s governance indicators, including climate-on-agenda percentages, committee meeting frequencies and climate-linked remuneration percentages, provide additional insight into the role of the ESG & Sustainability Committee and the Board in overseeing climate-related risks and opportunities.

#### Management responsibility for climate-related risks and opportunities

Management responsibility for climate-related risks and opportunities in 2024 is assigned to the Climate Risk Management Committee. This committee is responsible for coordinating the identification and assessment of climate-related risks, overseeing the climate risk register and providing climate risk reporting to the Board on a semi-annual basis. The committee also supports the application of climate considerations to major transactions, and the Bank performed climate checks for major transactions in 2024.

The Bank uses a climate risk register that, in 2024, recorded 8 climate-related risks. These risks cover a range of categories, including physical acute, physical chronic, transition market, transition policy, transition reputational and transition technology risks. The register records risk ratings of high or medium and classifies risks by time horizon, including short (0–2 years), medium (2–5 years) and long (more than 5 years).

Monitoring frequencies recorded in the register include quarterly and semi-annual monitoring, reflecting how often specific climate-related risks are reviewed by management. Scenario analysis identifiers are recorded for several risks, indicating linkages between the risk register and the Bank’s climate scenario analysis. The register also records mitigation actions, including:

- Climate scenario stress testing integrated into the Internal Capital Adequacy Assessment Process (ICAAP);
- Collateral revaluation and flood mapping overlay;
- Engagement and transition-plan covenants with borrowers;
- Green retrofit financing incentives;
- Portfolio decarbonisation glide-path monitoring; and
- Sector exposure limits and enhanced due diligence.

ERM integration indicators are recorded at the risk level. In 2024, 6 of 8 recorded climate-related risks carry an ERM-integration flag; enterprise-wide integration into the enterprise risk management framework is not evidenced. The register also records whether risks have changed since the prior period; 4 of the 8 climate-related risks recorded in 2024 changed compared with the prior period.

Material risk examples recorded in the climate risk register include:

- Physical risk — mortgage book flood exposure (physical acute, high rating, long time horizon, quarterly monitoring, ERM-integrated, linked to scenario analysis);
- Transition risk — EV shift in auto lending (transition technology, high rating, short time horizon, quarterly monitoring, ERM-integrated, linked to scenario analysis);
- Stranded assets — fossil fuel sector exposure (transition market, high rating, long time horizon, quarterly monitoring, ERM-integrated);
- Transition risk — carbon pricing on industrial loans (transition policy, high rating, medium time horizon, quarterly monitoring, ERM-integrated); and
- Reputational risk — financed emissions scrutiny (transition reputational, medium rating, medium time horizon, semi-annual monitoring, ERM-integrated, linked to scenario analysis).

These recorded risks illustrate how the Climate Risk Management Committee uses the climate risk register to classify, monitor and manage climate-related risks, and how ERM integration indicators and scenario analysis identifiers are applied at the risk level. Formal escalation thresholds or trigger-based escalation mechanics are not evidenced in the climate risk register.

#### Skills, competencies and remuneration

Board-level climate expertise is an important component of the Bank’s climate-related governance. In 2024, 37.5% of Board members were recorded as having climate-related expertise, compared with 35.5% in 2023 and 33.5% in 2022. The Bank operates a skills development programme that includes climate-related topics for Board members and senior management, with the aim of maintaining and enhancing the skills and competencies required to oversee climate-related risks and opportunities. The Bank does not currently disclose a formal process for assessing the adequacy of climate-related skills at Board level.

Climate-related performance is reflected in executive remuneration. The Chief Executive Officer’s remuneration includes an ESG-linked component, which represented 9.3% of total CEO remuneration in 2024, compared with 7.8% in 2023 and 6.3% in 2022. For all executives, climate-linked remuneration represented 15.1% of total remuneration in 2024, compared with 15.3% in 2023 and 8.7% in 2022. These climate-linked remuneration components are part of the Bank’s approach to aligning management incentives with the delivery of its climate-related strategy, targets and risk management objectives.

#### Governance decisions, controls and evidence boundaries

In 2024, the Board and its ESG & Sustainability Committee took a number of specific climate-related decisions that illustrate how governance processes, controls and procedures are applied to climate-related risks and opportunities.

Key 2024 climate-related decisions included:

- On 24 January 2024, the full Board approved the 2024 ESG report for publication, following consideration of climate-related disclosures and associated reporting.
- On 15 April 2024 (with related committee and Board discussions on 10 June, 17 July and 29 August 2024), the ESG & Sustainability Committee and the full Board approved the climate scenario analysis methodology to be used for assessing climate-related risks and opportunities.
- On 15 April 2024 (with related committee and Board discussions on 20 May, 19 July and 26 November 2024), the ESG & Sustainability Committee and the full Board approved the carbon credit procurement budget for 2024.
- On 7 October 2024, the full Board endorsed an updated transition plan for the Bank.
- On 1 November 2024, the full Board endorsed a revision of the Bank’s interim net-zero targets, following consideration of progress against existing targets and updated climate-related analysis.

These decisions were taken alongside committee-level discussions on topics such as executive remuneration ESG key performance indicators, physical risk updates, climate scenario analysis and TCFD-related reporting, as recorded in the Board and committee minutes for 2024. The Board decision evidence identifies climate-related decisions but does not describe specific quantified trade-offs such as profitability, capital allocation, implementation cost, risk appetite or competing strategic priorities.

Management controls and procedures supporting these decisions include:

- The use of a climate risk register that, in 2024, recorded 8 climate-related risks, with classification by category, rating and time horizon, monitoring frequencies, scenario analysis identifiers, mitigation actions and ERM integration indicators;
- Semi-annual climate risk reporting to the Board, providing an overview of material climate-related risks drawn from the climate risk register;
- ERM integration indicators at the risk level, with 6 of 8 recorded climate-related risks carrying an ERM-integration flag; and
- Climate checks performed for major transactions in 2024.

Formal escalation thresholds or trigger-based escalation mechanics for climate-related risks are not evidenced in the Bank’s governance documentation. Management committee names differ across years (Group Sustainability Committee in 2022, ESG Executive Committee in 2023 and Climate Risk Management Committee in 2024), and the Bank’s records do not evidence whether these represent a single evolving committee or separate committees. The Bank does not currently disclose a separate climate-specific charter, terms of reference or formal mandate for the Board or its committees.

External assurance over climate-related information in this report is limited to Scope 1 and Scope 2 greenhouse gas emissions. In 2024, EY provided limited assurance in accordance with ISAE 3000 over the Bank’s Scope 1 and Scope 2 emissions, as disclosed in the metrics and targets section. Financed emissions and other Scope 3 categories, including the Bank’s 2024 financed emissions of 35,973,168 tCO2e, are outside the stated assurance scope.

The Bank’s governance indicators include climate-on-agenda percentages, Board climate-expertise percentages and climate-linked remuneration percentages. These indicators are used to inform internal governance discussions. The Bank reports that its climate-related disclosures are aligned with TCFD and IFRS S2. The Bank does not currently describe a separate governance control process designed specifically to verify or assure such alignment beyond the governance mechanisms described above.
