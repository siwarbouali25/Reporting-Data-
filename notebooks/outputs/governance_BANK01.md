### Governance

#### Overview

For 2024, Eurolux Universal Bank AG’s climate-related governance is organised across the Board of Directors, the ESG & Sustainability Committee, and executive management. Available evidence indicates that climate-related governance is reflected in Board oversight, ESG & Sustainability Committee activity, executive management responsibility, semi-annual Board reporting, ERM integration flags, climate checks for major transactions, and the use of a climate risk register. The Board receives semi-annual climate risk reports, and climate-related topics appeared on the Board agenda at 72.6% of meetings in 2024 (73.1% in 2023, 69.8% in 2022). The ESG & Sustainability Committee met seven times in 2024, up from six in 2023 and five in 2022, evidencing regular engagement. Executive responsibility for climate-related risks and opportunities is assigned to the Climate Risk Management Committee, which reports to the Board on a semi-annual basis. Management uses a climate risk register that, in 2024, recorded eight climate-related risks, with links to the Group’s ERM framework and climate checks for major transactions. Board-level climate expertise, a skills development programme, and climate-linked remuneration for senior management are also evidenced. External limited assurance over selected climate metrics is provided within the scope described in the final subsection of this Governance section.

#### The role of the Board of Directors

The Board of Directors comprised 10 members in 2024, with 68.5% classified as independent. Source data indicates that 37.5% of Board members had climate-related expertise in 2024, compared with 35.5% in 2023 and 33.5% in 2022. The Board met eight times in 2024 (seven in 2023, four in 2022), with climate-related topics included on the agenda at 72.6% of meetings in 2024. Board oversight of climate-related matters is evidenced through agenda coverage, semi-annual climate risk reporting, and documented 2024 Board and committee decisions. Climate risk reporting to the Board is on a semi-annual basis, drawing on the climate risk register and ERM integration indicators to provide information on material climate-related risks, their categorisation, and monitoring status.

#### Board committees and climate-related oversight

The ESG & Sustainability Committee is the primary Board committee addressing climate-related topics in 2024. The committee met seven times in 2024 (six in 2023, five in 2022), evidencing regular engagement with climate-related issues. Available documentation evidences committee activity and meeting frequency but does not include the committee charter or terms of reference. The ESG & Sustainability Committee reviews climate-related risks and opportunities, including information derived from the climate risk register and management reports, and provides recommendations to the full Board. Its work in 2024 included consideration of climate-related performance indicators, risk updates, and other climate-related topics that informed documented 2024 Board and committee decisions. The committee’s oversight is complemented by the Board’s own agenda coverage and semi-annual climate risk reporting, providing a structured flow of climate-related information from management to the Board.

#### Management responsibility for climate-related risks and opportunities

In 2024, executive responsibility for climate-related risks and opportunities rests with the Climate Risk Management Committee. This management committee reports to the Board on a semi-annual basis and uses a climate risk register as part of its process. The climate risk register recorded eight climate-related risks in 2024. Risks are identified and documented in the register, classified by category (physical acute, physical chronic, transition market, transition policy, transition reputational, and transition technology), assigned a rating (high or medium), and a time horizon (short term 0–2 years, medium term 2–5 years, or long term 5 years and beyond). Each risk is associated with a defined monitoring frequency, typically quarterly or semi-annual. In 2024, six of the eight recorded climate-related risks were flagged as integrated into the Group’s ERM framework, and four risks changed since the prior period.

The climate risk register links selected risks to climate scenario analysis references (for example, SCN0002, SCN0004, SCN0011, and SCN0013), indicating that scenario outputs are used in assessing potential financial impacts. Mitigation actions recorded in the climate risk register include climate scenario stress testing integrated into the Internal Capital Adequacy Assessment Process (ICAAP), collateral revaluation and flood mapping overlays, engagement and transition-plan covenants with borrowers, green retrofit financing incentives, portfolio decarbonisation glide-path monitoring, and sector exposure limits and enhanced due diligence.

Material risk examples in the 2024 register include: transition technology risk related to the shift to electric vehicles in auto lending (financial impact: €165.2 million, high rating, quarterly monitoring); transition market risk associated with potential stranded assets in fossil fuel sector exposures (financial impact: €136.5 million, high rating, quarterly monitoring); transition policy risk linked to carbon pricing on industrial loans (financial impact: €67.5 million, high rating, quarterly monitoring); physical acute risk related to mortgage book flood exposure (financial impact: €24.8 million, high rating, quarterly monitoring); and transition reputational risk arising from scrutiny of financed emissions (financial impact: €65.5 million, medium rating, semi-annual monitoring). Where indicated, these risks are integrated into ERM.

Source data indicates that climate checks are performed for major transactions, evidencing that climate considerations are taken into account in the assessment of significant transactions, although available documentation does not evidence a formal mandatory policy or detailed trigger framework. Documentation reviewed does not evidence formal escalation thresholds or trigger-based escalation mechanics for climate-related risks beyond the ERM integration indicators and management committee oversight described above.

#### Skills, competencies and remuneration

Board-level climate expertise is tracked as a percentage of Board members with relevant experience, increasing from 33.5% in 2022 to 35.5% in 2023 and 37.5% in 2024. Source data also records the existence of a skills development programme covering climate-related topics for Board members and senior management. Available documentation does not evidence a separate formal process for assessing the adequacy of Board climate skills beyond these metrics and the development programme.

Remuneration structures incorporate climate and broader ESG considerations. The CEO’s variable compensation includes ESG-linked components, which represented 6.3% of total CEO compensation in 2022, 7.8% in 2023, and 9.3% in 2024. For the wider executive population, climate-linked remuneration elements accounted for 8.7% of total executive compensation in 2022, 15.3% in 2023, and 15.1% in 2024. These metrics indicate that climate and ESG performance are factors in executive remuneration outcomes, although available documentation does not provide a detailed breakdown of the underlying performance indicators in this section.

#### Governance decisions, controls and evidence boundaries

**Board and committee decisions**

Available Board and committee minutes for 2024 evidence a series of specific climate-related decisions:

- On 24 January 2024, the full Board approved the 2024 ESG report for publication, following consideration of topics including carbon credit approval and transition plan review.
- On 15 April 2024, the ESG & Sustainability Committee and, subsequently, the full Board approved the climate scenario analysis methodology, following discussions that included executive remuneration ESG KPIs, net-zero progress, physical risk updates, TCFD-related matters, and transition plan review. Related discussions continued on 10 June, 17 July, and 29 August 2024 at committee and Board level.
- On 15 April, 20 May, 19 July, and 26 November 2024, the ESG & Sustainability Committee and the full Board approved the 2024 carbon credit procurement budget, in the context of discussions covering executive remuneration ESG KPIs, net-zero progress, physical risk updates, scenario analysis, TCFD-related matters, and transition plan review.
- On 7 October 2024, the full Board endorsed an updated transition plan, following consideration of green finance growth and TCFD-related topics.
- On 1 November 2024, the full Board endorsed a revision to the Bank’s net-zero interim targets, in the context of a net-zero progress review.

These decisions, together with the semi-annual climate risk reports and the climate risk register, form the core documented evidence of Board and committee oversight of climate-related risks and opportunities in 2024.

**Controls, assurance and alignment indicators**

Management governance controls for climate-related risks in 2024 are reflected in the operation of the Climate Risk Management Committee, the use of a climate risk register with eight recorded risks, ERM integration indicators for six of these risks, monitoring frequencies (quarterly or semi-annual), links to climate scenario analysis, and the application of mitigation actions recorded in the climate risk register such as ICAAP climate stress testing, collateral and flood mapping overlays, borrower engagement and covenants, green retrofit incentives, portfolio decarbonisation monitoring, and sector exposure limits with enhanced due diligence. Climate checks for major transactions are also recorded in source data.

External limited assurance over climate-related data is provided by EY in accordance with ISAE 3000. The assurance scope covers the Bank’s Scope 1 and Scope 2 greenhouse gas emissions for 2024, as specified in the governance evidence. Based on available documentation, financed emissions and other Scope 3 categories, including the reported 2024 financed emissions of 35,973,167.69 tCO2e, are outside the stated assurance scope.

Source data flags the Bank’s climate disclosures as aligned with TCFD and IFRS S2. However, available documentation does not evidence a separate governance control process specifically designed to verify or assure TCFD or IFRS S2 alignment beyond the general governance, management, and assurance arrangements described above.

**Evidence boundaries**

- Formal mandate and committee charters: Available documentation evidences Board and ESG & Sustainability Committee activity, meeting frequency, and decisions, but does not include a separate climate-specific Board mandate or the ESG & Sustainability Committee charter or terms of reference. This section therefore does not describe detailed formal mandates beyond the roles implied by the activities and decisions recorded.
- Trade-offs: The Board decision evidence identifies climate-related decisions, including those on net-zero targets, the transition plan, climate scenario analysis methodology, carbon credit procurement, and ESG reporting. However, available documentation does not describe specific trade-offs such as profitability, capital allocation, implementation cost, risk appetite, or competing strategic priorities, and therefore none are reported.
- Skills adequacy: Available documentation evidences Board climate expertise percentages and a skills development programme but does not evidence a separate formal process for assessing the adequacy of Board climate skills.
- Escalation thresholds: Documentation reviewed does not evidence formal escalation thresholds or trigger-based escalation mechanics for climate-related risks beyond the ERM integration indicators and management committee oversight described above.
- Assurance scope: External limited assurance for 2024 covers only Scope 1 and Scope 2 emissions. Financed emissions and other Scope 3 categories are outside the stated assurance scope.
- Alignment indicators: Source data includes TCFD and IFRS S2 alignment flags. Available documentation does not evidence a separate governance control process specifically designed to verify or assure alignment.
