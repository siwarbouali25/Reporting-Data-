### Governance

#### Board oversight

In 2024, the supervisory board of Eurolux Universal Bank AG comprised 10 members, with 68.5% classified as independent. Climate-related topics were overseen by both the full board and a dedicated ESG & Sustainability Committee, which met 7 times during the year. The available evidence demonstrates committee activity and meeting frequency, but does not include the committee charter or terms of reference; therefore, the formal mandate, approving body, and approval or last-review date are not evidenced.

The board was informed about climate matters through semi-annual climate risk reporting and regular inclusion of climate topics on board agendas, with climate featured in 72.6% of the 8 full board meetings in 2024. This is consistent with prior years (69.8% in 2022, 73.1% in 2023). Climate-related discussions at the ESG & Sustainability Committee included scenario analysis, physical risk updates, transition plan review, green finance growth, and net-zero progress.

Board oversight of strategy and major transactions is evidenced by the requirement for climate checks on major transactions. The board’s role in risk management is supported by semi-annual reporting, a climate risk register, and integration of climate risks into the enterprise risk management (ERM) framework. Oversight of metrics and targets included endorsement of net-zero interim target revisions and review of net-zero progress, with climate metrics also linked to executive remuneration.

The available board minutes evidence identifies climate-related decisions but does not describe specific trade-offs considered by the board, such as profitability, capital allocation, implementation cost, risk appetite, or competing strategic priorities. Detailed decisions are provided in the “Board and committee decisions” subsection below.

#### Management responsibility

In 2024, climate governance was led by the Climate Risk Management Committee. Evidence for prior-year committee naming or continuity is not provided.

The management process flow is as follows:
- **Identification and recording:** Climate-related risks are identified and recorded in a climate risk register (8 risks in 2024, 6 integrated into ERM).
- **Classification:** Risks are classified by category (physical acute, physical chronic, transition market, transition policy, transition reputational, transition technology), time horizon (short, medium, long), and rating (high, medium).
- **Monitoring:** Risks are monitored at quarterly or semi-annual frequencies, aligned with ERM cycles.
- **Scenario analysis linkage:** Relevant risks are linked to scenario analyses where a scenario link exists.
- **Mitigation actions:** Management defines and implements mitigation actions, such as climate scenario stress testing in ICAAP, collateral revaluation, transition-plan covenants, green retrofit incentives, portfolio decarbonisation monitoring, and sector exposure limits.
- **ERM integration and review:** ERM integration supports management monitoring and provides a structured basis for board and committee review where required.

The evidence does not specify formal escalation thresholds for climate risks. Escalation occurs through management monitoring and board or committee review where required, but the absence of explicit escalation thresholds is noted as a control gap.

Four climate-related risks changed since the prior period, reflecting updates in assessments, time horizons, or mitigation measures.

#### Climate skills and competencies

In 2024, 37.5% of board members had climate-related expertise. The bank operated a board-level skills development programme to enhance climate governance capabilities. The available evidence demonstrates the percentage of board members with climate expertise and the existence of a skills development programme, but does not describe a formal board skills adequacy assessment process, such as a skills matrix, annual evaluation, or nomination committee review.

#### Remuneration and climate incentives

Climate-related performance metrics were incorporated into executive remuneration in 2024. Specifically, 9.3% of the CEO’s compensation was linked to ESG-related metrics, and 15.1% of all executives’ remuneration was linked to climate-related performance metrics. For comparison, the all-executive climate-linked remuneration percentages were 8.7% in 2022 and 15.3% in 2023. This linkage provides the board with an additional lever for overseeing management’s delivery on climate objectives.

#### External assurance and controls

External assurance over climate-related disclosures in 2024 was provided by EY, applying the ISAE3000 standard. The assurance was limited in scope and covered only Scope 1 and Scope 2 emissions. Limited assurance is a lower level of assurance than reasonable assurance. Based on available evidence, financed emissions and other Scope 3 categories are outside the stated assurance scope.
