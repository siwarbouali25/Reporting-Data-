# Eurolux Universal Bank AG - Climate Disclosure: Data Limitations & Expert-Review Log

**Internal quality-assurance document - not part of the published report.**

For each disclosure section this log records (a) the IFRS S1 / S2 requirement areas that could not be fully satisfied because the underlying data does not contain the relevant information, and (b) residual items flagged for expert review before the report is finalised. Sections are generated under an evidence-bounded policy: where a requirement cannot be supported by the available data it is disclosed as a boundary rather than estimated. None of the items below indicate fabricated content; they indicate where expert judgement and/or additional source data are required.

## General Requirements  
*Automated score: 7/10 - needs human review*

### IFRS S1/S2 requirement areas limited by available data
_No data-driven requirement gaps recorded for this section._

### Items requiring expert correction before finalisation (material)
- [ ] “This subsection explains the main sources of guidance used to frame the Bank’s… disclosures.” followed by “No other formal disclosure frameworks… are described as having been applied…” — the evidence package lists sources_of_guidance as empty. The draft should not imply a documented selection of guidance beyond IFRS S1/S2 unless the Bank explicitly states it; reframe to: 'This report is prepared using an IFRS S1/S2-style structure; the Bank has not applied SASB topics/metrics in this report.'
- [ ] Implied assurance positioning: stating limited external assurance exists for selected Scope 1 and Scope 2 emissions without clarifying whether those Scope 1/2 emissions are disclosed in this report (operational_ghg_records_available=false). This can mislead users; must clarify the boundary (e.g., 'Scope 1 and Scope 2 emissions are not disclosed in this report / are outside scope / are under development', if that is the Bank’s position).

### Minor wording for editorial review (optional)
- “intended to be read as a dedicated chapter of a bank-focused sustainability report” — acceptable, but could be tightened to avoid implying publication form if not specified elsewhere.
- “The report is designed to complement, and be read alongside, the Bank’s general purpose financial statements.” — acceptable, but IFRS S1 also expects identification of the related financial statements; consider explicitly identifying them (e.g., 'the consolidated financial statements for the year ended …') once reporting period is known.
- Comparatives: repeated use of “may be provided” could be tightened to “are provided where available” to align with the stated policy, unless the Bank truly does not include them.

_Disclosed in the report as evidence boundaries because the source data does not contain the underlying information. An expert should confirm the boundary wording and supply the data if it exists elsewhere._

- “The Bank does not disclose a separate ESG & Sustainability Committee charter or terms of reference in this report.”
- “The Bank does not currently describe a separate formal process for assessing the adequacy of Board climate skills in this report.”
- “ERM integration indicators show that 6 of 8 recorded climate-related risks carry an ERM-integration flag; enterprise-wide integration into the enterprise risk management framework is not evidenced.”
- “Formal escalation thresholds or trigger-based escalation mechanics are not evidenced in the climate risk register.”
- “Board decision evidence identifies climate-related decisions but does not describe quantified or specific Board-level trade-offs…”
- Assurance boundary: limited assurance by EY under ISAE 3000 over Scope 1 and Scope 2 only; financed emissions and other Scope 3 outside scope.

### Items requiring expert correction before finalisation (material)
- [ ] Risk register example: “...associated with an estimated potential financial impact of EUR 165.2 million.” Evidence supports EUR 165.2 million, but the draft labels it “estimated potential financial impact” without confirming the register uses that exact definition; should be stated neutrally as “financial impact of EUR 165.2 million (as recorded in the register)” or similar.
- [ ] Mitigation actions list includes items that are evidenced as labels in the register, but the draft presents them as implemented controls (e.g., “engagement and transition-plan covenants with borrowers”, “sector exposure limits and enhanced due diligence”). Without additional control-design/operating-effectiveness evidence, these should be framed as “recorded mitigation actions” rather than implying they are fully implemented bank-wide controls.
- [ ] Overview: “climate checks for major transactions” is supported as an activity indicator, but the surrounding phrasing implies a more formalised control framework (“ERM integration indicators”, “climate checks”, “risk register” presented as a cohesive governance-control system). Needs tightening to avoid implying a formal control system beyond what is evidenced.

### Minor wording for editorial review (optional)
- “These indicators show that climate-related matters were regularly considered at Board level.” (reasonable inference from agenda coverage; could be softened to “indicate”)
- “The committee’s role in climate-related oversight is therefore described on the basis of its meeting frequency and recorded climate-related agenda coverage.” (acceptable, but could be simplified to avoid sounding methodological)
- “The Bank operates a skills development programme that includes climate-related topics.” (supported as programme existence; could clarify it is a programme including climate topics, without implying breadth/mandatory coverage)

## Strategy  
*Automated score: 8/10 - needs human review*

### IFRS S1/S2 requirement areas limited by available data
_Disclosed in the report as evidence boundaries because the source data does not contain the underlying information. An expert should confirm the boundary wording and supply the data if it exists elsewhere._

- “We have not yet published a standalone, fully specified climate transition plan. The Bank does not currently disclose the detailed assumptions or dependencies that would underpin such a plan …”
- “The Bank does not currently disclose a separate, detailed business‑model impact assessment that quantifies how climate-related factors may reshape the overall business model beyond the portfolio and product effects described above.”
- “The Bank also does not currently disclose quantified or documented trade‑off analysis for strategy decisions …”
- “These figures are modelled estimates and not realised losses or impairments.” (scenario outputs)
- “These figures are modelled estimates with medium confidence and should not be interpreted as assured future revenue.” (opportunities)
- “The Bank does not currently disclose a granular mapping of climate-related financial effects to specific financial statement line items …”
- “The Bank does not currently disclose planned sources of funding specifically earmarked to implement its climate strategy …”

### Items requiring expert correction before finalisation (material)
- [ ] Targets: “aligned with an SBTi 1.5°C pathway” and “under a net‑zero banking framework” are presented as alignment/validation-style claims. With only target summary available, the Strategy should avoid implying external validation/assurance or detailed methodological alignment beyond naming the referenced framework (e.g., ‘using an SBTi 1.5°C pathway’ / ‘under the NZBA framework’) and should not imply SBTi validation or detailed conformance.
- [ ] Financial effects (current period): The Strategy does not provide supported disclosure of how climate-related risks/opportunities affected financial position/performance/cash flows for the reporting period (it provides exposures, modelled scenario outputs, and capex/opex indicators, but not actual current-period effects). As written, it risks being read as meeting the ‘current financial effects’ requirement without actually providing them; this needs an explicit boundary statement that current-period financial statement impacts have not been separately quantified/disclosed.

### Minor wording for editorial review (optional)
- “Scenario analysis under the NGFS v4 framework informs our understanding of potential financial effects …” (Tighten to avoid implying direct incorporation into financial statements.)
- “Transition risk losses remain within our internal capital buffer thresholds.” (Keep as scenario-specific model output; consider adding ‘in our modelling’ to avoid sounding like an observed fact.)
- “Near‑term resilience is acceptable …” (Add ‘based on our scenario analysis’ to keep it explicitly bounded.)

## Risk Management  
*Automated score: 8/10 - needs human review*

### IFRS S1/S2 requirement areas limited by available data
_Disclosed in the report as evidence boundaries because the source data does not contain the underlying information. An expert should confirm the boundary wording and supply the data if it exists elsewhere._

- No formal climate-specific risk appetite thresholds or limits are disclosed.
- No detailed climate risk policies, formal control procedures or internal standards are disclosed beyond the mitigation actions recorded in the register.
- No formal escalation thresholds or triggers for climate-related risks are disclosed.
- Upstream (funding/suppliers) and internal operational climate risks are not separately captured in the 2024 climate risk register and are outside the scope of this report.
- No dedicated processes for identifying, assessing, prioritising or monitoring climate-related opportunities are disclosed.
- Scenario analysis is described as forward-looking, modelled estimates and not treated as forecasts or realised losses.
- No detailed probability models, quantitative scoring methodologies, or scenario model mechanics are disclosed.

### Items requiring expert correction before finalisation (material)
- [ ] “These indicative financial impacts, together with the qualitative ratings and time horizons, are used to prioritise climate-related risks relative to one another and to other material risks.” (Over-claims integration into cross-risk prioritisation; the payload does not evidence how climate risks are prioritised relative to other risk types within overall risk taxonomy/ERM beyond an integration flag.)

### Minor wording for editorial review (optional)
- “Mitigation actions are recorded for the risks disclosed in this report.” → tighten to “Mitigation actions are recorded in the climate risk register for each of the eight climate-related risks.”
- “We identify climate-related risks through our risk register process …” → acceptable, but could clarify that the register is the primary mechanism disclosed (not necessarily the only mechanism used).
- “Any upstream implications … are considered indirectly through the transition and reputational risks recorded in the climate risk register …” → defensible, but could clarify this is not a distinct upstream process.

## Metrics and Targets  
*Automated score: 8/10 - needs human review*

### IFRS S1/S2 requirement areas limited by available data
_Disclosed in the report as evidence boundaries because the source data does not contain the underlying information. An expert should confirm the boundary wording and supply the data if it exists elsewhere._

- Operational Scope 1, Scope 2 and Scope 3 emissions for 2024 are not disclosed because detailed records are unavailable.
- Financed-emissions perimeter (asset class/sector coverage and exclusions) is not separately disclosed.
- No sector/geography/asset-class disaggregation of financed emissions is provided.
- Detailed calculation methodologies, data quality assessments and key assumptions for metrics are not described.
- Financed emissions, exposure metrics and climate-related financial metrics have not been independently verified.
- Progress indicators are internal target-record metrics and have not been independently verified.

### Items requiring expert correction before finalisation (material)
- [ ] “Third-party validation: … SBTi identified as the validation body” / “UNEP FI identified as the validation body” presented as if the targets are validated by those bodies. The only supported fact is that the target record flags third_party_validated=true and names a validation body; this must be framed more explicitly as ‘recorded in the target record’ and not as an external validation conclusion.
- [ ] “Framework: … SBTi 1.5°C pathway” and “NZBA … framework” could be interpreted as confirmed alignment/acceptance by those initiatives. Without methodology/acceptance evidence, this must be framed strictly as the framework field in the target record (not as confirmed alignment or membership-based validation).

### Minor wording for editorial review (optional)
- “It covers portfolio emissions …” could be tightened to avoid implying full portfolio coverage, because the financed-emissions perimeter is explicitly not disclosed.
- “These metrics are used internally to … inform risk management, client engagement and strategic planning.” (fine, but could be tightened to avoid implying systematic integration across all processes without further support).
- “Climate-related capital expenditure … directed towards climate-related initiatives …” and similar for opex: acceptable, but could be tightened given the absence of classification methodology detail.
