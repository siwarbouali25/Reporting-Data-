### Executive summary

#### Overview

Eurolux Universal Bank AG, a commercial bank headquartered in Germany, presents this climate-related sustainability executive summary for the 2024 reporting year in EUR under the CSRD regulatory regime. The summary is based on the generated Strategy, Risk management, and Metrics and targets sections, as well as available quantitative Governance highlights. A full Governance narrative section is not available; therefore, this summary reflects only the evidence currently accessible.

The disclosures focus on the bank’s lending and banking book activities, associated physical and transition climate risks, and related opportunities, including green finance and climate-focused capital and operating expenditure.

#### Key climate-related risks and opportunities

The bank’s 2024 risk register records eight climate-related risks: three physical and five transition risks. Top disclosed transition risks include technology-driven impacts on auto lending (EUR 165.2 million potential financial impact), stranded asset exposure in fossil fuel sectors (EUR 136.5 million), and policy risk on industrial loans (EUR 67.5 million). The leading physical risk is flood exposure in the mortgage portfolio, rated high risk with a quantified impact of EUR 24.8 million. Reputational risk linked to financed emissions scrutiny is also noted (EUR 65.5 million).

Scenario analysis is used to model potential losses under NGFS v4 orderly, disorderly, and hot-house scenarios. The maximum modelled transition risk loss is 23.1% of capital (short-term disorderly scenario), and the maximum modelled physical risk loss is 32.0% of capital (long-term hot-house scenario). These outputs are scenario-based estimates, not realised outcomes.

Climate-related opportunities are also identified. In 2024, green loans total EUR 3,335.75 million (10.71% of total loans), with climate-related capex of EUR 476.95 million and opex of EUR 219.32 million. Quantified opportunity revenue estimates of EUR 465.0 million are scenario-based and medium confidence.

#### Governance and risk management highlights

In 2024, the Board comprises 10 members, with 68.5% independent directors and 37.5% with climate expertise. Climate topics appear on 72.6% of Board agendas, and climate risk reporting to the Board is semi-annual. Board climate expertise increased from 33.5% in 2022 to 37.5% in 2024; CEO climate-linked pay share rose from 6.3% to 9.3% over the same period.

Management oversight is exercised through the Climate Risk Management Committee. In 2024, climate-linked elements represent 9.3% of CEO compensation and 15.1% of all executive remuneration.

Risk management is supported by a climate risk register covering eight risks, with 75% integrated into the enterprise risk management framework. Monitoring frequencies are quarterly or semi-annual. Documented mitigation actions include scenario stress testing integrated into ICAAP, collateral revaluation and flood mapping overlays, sector exposure limits, enhanced due diligence, and portfolio decarbonisation glide-path monitoring. Scenario analysis provides forward-looking inputs; modelled estimates are linked to specific risks but do not represent actual losses. Formal climate risk appetite thresholds, detailed control procedures, escalation thresholds, and quantified mitigation effectiveness are not evidenced.

#### Metrics and targets highlights

Financed emissions for 2024 are reported at 35,973,167.69 tCO2e, with a portfolio carbon intensity of 1,154.8131 tCO2e per EUR million lending. High-carbon sector exposure is 35.5% (EUR 11,059.49 million), and fossil fuel exposure is 22.15% (EUR 6,899.59 million). Green loans, climate-related capex, and opex are reported as above. No sectoral breakdowns of emissions or exposures are available.

Operational Scope 1, Scope 2, and other Scope 3 emissions (beyond financed emissions) are not quantified; no 2024 operational emissions figures are reported. Detailed calculation methodologies, data quality assessments, and assumptions for financed emissions and intensity metrics are not available.

Three climate-related targets are recorded in the target dataset: an absolute reduction target for Scope 1 and 2, an intensity reduction target for Scope 3 category 15 (financed emissions), and a net-zero target across all scopes. All target parameters—including baseline years, interim milestones, percentage reduction goals, scopes covered, third_party_validated flags, and named validation bodies—are target-record attributes from source data and do not constitute assurance or verification of targets or progress. Status fields such as “on_track” and target_progress_pct_2024 are also dataset fields. No underlying operational emissions inventory, methodology, or assurance statement is available for 2024.

#### Key limitations

This executive summary is limited by the scope of available evidence. Governance narrative is restricted to quantitative highlights; a full Governance section is not available. Business model impacts and formal trade-off analyses in Strategy are not separately documented. Scenario analysis outputs and opportunity revenue estimates are modelled and scenario-specific, not historical results.

Operational greenhouse gas emissions (Scope 1, Scope 2, and other Scope 3 beyond financed emissions) are not available for 2024, and no operational emissions figures are reported. Financed emissions are disclosed only as consolidated totals and an overall intensity metric, without sector or asset-class disaggregation. Detailed calculation methodologies, data quality assessments, and key assumptions for emissions metrics and scenario analyses are not available. Internal carbon price and carbon credit usage metrics, beyond planned carbon credit parameters in one net-zero target record, are not separately evidenced.

Formal climate risk appetite thresholds, detailed control frameworks, escalation procedures, and quantified assessments of mitigation effectiveness are not documented. All target and progress parameters are target-record attributes and do not constitute independent assurance or verification. These limitations should be considered when interpreting the bank’s 2024 climate-related disclosures.
